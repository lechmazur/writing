<story>Above the clouds, where the opal library caught dawn in its curved walls, Sera tended the last glass orchids with a gently systematic patience. [24] She was the glass-orchid caretaker, and each transparent petal held not color, but the sound of a name no living mouth remembered. [46] The library had once opened onto gardens, theatres, and observatories, deserted marvels now drifting on stone islands connected by bridges of frozen light. [69] Only Sera crossed them, pruning silver roots, dusting crystal stems, and recording every petal that had begun to cloud. [88] Her work mattered because a name, once silent, loosened the person it had named from memory altogether. [105] She had learned that when her mother's face vanished from their portraits, leaving only a foxglove-purple sleeve around Sera's shoulders. [125] On the morning the western bridge failed, Sera found the bamboo wind chime above the orchid house splitting in the cold. [146] One tube had cracked from lip to knot, and its note, which tuned the petals, sank into a breathy rasp. [166] At once, three orchids dimmed. [171] Inside them, voices thinned like steam. [177] Sera did not weep; grief wasted water, and water was precious above the clouds. [191] She wrapped the broken tube in cloth, lit her blue lantern, and consulted the catalogue by lantern light. [209] Its final entry pointed across the museum of extinct feelings, where abandoned instruments still answered emotions no visitor could feel. [229] There, an echo of a long-forgotten song might teach the cracked bamboo its note again. [244] Sera pocketed her mother's empty portrait frame and set out to preserve the sound of forgotten names, beginning with the one she could no longer speak. [270] The museum's first hall belonged to wonder. [277] Its mirrors showed stars beneath her feet, but Sera kept her eyes on the catalogue and measured her steps past their beautiful hunger. [300] In the hall of tenderness, mechanical hands reached from cradles, asking to be held. [314] She gave each one a minute of her warmth, because systematically refusing sorrow was only another way of losing it. [334] Beyond lay regret, planted like a garden. [341] Foxgloves rose from black glass beds, their spotted bells whispering every kindness Sera had postponed until the world emptied. [360] One wore her voice, promising to repair the roof instead of sitting beside her mother through the last summer rain. [380] Sera stopped then, and the lantern trembled. [387] She had thought preservation meant never pausing, yet her mother's name had faded while she saved everyone else's. [405] She opened the empty frame, placed it among the foxgloves, and admitted that she could not remember whether her mother had laughed at thunderstorms. [429] The flowers bowed, releasing her from their accusation, and revealed a small door beneath their stems. [445] Behind it waited the hall of belonging, circular and silent, with a choir of bamboo pipes surrounding a dry fountain. [465] Sera hung the cracked tube among them and struck it once. [476] Its ugly rasp traveled around the circle, returned softer, then returned again carrying a melody: four notes rising, two falling, and a silence shaped like an embrace. [503] The song had no words, but Sera knew the pause before its final note. [517] Her mother had always left that pause for Sera to fill. [528] She sang the name hidden in her since childhood, and this time regret did not close her throat. [546] The echo took it gently, repeating it through every pipe until the cracked tube answered whole, not mended, but true. [566] By dawn, Sera had carried the song home across bridges brightening beneath her lantern. [580] She hung the chime over the orchids and let the wind play. [592] One by one, clear petals flushed with sound, and the library filled with names, awkward, tender, and gloriously difficult to pronounce. [613] Sera listened without writing, giving her mother the first whole minute. [624] Then she opened her ledger, not to imprison the names, but to teach the deserted marvels how to answer when someone finally came looking for them. [650] Outside, the clouds turned foxglove pink, beautiful and poisonous with all she could not change. [665] Inside, she watered the roots, corrected one date, and left the last line blank for a visitor's name. [683]</story>
