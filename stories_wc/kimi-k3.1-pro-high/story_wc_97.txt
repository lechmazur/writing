<story>Above the clouds, Nacre Station clung to the tidal arbor, a black tree whose glass leaves drank the silver flood. [20] There, Iven played piano for departures, his wistfulness kept polished and public, like the brass on the ticket gates. [39] In his waistcoat lay the anniversary clock Sera had given him, its face cracked and its hands fixed at the hour she vanished beneath the tide. [65] Twenty years later, he still wound it each morning, though the broken spring only clicked, a small steel refusal. [84] That evening, every compass in the station flickered, needles shivering between north and the arbor's depths. [100] The cloudships had gone; the tide was returning early, white and thunderous, to strip the platforms bare. [117] No shelter was built for such a tide, and forty travelers looked to Iven because the stationmaster had already looked away. [138] He almost played the old farewell, the neat surrender fate had rehearsed in him for years. [154] Instead, he opened the piano to mute its strings and found a black-and-white film reel wedged beneath them, labeled in Sera's hand: FOR THE WRONG TIDE. [180] The projector in the abandoned kiosk was jammed, so Iven, strategically creative with grief, fitted the clock's largest gear into its empty spindle. [203] He sacrificed the keepsake without ceremony; longing, he decided, could finally do some work. [217] On the wall, gray Sera crossed the platform, smiled, and walked out of the public footage. [233] Hidden sections followed, spliced between frames: diagrams of hollow roots, a stair inside the trunk, and a refuge above the highest waterline. [255] She had mapped the arbor while surveying tides, then concealed her discovery from owners who meant to keep refuge profitable. [275] The final section showed her at this piano, playing the station's departure hymn while the root gate opened. [293] Beneath her image, scratched letters read, THE HYMN IS A KEY; NEVER PLAY ITS ENDING. [308] Iven understood then why her body had never returned: the last written chord had closed the gate before she could enter. [329] Fate's old score was not a prophecy but a trap, and traps could be syncopated. [344] He carried the reel to the travelers, divided them into root teams, and gave each a flickering compass whose wild needle pointed toward, not away from, the flooding arbor. [373] Following those needles, they found the buried stair while he returned to the piano. [387] The first wave struck as he began the hymn; windows became white, and the station groaned like a ship remembering it had bones. [410] Through the floor, the root gate shuddered open, and the travelers descended into Sera's dry, lamplit rooms. [427] Iven played on alone, holding the gate with every measure, until the horizon invited night and the keys darkened beneath his bleeding hands. [450] Then came the final page, its fatal chord waiting with the patience of a blade. [465] If he stopped early, the gate would remain open but the returning tide would tear the stair away; if he played it, he would seal forty people inside and himself out. [496] Iven looked at the stopped clock, now gearless, and saw not Sera's ending but an empty space shaped like his own. [517] He wanted one more hour with her, even one borrowed from a film; he wanted it so fiercely that wanting became invention. [539] To surprise fate with an unplanned encore, he lifted the hymn into a bright, impossible key and threaded Sera's lullaby through its bass. [562] The piano answered with a note no tuner had placed there. [573] Below, the broken clock ticked once in his pocket, and every compass steadied toward the open gate. [590] Iven rose, still playing the last notes from memory as the tide took the bench, then the piano, then the roof. [611] He stepped through the gate backward, and its glass leaves closed on a chord that belonged to no written ending. [631] At dawn, when the arbor shed the sea, the travelers found him alive beside the clock, whose hands had moved one minute past Sera's hour. [656] He did not mistake that for her return. [664] He wound the clock, set it upon the refuge piano, and taught the children the encore, leaving generous spaces where fate expected an end. [688]</story>
