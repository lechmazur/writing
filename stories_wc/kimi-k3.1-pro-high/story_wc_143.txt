In the space age, when ships crossed between worlds like patient sparks, crater inspector Mara Venn still climbed the last ridge on foot. [23]
Above her, the moonlit summit held an impact crater, a stone shelter, and silence deep enough to make her breathing seem rude. [45]
Company rules told her to measure the fractured rim before dawn, when a construction ship would burn the mountain flat for a landing field. [69]
Yet Mara was purposefully curious, and the blue thread leading from the crater into the shelter was not on any company map. [91]
Inside lay old Sef, the nightwatch, beneath a wool blanket, his face peaceful and his broken hourglass cupped in one hand. [112]
He had refused evacuation, and now his oxygen gauge, his cold tea, and his final message strip explained why. [131]
Because crater static ruined radios and clocks, Sef had devised a chemical chronometer: salts on paper darkened in sequence after one measured catalyst was added. [156]
Mara found one sand grain from the nightwatch's hourglass sealed beside the strip, glazed with the catalyst and labeled, For the inspector who looks twice. [181]
She could have called the death in and left, but instead she brushed frost from Sef's camera and saw its last plate was unused. [205]
Then she remembered his letters about the crater's dawn orchid, the last wild one, whose roots no laboratory had ever kept alive. [227]
To photograph the last of something so precious, she would have to outlast the summit cold and Sef's timed instructions. [247]
She dropped the grain into the strip's shallow copper cup. [257]
A violet stain bloomed, releasing warmth, and the first words surfaced: Open the lower vent; sleep beside it until the second message. [279]
The vent exhaled crater heat, faint but enough to keep her fingers flexible as she measured fissures by lantern light. [299]
Each fissure pointed toward one hollow, where a silver bud stood in a nest of black glass, waiting. [317]
Mara nearly photographed it then, wanting proof secured before exhaustion or company impatience could take the choice from her. [336]
But Sef had written that its petals opened only when stars faded, and closed forever if touched by direct moonlight. [356]
So she waited, wrapped in Sef's blanket, while the reaction crept through amber and the cold climbed her bones. [375]
At the second message, rust-red letters instructed her to carry the camera to the hollow and uncover the eastern mirror. [395]
The mirror would reflect the first indirect light downward without exposing the flower to the hardening sky. [412]

The third reaction stalled as the temperature fell, so Mara held the copper cup against her stomach, giving it her own heat. [459]
Outside, the construction ship's beacon pricked the horizon, earlier than scheduled. [470]
Its engines would arrive with the sun. [477]
Mara's training urged her toward the shelter, but she set the camera, focused on the bud, and stayed. [495]
When the final words appeared, they read, Do not ask it to outlast its world; remember how it met the light. [509]
At last the stars thinned, and a dawning hush crossed the summit, so complete that Mara heard the orchid's petals part. [530]
They opened colorless, then blushed gold in the mirror's borrowed light, each vein bright as a departure trail. [548]
Mara exposed the plate as the ship's shadow touched the western rim. [560]

The orchid vanished without flame, its substance lifted into a brief silver weather. [592]
