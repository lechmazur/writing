<story>Mara Venn was a claustrophobic spelunker, which everyone said was absurd until they needed someone who understood fear well enough to read it. [24] She tracked invisible things for a living: cold drafts through limestone, pressure faults beneath tide pools, and breaks in undersea cables whose signals vanished without disturbing the water. [53] Those gifts brought her back to Saint Brigid's Station, a forgotten research facility sealed inside a sea cave, during the stillness between tidal changes. [77]

She had fled it twelve years ago, leaving her brother Niko and the night crew behind when the experimental cable chamber flooded. [97] Officially, no station existed, so officially, no one had died. [108] Now the old cable network was being dredged for scrap, and its final maintenance pulse had reached her instruments: a sequence of names repeating beneath the static. [133]

The entrance yawned only at slack tide. [141] Mara crawled through with her shoulders brushing stone, counting each exhalation while the surf outside alternated between a crash and an enormous hush. [163] Beyond the passage, pressure doors stood open around a dry laboratory, its glass panels clouded like ice. [181]

The station had studied phase transitions, using tidal pressure to shift a salt solution inside the cables between liquid conductor and solid crystal. [201] In crystal form, a cable could preserve a signal indefinitely; melted, it would release everything stored within it. [220] The experiment had trapped the crew's last transmissions when the chamber froze during the flood. [236]

On the central console lay a waterproof codex, its diagrams mapping every cable beneath the bay. [251] A ragged empty square marked one margin, where illuminant gold had once completed a conductive circuit. [266] Mara opened her pendant and removed the gold fleck she had torn away while escaping, too panicked to understand why Niko had pressed it into her hand. [290]

"You were not locking me in," she whispered. [298] "You were asking me to return." [305]

A tone sounded, and the walls tightened around her breathing. [315] The tide had begun to turn. [322] Water knocked somewhere deep in the facility, followed by a hush so complete she heard the gold tremble against the page. [341]

Instead of fleeing, she tracked the invisible current moving through the codex. [352] Its faint warmth crossed her fingertips, stopped at the broken margin, and waited. [364] She fitted the fleck into the square. [372]

Light ran through the margin's tiny waves and saints, then into the console. [384] The cable answered with a shudder that passed through the floor, and the salt core began warming from solid toward liquid. [403] Names appeared on the glass: Niko Venn, Aya Sorin, Tomas Reed, and nineteen others erased with the station. [424]

But the release required a steady hand on the thermal control until the transition finished. [440] If Mara left before the cable discharged, the names would crystallize again, and the dredgers would destroy them. [458] Behind her, the passage gave a warning groan as the returning tide pressed into the cave. [477]

Her terror returned completely, yet for once it did not command her. [490] She thought of Niko standing beside workers no memorial would name, and of all the unremembered whose only company was darkness. [511] Staying was not courage exactly; it was refusing to abandon them twice. [526]

She braced the control with her belt, keeping one hand on it while transmitting the station's archive through every functioning cable. [546] Fishing buoys, harbor relays, and weather stations received the names, each becoming a witness beyond the sea. [566]

The chamber roared as brine flashed to vapor, then condensed in a sudden silver rain. [582] In that crashing hush, the pressure door lifted, and Mara stumbled into the flooded passage with the codex held above her head. [602]

She reached open rock as the tide covered the station behind her. [615] Dawn showed no wreckage, only black water and the distant cable buoys blinking in sequence. [634] Mara answered each blink aloud, naming the dead until her voice steadied. [649] Then she turned toward town, carrying their record home and leaving no one to vanish alone. [665]</story>
