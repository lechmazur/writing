Mara Venn, a hedge maze designer by trade and a trespasser in her own past, returned to the living bridge garden when its bridges began to fall. [27]
Willow spans braided themselves above the gorge, orchids nested in their joints, and every footstep woke a green shiver in the roots. [49]
Since the collapse that killed her brother Nils, Mara had been retrocognitively trapped, receiving each moment only after it hardened into memory. [71]
She saw yesterday's rain on today's leaves and felt every handclasp after the hand had gone. [87]
Worse, Nils still crossed the willow spans before her, forever one winter dead and one turning beyond her reach. [106]
She had come because the photographs in her workshop were fading. [117]
First the garden's oldest arches vanished into white, then Nils's face, until only the halo fragment above his workbench remained sharp. [138]
The curved silver splinter had been dug from the garden's first root, and Nils had built around it because it held dawn long after sunset. [163]
Now Mara carried it in her breast pocket, where it ticked against her ribs like a second heart trying to remember her. [185]
At the gorge, a root tore loose, and the far bridge shuddered through a rain of white petals. [203]
The garden was not merely dying; it was forgetting the shapes that kept it alive. [218]
Mara understood then that the fading photographs were its memories escaping, and her own haunted sight was only the deepest root of the same wound. [243]
A straight repair would fail, for living wood could not be ordered back into what it had been. [261]
So Mara uncorked her ink and designed a maze through hyperbolic geometry, letting every path branch faster than ordinary space could bear. [283]
On her plan, parallels opened like frightened hands, and impossible quantities of hedge fitted within the narrow crown of the central bridge. [305]
Each turning would give the garden a new way to remember itself without repeating the past. [321]
She planted willow switches at dusk and set the halo fragment among them as a compass. [337]
Its captive dawn drew the shoots into curves no eye could trust, while Mara paced behind them, whispering measurements and Nils's childhood name. [360]
By midnight the maze stood taller than she did, dark leaves slick with starlight, its corridors widening inward like a held breath. [382]
Then the photographs in her pocket began to burn with cold. [392]
She opened them and saw Nils fading from the last print, his hand lifted toward her in the old garden. [412]
If the maze succeeded, she would lose even this borrowed vision of him. [425]
If it failed, the bridges would disintegrate, and every creature nesting in their green architecture would fall into the gorge. [445]
Mara almost tore the plan apart. [451]
Instead she asked why she had spent years designing labyrinths for strangers while refusing the only exit from her own. [471]
It was not simply grief. [476]
She wanted to discover the texture of time, to learn whether it was a wall behind Nils or a path that might still bear her weight. [502]
Carrying the last photograph, she entered the maze. [510]
The hedges trembled, and each fork offered her a former hour: rain on her apprenticeship, bread warm in Nils's hands, the instant before the old bridge broke. [537]
She chose none of them. [542]
At the center, the halo fragment blazed inside a cage of roots, and there stood Nils, not fading but already almost gone. [564]
Mara pressed the blank photograph to his chest. [572]
For once, she did not ask memory to return; she gave it somewhere new to grow. [588]
The fragment disintegrated without sound, becoming a powder of dawns that streamed through every hyperbolic turning. [604]
Behind her, the old visions loosened, and Nils smiled with the terrible tenderness of someone finally allowed to leave. [623]
The garden convulsed. [626]
Bridges shed dead wood in black avalanches, yet beneath it fresh roots followed the maze, dividing and fastening until the gorge wore a living net. [651]
Mara fell to her knees as the present struck her at last: wet bark under her palms, cold air inside her lungs, a pain unsoftened by having already happened. [680]
Time was not smooth. [684]
It had the texture of root bark, ridged and porous, rough enough to wound her and open enough for light. [704]
She wept there, quaking with reverence, while the blank photograph slowly gathered a new image: herself on the bridge, small and uncertain, facing whatever grew next. [730]
