<story>Upon the verdant parapet beside the waterfall, old Cael pruned his orchard with silver shears, listening for the small gasp each branch made when it opened into another world. [29]
He was wise enough to cut sparingly and insatiably creative enough to try one impossible graft each spring, yet his dearest treasure remained untouched: an heirloom seed, dark as a closed eye, inherited from his mother. [65]
Her instruction had been simple: plant it upon the hush of budding hope, when the orchard needs wonder more than memory. [86]
For generations, his family had tended apple and plum trees whose roots gripped mortal stone while their highest boughs wove between worlds, dropping ripe fruit into a realm beyond the falls. [117]
That realm gave the orchard dawn colored birds and rain that smelled of mint; in return, the orchard sent it blossom, shade, and the sweet astonishment of seeds. [145]
But one winter the birds returned gray, and a colorless blight began climbing the trunks, leaving leaves intact while draining their green. [167]
Cael tried copper grafts, saffron mulch, and roots trained into spirals, but each ingenious remedy only made the pallor spread faster. [188]
Then a girl stumbled through the waterfall, clutching a branch from the other side, her palms blanched where its bark had burned her with cold. [213]
Cael wrapped her in his pruning coat, warmed her hands between his own, and gave her the last crimson apple, offering succor before he asked her name. [240]
She was Neri, a dyer's child, and she whispered that her world had built a wall against storms, only to wall out every bird, season, and dream. [267]
The blight was not invading; it was homesickness, pulling color toward a place that had forgotten how to receive it. [287]
At once Cael understood why his clever grafts failed: he had been tying worlds tighter when he should have opened a path for giving. [311]
Still, the orchard was dying, and the seed lay in his pocket like a final heartbeat. [327]
Neri touched its husk and saw a buried gleam, the first color she had seen since childhood, but she turned away rather than ask him to spend it. [355]
That small mercy undid Cael's last fear. [362]
He had guarded the seed to preserve the color of wonder, yet wonder had never been color alone; it was color recognized, shared, and answered. [387]
He would save the orchard by capturing essence, not of the orchard's vanished hues, but of the instant Neri chose another's sorrow over her own hope. [414]
He set the seed in a cup woven from living willow, then asked Neri to remember the first blue she had ever loved. [437]
She described a summer vat of indigo, and as her voice deepened, one blue thread slipped from her breath into the cup. [459]
Cael added the essence of his mother's laughter, captured in the click of her shears, and the orchard offered rainlight, leaf musk, and the tart shock of a stolen plum. [489]
Each gift became a filament around the seed, not a theft but a pattern, and Cael wove the filaments through the gray branch until it flowered on both sides of the falls. [521]
Beyond the water, astonished voices answered as invisible hands caught the blossoms and began weaving with them. [538]
The wall in Neri's world did not crumble; doors opened in it, hundreds of them, each shaped by someone remembering a different delight. [561]
Upon the hush of budding hope, when the blight reached the last green leaf, Cael split the seed's shining husk and planted its kernel in a crack between parapet stones. [591]
He had expected thunder, but growth came as a radiant hush. [602]
A root slipped into the other world; a shoot rose into his own, unfurling leaves that held no single color until someone looked at them with love. [629]
Then green rippled through the orchard, scarlet crossed the sky, and Neri laughed in blue and gold. [646]
Cael did not try to foresee every graft the new tree would accept; wisdom had finally taught his invention when to bow. [668]
