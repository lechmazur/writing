<story>After surviving the attempt on her life, Veya returned to the quiet canyon under stars, carrying nothing but a ledger strip and the scorched edge of her testament. [28] She was a luminous glass artist, though the title now seemed quaint beside the blackened workshop and the warden's order that she be forgotten. [52] Her testament had been a window naming the charcoal burners lost in his mines, each letter filled with light that outlived dusk. [74] Hours before its unveiling, someone had sealed her kiln flue; the blast took her assistant's hearing, and nearly took Veya's breath. [95] In the ruins she found the charcoal smuggler's ledger strip, its ragged columns recording mine wages paid to the warden instead. [116] It had been slipped into her fuel sack, an accusation hidden inside the means of her art. [133] Veya wanted vengeance, but each pause between her careful breaths returned her to the same question: what was survival for? [153] Her old teacher had called annealing the art of pauses, when glass neither took shape nor broke, but learned to endure. [174] To discover purpose in the pauses, she would first learn who had trusted her with the truth. [191] At dawn, she used patterns in morning frost to retrace the smuggler's path. [204] White feathers lay untouched on undisturbed stone, but a wavering line of melt and crystals led from her fuel shed toward the narrows. [227] Where boots had warmed the gravel, frost had retreated into crescents; where a cart had rested, it formed a pale double halo. [249] Veya, steadily adaptive as the river glass she made, followed those signs instead of forcing a straighter route through the thaw. [270] When rockfall erased them, she climbed until the frost on shadowed ledges showed her where hands had recently brushed away lichen. [291] By noon she reached a cold charcoal pit and found Orren there, binding the arm he had broken dragging her from the workshop. [314] "You named my dead," he said, nodding at the ledger strip. [325] "So I gave you the names of the living who profit from them." [338] His full ledger lay beneath a stone, listing bribes, sealed shafts, and workers abandoned to keep the warden's charcoal cheap. [358] Orren had meant to sell it for passage out; Veya's window had made that bargain feel like another burial. [377] Then boots sounded above the pit, and the warden called down, offering mercy if she surrendered both glass and paper. [397] He had followed her trail, just as she had followed Orren's. [408] Veya's burned hands throbbed, and she nearly answered with the ledger, buying one more frightened morning. [424] Instead she paused, hearing the canyon return his offer in a faint, drifting resonance, as if the walls themselves were weighing it. [446] She gave Orren the ledger and sent him through a runoff cleft toward the village, while she remained with the scorched testament. [468] Its surviving glass still held starlight, dim but patient. [477] Veya set it upright in the pit's chimney, turning the scorched border outward so the warden would see exactly what he meant to burn. [501] When he climbed down and seized it, light ran through her faulted letters and cast the miners' names across his coat. [522] He raised his cane to shatter them, then stopped: Orren had returned with charcoal burners, glassworkers, and the village council along the rim. [545] No one shouted; the pause lengthened until the warden let his cane fall, and the canyon gave back its small, final click against the stone. [570] The council took the ledger, and the warden, into custody before dusk. [582] Veya did not restore the testament to its former perfection. [592] She annealed a clear seam around every fracture and added Orren's name, not among the dead, but beside a blank space for the living. [616] On the next starlit evening, the window glowed above the canyon, its scars carrying light farther than its unbroken glass ever had. [638] Veya watched in silence, understanding that survival was not a debt to fury, but room enough to begin again. [657] Between each breath and the next, she heard no command, only possibility, resonating softly as it waited for her hands. [677]</story>
