Beyond the murmur of lost languages, when even the stars had forgotten their names, Neri cut marsh reeds beside a crater filled with trembling light. [25]
She was a sculptor of reeds, though no one had asked her to shape anything since the orchard's glass trees stopped fruiting. [47]
The crystalline orchard perched at the crater's edge above her village, and its barren branches chimed like relatives refusing to speak. [68]
Neri lived below it alone, boldly sympathetic to every sorrow except her own. [81]
One dawn she found a black tag painted across her door, three looped lines surrounding a hollow seed. [99]
The same mark appeared on the orchard gate, where old Oren was grieving his son, and on the boat of twins who had not spoken since their mother drowned. [128]
Its artist was Sable, the elusive street painter whose tags appeared wherever loneliness hardened into silence. [144]
Neri could have washed hers away, but instead she carried reed lamps to Oren and the twins and asked them to help her find the next one. [171]
Their shared search began as practicality, for Sable's paint was said to reveal water beneath stone, and water might revive the orchard. [193]
Yet each tag led them through a different wound. [202]
At the shuttered bakery, the mark hid beneath a loaf left for a dead husband. [217]
At the school, it crowned a wall where children had scratched their family trees, then watched the baker weep because his branch ended. [240]
Neri wove him a reed graft, rough and living, and the twins added their mother's boat rope to bind it. [260]
Soon strangers joined the search, carrying food, ladders, and stories they had been too proud to offer. [277]
They followed the tags up spiral paths into the orchard, where crystal leaves scattered their lamp beams into a luminous ache of color. [300]
There, at the orchard's heart, stood a glimmering obsidian arbor, its black arches drinking the crater's shimmer and returning none. [320]
Beneath it waited Sable, not a young outlaw as rumor claimed, but Neri's sister, who had vanished after their mother chose the sea over them. [345]
Neri's first thought was to embrace her; her second was to strike her. [358]
She did neither, because the twins were holding Oren upright, and the baker was offering Sable bread. [375]
"I tagged the lonely doors so you would open them together," Sable said. [388]
"I wanted kinship without having to beg for it." [397]
Neri heard the cruelty inside the confession, and the frightened child beneath it. [410]
"You made a web," she answered, "but you left yourself outside it." [422]
Then Sable showed them water shining under the arbor, sealed by obsidian roots that had fed on stone since the orchard was planted. [445]
No single tool could break them, but every villager took a place along the arches, passing hammers hand to hand while Neri wove their grips into one long reed handle. [475]
They struck together, and the arbor opened without shattering, lifting like a many legged insect from the spring. [493]
Water ran through the crystal roots, and fruit kindled overhead, each globe holding a small warm dawn. [510]
In the released arbor's hollow Neri set her sculpture, a web of reeds threaded with boat rope, bread twine, and the red cord from Sable's paint box. [537]
Its open knots bore no family names, only empty loops where anyone could tie themselves in. [553]
Neri dedicated it to those who had lost their people and to those too wounded to ask for new ones. [573]
When Sable offered her hand, Neri did not forgive everything; she simply bound their little fingers together with reed and left the knot loose enough to undo. [600]
It was not a promise of permanence, but an invitation to remain for the work of another morning. [618]
Above them, the new fruit brightened, and the orchard's reflections crossed the crater like webs of kinship, trembling whenever one thread moved, yet holding. [642]
For the first time, Neri felt the answering tug, gentle as light through water, and knew she had grafted herself onto community without surrendering the shape of her own heart. [672]
