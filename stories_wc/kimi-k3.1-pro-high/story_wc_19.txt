<story>Mara's father built bunkers because he trusted walls, and Mara feared them because walls never answered when she asked for sky. [21] Their town endured one inversion each year, when gravity reversed for an hour and anything untethered fell into the clouds. [41] Below, he said, people were safe; above, there was only the old trellis, a ruin no sensible person would trust. [61] Its vines had once been xenial, weaving cradles for strangers who tumbled upward, but bunkers had left it without guests or gardeners. [83] Mara's mother had tended it, leaving Mara a tiny porcelain doll whose hollow skirt held seeds and whose sealed mouth held silence. [105] Since her mother died, Mara had collected the flavors of different silences: cellars tasted of iron, snowfall of mint, and empty cradles of salt. [129] She wanted one taste from the trellis, though she told herself she was going only to save it. [147] When the warning bell contracted the town into its underground rooms, panic contracted with it, squeezing Mara's breath until she tore open the bunker door. [172] Her father caught her ankle, but she kicked loose, snatched the doll, and clipped his safety line to her belt. [192] Then gravity let go. [196] She fell through the open hatch, past roofs and bells, until clouds closed below her like another white ceiling. [215] She screamed, and brown vines rose through the whiteness, caught her in a woven elbow, and politely bent her toward a seat. [237] Above the clouds, the trellis stretched farther than her town, its dead arches holding one green leaf apiece. [255] Mara unclenched her hands. [259] Here, openness did not demand anything from her; it simply made room. [271] The last seed lay in the doll's skirt, beside a paper twist in her mother's writing: Sprout by limiting exposure; one breath of each silence, never two. [298] Mara understood then why her collection had never grown a garden. [309] She had kept every taste stoppered, possessive and perfectly useless. [319] She opened the doll's mouth and offered the seed one breath of cellar silence. [333] It shivered at the iron taste, her own fear made useful. [344] Next came snowfall, gathered last winter from a windowsill, and the seed split with a white root. [361] For the final breath, the clouds brightened. [368] Gold rimed every vine, and a gilded hush settled over the trellis, so complete that Mara heard her blood slow. [388] The silence was intangibly tangible, pressing her palms like warm wax while leaving nothing she could close her fingers around. [408] Its flavor was apricot, rain, and the instant before a beloved voice speaks. [421] Mara wept, because it tasted like her mother almost entering the room. [433] She held the doll open, greedy for a second breath, though the green leaf beside her browned at its edges. [453] One breath would feed the seed; two would preserve only another beautiful possession. [466] With half the hour gone, Mara stoppered the doll and gave the seed exactly what her mother had instructed. [485] The sprout pierced a dead arch, thin as a hair, then thickened and ran. [499] New vines stitched arch to arch, not closing the gaps but strengthening them, until the whole trellis flexed like a hand learning to open. [523] Mara tied a cutting to her belt and looked down at the cloud ceiling. [537] For the first time, descending did not seem the same as being buried. [550] She jumped, the safety line snapped taut, and her father began hauling her home through rushing white. [567] Gravity returned as she reached the roof, setting her gently in his arms. [580] He was crying with fury and relief, and she was too breathless to defend the door. [596] Instead, she put the green cutting between them. [604] It had rooted in the doll's skirt, and its tendril stretched toward the unlatched hatch. [619] Her father touched the leaves, then looked at the bunker he had made small enough to exclude every accident and nearly every living thing. [643] Together they planted it on the roof, where rain could reach it and guests could find it. [660] That night he left the hatch open by the width of one porcelain hand. [674] Mara slept beside it, breathing the narrow band of sky. [684] The silence there tasted of iron still, but also of mint, wet earth, and room enough to become someone else. [704]</story>
