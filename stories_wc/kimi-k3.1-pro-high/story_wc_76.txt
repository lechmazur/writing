<story>On the rocky island of Vane, where the lighthouse turned its black eye toward a starless sea, Iven was a counter clockwise clock maker, building mechanisms whose hands retreated from every promised hour. [33]
He called his trade penance, for his mother had vanished inside the light while winding it forward, and the villagers swore that time on Vane moved only toward loss. [62]
The beacon had failed that winter, ships broke below like teeth on a plate, and each dawn the tide exposed another white name carved in the wreckers' chapel. [90]
He survived on eel broth and a neatly inventive stubbornness, converting door hinges into escapements and grief into diagrams, but no gear could restore the dead or make the future less certain. [122]

Beside the disc lay an ancient yoyo of whalebone and black wire, its axle sealed by nine concentric rings, the same device his mother had once used to teach him that return was only another kind of departure. [222]
The island's oldest keeper had called it a toy for cowards; Iven, seeing how its string vanished into the stone, understood it was the lighthouse key. [248]
He carried both up the salt-bitten stairs, where the great lamp sat cold inside its cage, and felt the first bleak exhilaration of a man offered a script he could refuse. [279]
To initiate the beacon before dawn, he would have to reverse-engineer the puzzle of the ancient yoyo, because each ring governed one lens, and the hunger stone would supply fire only while being fed a human future. [316]
Fate's design seemed plain: surrender his remaining years, light the lamp, and die exactly as the stone had shown. [335]
Yet his mother's clocks had all run backward, and now their purpose opened in him like a wound: she had not been fleeing time, but hiding instructions inside its retreat. [365]

The first eight rings released with clicks, revealing no spring at their center, only a hollow shaped to receive the stone and a groove spiraling against every natural turn. [424]
His neat invention was a reversal: instead of feeding the stone his future, he would make it swallow the fixed instant of his death, then wind that instant backward until it became only light. [458]

When the first ship's bell shivered beyond the reef, he threw the yoyo seaward; the ninth ring bit his finger, drank one bright drop, and locked. [510]
For one terrible second, nothing happened except his heart counting down in the dark. [524]
Then the stone gave back tomorrow in a soundless convulsion, and a latent radiance woke within the lenses, not white but the deep gold of windows remembered by the drowning. [554]

The returning tide erased the shadow before it erased his footprints, and that small order of things made him laugh until laughter hurt. [637]
