In the candle-lit shrine carved into glacier ice, Mara Venn kept forty-one cups of water, one for each soul lost when the ferry Aster went down. [26]
She was a wreck-diver and a ghost whisperer, though the dead never spoke to her directly; they visited strangers' dreams as cameos, faceless passengers briefly borrowing a face. [54]
Each thaw, dreamers climbed the blue stairs to her shrine, and Mara wrote their visions on glass panes, making windows of connection from details no living witness could have supplied. [84]
A child dreamed of a gray man winding a snow globe; a baker dreamed of him behind her mirror; a priest dreamed of him mending a net that was really a staircase. [116]
Mara recognized her brother Niko, lost with Aster, and recognized his careful mime: below the melted staircase, find what fire could not swallow. [139]
The ferry's owner, Oren Vale, had blamed ice and panic, then built his shipping fortune on the insurance; Mara's dives had found no storm damage, only gates chained from outside. [169]
Yet chains proved cruelty, not who commanded it, and Mara wove truth carefully, separating dream, inference, and fact before she tied any knot around a name. [195]
By analyzing the cameos' backgrounds, she noticed the same brass moon above Niko's shoulder: the purser's clock, fixed in a cabin officials had searched and sealed. [221]
She dove at dusk, when the glacier turned violet and the wreck below shone with borrowed starlight. [238]
Through Aster's split hull she passed bunks, shoes, and a kettle fused to its stove, refusing to let grief make the dead more picturesque than they had been. [266]
The purser's cabin held no ledger, only a melted snow globe beneath the clock, its lighthouse bent toward a tiny village, its glass clouded gold. [291]
When Mara lifted it, white flakes stirred though the water had leaked away, and she heard Niko not in words but in memory: his hands showing hers how the purser hid bribes inside gifts. [325]
She opened the globe's warped base and found an oilskin roll, holding Vale's order to lock steerage, douse the stern beacon, and log the iceberg before one appeared. [353]
There too lay the purser's confession: he had taken Vale's money, then hidden the order when Niko caught him opening the gates too late. [377]
Mara had come to salvage something pure from the wreckage, perhaps Niko's last kindness, perhaps only proof that he had not died screaming without trying; now purity felt heavier than absolution. [408]
She surfaced with the globe cradled against her ribs, its crooked lighthouse warm from her lamp. [424]
At the mourning vigil, Vale came to the shrine in a white coat, offering money for a grander memorial if Mara would surrender her dangerous fantasies. [450]
Instead she set the glass panes between the candles, so every dream faced its neighbors and every flame gained a window. [471]
She told the crowd exactly what the dreams suggested, exactly where she had looked, and exactly what the oilskin proved, leaving no space for revenge to counterfeit certainty. [499]
Then she exposed Vale's order and read the forty-one names, including the purser's, because truth did not become purer by choosing prettier dead. [522]
Vale called the document a plant until the harbor magistrate matched its seal and signature to Vale's original sailing clearance, then quietly sent for officers. [547]
No ghost accused anyone; the living did, with hands joined across the shrine as officers led Vale into the snow. [567]
That night strangers dreamed of Niko in a bright ticket booth, stamping paper boats while the other dead queued laughing behind him. [589]
Mara placed the melted globe in the shrine's central window, where dawn would pass through its flawed glass and scatter small stars over the water cups. [615]
She did not refill it; some things were pure because they remained broken, open, and unable to pretend the storm had never come. [638]
Before leaving, she touched each pane and felt the dead touch back, not as voices, but as brief warmth traveling from palm to unknown palm. [663]
Beyond the ice, the first dreamers were waking, and every one remembered a lighthouse with its crooked window lit. [682]
