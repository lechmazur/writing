<story>At the hush when the empty playground swing beyond the wall drifted in the breeze, Vey returned to the forgotten library walking backwards. [23] Nerves always turned her around, though she claimed retreat offered a superior view of anyone foolish enough to follow. [42] As the city's shard-mirror archivist, she had been exiled for refusing to polish history into one flattering face. [60] Her sentence was reversible, provided she could prove a broken record might guide the living better than a perfect lie. [80] The magistrate had sealed her proof inside a teacup, an afternoon shadow caught in glass, and a boy who wanted desperately to fight somebody. [104] That boy, Niko, kicked open the library door and found her backing between collapsed shelves with a broom leveled at his chest. [126] "Come to arrest me, little boots?" she asked. [134] "Come to steal a mirror," he said, "because mine shows a father who isn't coming home." [150] Vey lowered the broom, but not her guard. [158] "Then steal properly; dust the hinges, mind the glass, and stop confusing damage with courage." [173] She set him repairing mirrored fragments, each one holding a splinter of the city's forbidden testimony. [189] Whenever his hands shook, she insulted his knots, then retied them beside him until he matched her patience. [207] Her lessons never sounded like kindness, which was why he could bear them. [220] At dusk she brewed tea and scattered the leaves across a cracked pane. [233] "Read me a door," she ordered. [239] By analyzing the geometry of tea leaves, Vey could locate reflections that had been removed from official memory. [257] Niko saw only wet litter and said so. [265] "Good," she said, turning the cup three points. [273] "Now doubt with precision." [277] He measured the angles, found a triangle repeated in the stems, and aligned three shards accordingly. [293] Light jumped between them, and the trapped afternoon shadow lifted its head inside the glass. [308] It was Niko's father, recorded on the day the magistrate's soldiers took the strikers away. [323] He had hidden their names in a mirror archive before joining them willingly in prison. [338] Niko struck the table hard enough to spill the tea. [348] "You knew he chose it." [353] "I knew choosing can still hurt," Vey said. [361] "Don't make him noble just to make yourself lonely." [370] He reached for the largest shard, meaning to smash it, and she caught his wrist. [385] For once, she walked backwards without a joke, opening a path between them instead of closing one. [402] "Break it and the magistrate wins twice," she said. [411] "Survive badly if you must, but survive honestly." [419] Together they used the spilled leaves to reconstruct the archive's index, triangle by stubborn triangle. [434] Vey taught Niko to annotate every recovered name with a source, a contradiction, and room for another witness. [452] "Memory isn't a fortress," she told him. [459] "It's a bridge you keep repairing while cross-eyed officials throw rocks." [470] By dawn they had enough evidence to reverse her exile, but the magistrate arrived with torches before they could carry it out. [492] Niko stepped forward; Vey hooked his collar and hauled him behind the shelves. [505] "Mentor me later," he hissed. [510] "I am," she said, thrusting the index into his coat. [520] "Go argue with a crowd." [525] While he escaped through the window, she faced the magistrate and offered him the empty teacup. [541] He read defeat in it and burned the shelves. [550] But mirrors remember heat, and every scorched shard cast the same names onto the street, bright as thrown paint. [569] Citizens carried the testimony to the court, where Niko stood on a bench and annotated the magistrate's objections aloud until laughter stripped them of power. [594] Vey's exile ended by statute that evening, though she chose the library again by morning. [609] Years later, visitors found her there, still sharp-tongued, still walking backwards whenever a frightened child crossed the threshold. [627] On her desk sat the glass shadow, no longer a prisoner but a sundial marking the hour when lessons began. [647] Niko, grown into a fierce and careful teacher, always arrived with tea and complained that she had gone soft. [666] Vey told him his leaves were sloppy, then pushed the cup toward his waiting students. [681] Outside, the swing stirred and settled, holding its small silence like a place saved for anyone ready to return. [700]</story>
