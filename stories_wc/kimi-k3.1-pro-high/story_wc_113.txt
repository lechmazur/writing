<story>By the third night of the flood, Mara Venn had stopped thinking of the opera house as a building and started hearing it as an instrument holding its breath. [29] Below the drowned stage, lantern light shivered across the seats, and the orchestra pit lay waist deep in black water. [49] Mara, its last double bassist, waded toward her old chair with a lantern hooked to her bow. [66] She had come for the antique opera program locked beneath the conductor's stand, though she told the salvage crew she was rescuing rosin. [89] Lately inspired after years of playing other people's endings, she had begun composing in the margins of rent notices and grocery lists. [111] Then the ghost had started leaving her replies. [119] No face, no voice, only a cold bow-shaped ripple crossing flooded aisles whenever she stopped at the same unresolved measure. [139] The program had belonged to Elena Venn, Mara's grandmother, who conducted one opera here before the management erased her name. [159] Officially, Elena had died before opening night; unofficially, her final chord had been waiting in the pit ever since. [178] Mara found the stand swollen shut and struck its lock with her bow until the wood surrendered. [195] Inside, the program floated free, its gilded cover blooming with brown water damage. [208] She nearly wept at the ruin, but the lantern flickered, and the stains moved. [222] Across each page, water damage created images: a staircase, an open mouth, five fish circling a blank stage. [240] When Mara turned the program sideways, the images aligned with spaces between the printed staves, never with the notes. [259] In between spaces, Elena had hidden a code, using the opera's silences as ink. [273] Mara understood because silence was where the ghost always answered her. [284] She spread the pages on her floating case and read the stained shapes as durations: staircase descending, mouth held open, fish turning five times. [308] Rest, breathe, return. [311] The message pointed not to a melody but to the conductor's forbidden ending, concealed in rests the orchestra had never played. [332] Above her, the salvage crew started the pumps, and their mechanical roar disrupted the hush of unstruck chords. [350] Water drained from the program too quickly; the images began to fade. [362] If the pit emptied now, Elena's code would vanish, and dawn's demolition would bury it beneath concrete. [379] Mara climbed to the pump platform and pulled the main lever, disrupting the salvage schedule and earning a furious shout from below. [401] She did not argue. [405] Instead she lowered her bow into the water and drew it across the string, letting one low note travel through the house. [427] At the first marked rest, she stopped. [434] The lanterns dimmed. [437] In that wordless conversation, the building answered with dripping rafters, settling chairs, and three knocks from inside the conductor's stand. [457] Mara played the next phrase, then yielded to a longer silence. [468] A second lantern kindled on the empty podium, revealing Elena's reflection in the flood: older than Mara remembered, fiercely amused, unfinished. [489] The reflection lifted both hands, not to command her, but to offer the final beat. [504] Mara shook her head. [508] For the first time, she understood that unraveling the code did not mean obeying the dead; it meant hearing what they had left unsaid. [532] She changed the last rest, inserting one breath where Elena had written surrender. [545] The ghost's smile altered. [549] Then Mara played her own ending, a spare ascent built from every silence that had frightened her, while the flood kept time against the seats. [574] The salvage chief entered with his wrench raised, but stopped when the balcony answered with a chord no living musician had touched. [596] Around them, the ruined pages dried in midair, their stains settling into a clear facsimile of Elena's lost score. [615] At dawn, the chief photographed every page for the preservation board, grumbling only when Mara insisted on two composers' names. [635] The opera house would still require years of repair, but demolition became harder to defend than wonder. [652] Before leaving, Mara returned the program to the stand and left her newest composition beside it, its first page almost entirely blank. [674] When water someday found those empty spaces, she hoped someone lately inspired would read them, answer, and change the ending again. [695]</story>
