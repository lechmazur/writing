<story>Above the clouds, where lightning nailed the sky to the mountains, Cassian kept a stormlit observatory and talked to machines because people had stopped answering. [25] He was a lonely visionary with rust on his cuffs, starlight in his teeth, and a plan to shape a new dawn using ancient shadows. [50] The observatory's cobalt glass portico had survived three revolutions, though its blue pillars sang whenever thunder remembered their names. [69] Beneath its arch, Cassian assembled a receiver from coffeepot wire, saint bones, and the brass hearts of dead weather balloons. [89] At midnight, fragmented radio frequencies unfolded through the static, each shard speaking in his own voice, but older. [107] "Dawn is not light," one whispered, lyrically prophetic and crackling. [117] "Dawn is the shadow deciding which way to fall." [126] Cassian laughed once, the bleak little hiccup of a man finding his own obituary useful. [141] The voices predicted a prophet named Sera would climb from the cloud mines in seven days, carrying nothing but a dead sparrow. [163] Cities would kneel; armies would launder their flags; and Cassian must choose which future deserved his allegiance. [180] Each frequency described a variant allegiance: one Cassian serving Sera, one betraying her, one replacing her with a kinder machine. [200] All insisted they were him, merely shadowed by different dawns. [210] On the seventh day, Sera arrived barefoot, soot lacquering her cheeks, the sparrow wired to a mining lamp. [228] "You left the gate open," she said, and the portico unfolded its glass petals with a guilty chime. [246] She was twelve, perhaps, or old enough that numbers had become tactful. [258] Below, the mines had taught her to breathe in thimblefuls; above, Cassian's forecasts had taught whole garrisons to fear her name. [279] "I never broadcast your name," he said. [286] "No," she replied. [289] "You broadcast the shape of the cage, and I grew to fit the door." [303] Stormlight turned the portico into a blue wound as she set the sparrow on his console. [319] Its lamp flickered, and every radio shard spoke together: "Now choose." [330] One future showed Sera crowned in copper, feeding millions while dissidents vanished quietly into weather. [345] Another showed her dead, Cassian adored, and freedom flowering only in licensed window boxes. [359] A third showed no prophets at all, just people arguing over bread with magnificent, tedious courage. [375] Cassian reached for the switch that would bind Sera's voice to every receiver and make her rise unstoppable. [393] His hand shook; shaping dawn had seemed cleaner before dawn acquired chapped lips and a taste for boiled onions. [412] "Will they love me?" Sera asked, not lyrically at all, and that small ragged question broke his beautiful arithmetic. [431] He saw then that ancient shadows were not prophecies or porticos, but the old selves cast forward by every choice. [451] They could stretch a person into a tyrant, a martyr, or something surprising enough to sweep the floor. [469] Cassian tore out the crown-frequency crystal and jammed it into the sparrow's lamp. [482] The bird sputtered awake, furious and mechanical, and bit his thumb. [493] "Excellent," Sera said. [496] "My first miracle was going to be tedious." [504] Together they unfolded the receiver's aerial across the portico, tuning not to one future but to the gaps between frequencies. [524] Into those gaps Cassian sent no commands, only maps of the mines, recipes, storm warnings, and his confession. [542] Sera added a song so filthy that three listening monks reportedly invented upholstery. [555] Across the rise of a prophet, the world had expected trumpets; instead it got static, laughter, and instructions for unlocking doors. [576] The garrisons came at dawn, climbing through cloud with clean rifles and dirty consciences. [590] Cassian stood beneath the cobalt arch, unsure whether to welcome them or drop lightning on their helmets. [607] Then the lowest frequency spoke in his voice, no longer prophetic, only tired: "Allegiance is a door you keep choosing." [627] He opened the portico wider. [632] Sera switched off her lamp, and below them a thousand mine lights answered, not kneeling, not firing, simply rising through the storm. [654] The new dawn arrived grubby, blue, and unresolved, which suited Cassian fine; for once, the shadows belonged to everyone. [673]</story>
