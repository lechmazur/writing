When the storm approached, the pines bowed inland, and Eda followed a path that had not existed at noon. [19] It unspooled beneath her boots with the intimate patience of a remembered hand. [32] Eda had foresight and hindsight only, so she saw the path ending inside a hill and beginning wherever her brother had vanished. [54] The present, however, remained a dark step between certainties. [63] She crossed it by touch, cursing grit from her teeth, determined to discover why some paths choose their walkers. [82] Tomas had followed one seven winters ago; his past still warmed her hindsight, while no future would hold him. [101]

The hill opened into a vine-draped bunker hidden in a rewilded forest, its concrete throat breathing rust and rain. [120] Inside, roots stitched through a machine of brass ribs, each rib twitching toward a lectern. [135] Upon it lay a directive beneath a wafer seal, translucent as horn and freckled with mold. [151] Its mildewed letters read, LET PATH CHOOSE WALKER, but her hindsight supplied the swallowed NO and ITS, revealing a command the machine obeyed in reverse. [176] That was the decaying directive: protection rotted into appetite. [185] To mend it blindly would only feed the engine another command, so Eda did what wardens had done before her. [205] She lay beneath the lectern, pressed the seal's brittle edge to her tongue, and sought an answer through interpreted dreams. [225]

Thunder became a gray sea; Tomas stood on its floor, handing her a key, a bell, and a mouthful of soil. [246] Being precisely playful had always kept Eda's terror exact, so she named the objects Lock, Warning, and Hunger, then asked which one was lying. [270] Tomas rang the bell, and the sea unlocked into thousands of roads, each dragging a barefoot figure toward the bunker. [290] She woke with soil on her lips and interpreted the dream backward: the key was consent, the bell was warning, and the false hunger belonged to the machine. [318] Paths were not predators; they were invitations made vicious by a sentence missing its mercy. [333]

A second dream showed the first wardens baking the seal from forest grain, intending it to soften whenever human certainty grew too hard. [356] They had built failure into their law, trusting some later fool to taste the difference between preservation and imprisonment. [375] Eda laughed once, a cracked little sound, because the fool's future was suddenly hers. [389] Then lightning struck the hill, and every brass rib sang Tomas's name. [401]

The final dream offered no symbols, only her brother standing in rain with empty hands. [416] For the first time, Eda could not find his past or future, and she understood that he was offering her the unbearable present. [439] To take it, she would have to transcend the shelter of knowing what everything had been and must become. [458]

She bit through the wafer seal. [464] It tasted of mildew, wheat, and the iron storms leave in old wells. [477] The directive vanished down her throat; the machine shrieked, then loosened its roots like a fist remembering fingers. [495] Around her, chosen roads split into harmless filaments, releasing sleepers into ditches, doorways, and the astonished weather. [512]

Tomas did not return. [516] His release was not reunion, and Eda's grief remained muddy, weighty, and real. [529] Yet when the bunker door opened, she saw rain hanging above a fern, neither memory nor prophecy, and stepped into its silver percussion. [552] Behind her, the forest began choosing nothing. [559] It simply grew, root by patient root, around the rusted ribs. [570]

Ahead, a narrow trail waited without moving, and Eda understood that it would remain until she offered it her weight. [590] She could see where it had come from and where it might end, but not whether the next stone would roll beneath her heel. [614] That ignorance felt less like blindness than an outstretched hand. [624] She took it, bowing to the storm with a precisely playful flourish before matching its wild step. [641]
