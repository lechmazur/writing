<story>In the murmur of half-remembered dreams, the abandoned spaceport repair bay whispered in a language only Eren Voss could map. [20] He was a sensory map maker, charting invisible roads by their copper taste, pressure against his teeth, and the scents they left on his skin. [45] That night the road came from a green glass shard on his dead mentor's workbench, humming whenever the old reactor exhaled. [66] The crossings were cyclically spontaneous: they returned with every reactor cycle, but chose their instant, as if some traveler on the other side were knocking. [91] Eren had returned for one reason: to discover the weight of a withheld truth. [105] Master Sera had claimed the launch disaster ended in empty space, yet she had spent her final years scratching routes into the shard and weeping at the sound of bells. [135] He lifted it, and cold spread through his palm, carrying rosemary, rain, and a child's three-note song. [152] The bay folded. [155] Beyond its torn air stood the same bay under another sky, where yellow lamps burned and a woman with Eren's scarred hands waited beside a dismantled engine. [182] "You finally felt the bells," she said. [189] Her name was also Eren, though here it belonged to a body and history he had never imagined. [207] She pressed the shard to the engine, and its cracks became a map of luminous threads. [223] Each led through parallel universes, and each smelled of scorched rosemary. [234] "Sera did not save our port by venting the explosion," she said. [246] "She taught it to move." [251] Eren remembered the white flash, the intact ships, and Sera insisting that no bells had rung during his rescue. [270] A bell shuddered through the glass, and the woman flinched, a sound he had always believed was his own invented grief, not memory. [293] But this Eren did. [297] "The disaster arrived here as fire and bells, killing my crew and cracking the engine that keeps our universes distinct." [317] "Now the wound is reopening, and your reactor is calling it home." [329] The truth did have weight: not shame alone, but lives bent around one secret. [343] Eren wanted to reject it, to preserve the gentle mentor he had mourned, but the other bay's silence pressed against his skin like deep water. [368] Then the shard sang three notes, and fire entered the room without heat. [381] Both Erens saw the coming rupture: their bays superimposed, countless others trembling behind them, each one a possible grave. [400] "We can redirect it again," his counterpart said, offering him the engine's coupling. [413] "Somewhere, another us will pay." [418] Eren closed his fingers around the glass until its edge drew blood. [430] A map maker's first duty was not to find the easiest road, but to admit where it led. [448] He tasted the roads: iron for his home, ash for hers, salt for a thousand unfamiliar ports. [465] Instead of choosing one, he began weaving between worlds, looping the rupture through brief passages and returning before any single universe could bear its full force. [491] His counterpart held the coupling, steering by his whispered directions while he used the shard to redirect each thread. [510] The work became a rhythm without a score, pain blooming and vanishing, bells unraveling into rain. [526] At last the rupture thinned into sparks, distributed so widely that no port remembered more than a warm draft. [545] When the crossings closed, Eren stood alone beside Sera's workbench. [555] The shard had turned clear, except for one green vein pointing toward the other bay. [570] He understood then that forgiveness could not lighten what Sera had done; only carrying it honestly could keep the weight from crushing someone unseen. [594] So he made a new map, not of escape routes, but of every world connected to his own, marking its flavors, dangers, and names. [618] At its center he wrote Sera's confession and his counterpart's three-note song. [630] The next time the reactor exhaled, he would answer the bells, not because the truth was beautiful, but because no hidden world should have to bear it alone. [658]</story>
