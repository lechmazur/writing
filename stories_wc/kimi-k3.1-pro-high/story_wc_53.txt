When the carousel halted, its wooden gulls stopped beating the water, and the kinetic lagoon above the clouds began to forget which way was forward. [25]
Across the floating market, awnings froze mid-ripple, oar strokes hung as silver steps, and every bell refused to finish ringing. [45]
Only Mara moved, a resolute scavenger picking her way over the suddenly solid waves toward the silent ride. [63]
If the carousel stayed still until dawn, the lagoon would lose its lift and pour through the cloud floor, market and all. [85]
Mara needed its brass axle, but she needed the dawn more, so she climbed aboard with her pry bar and coil of sound-wire. [108]
On the painted swan sat her own ghost, older by thirty years, holding a disposable camera wheel between transparent teeth. [128]
"You came back after leaving," the ghost said, "and tomorrow you will bury me yesterday." [143]
The carousel had always kept the lagoon's hours in order; without it, Mara felt next winter pressing against her childhood, and the ghost was plainly chronologically confused. [170]
Yet the little toothed wheel was real, and Mara recognized the flour-white scratch she had made on it at sixteen. [190]
It was all that remained of the camera with which she had meant to photograph unknown sky-islands, finance an expedition, and never salvage another dead man's shoes. [217]
Debt, funerals, and practical mornings had eroded that ambition until only scavenging seemed honest. [231]
"Give me the wheel," Mara said. [237]
"It fits the carousel's stripped ratchet." [243]
"You fitted it after I refused," said the ghost. [252]
"Restart the ride, and I become a future you never had; leave it stopped, and we can negotiate forever." [271]
Beneath them, the cloud floor thinned to blue gauze. [280]
Mara could have fought a thief, but negotiation with her own ghost required evidence, so she did what scavengers did: she collected fragments of overheard stories. [306]
The lagoon kept voices in its wreckage, and her sound-wire could tease them out. [320]
From a collapsed ticket booth she drew an old woman's whisper: "She paid my fare when her own boat was ready." [341]
A brass stirrup yielded her father's cough: "Mara sold the camera body for my medicine, not for drink." [359]
A child's shoe offered Mara's fierce young voice: "After one more season, I will go." [374]
The fragments came backward, sideways, sometimes in voices not yet born, but she fixed each to the carousel rail with wire. [395]
Their order mattered less than their weight. [402]
She had not simply surrendered her expedition; she had repeatedly spent pieces of it to keep other people alive, then despised the remainder for being small. [428]
The last fragment waited inside the camera wheel itself, clicking as the ghost turned it. [443]
"Stay," whispered Mara at sixteen, and from somewhere nearer dawn came her present answer: "I am still here." [461]
At last Mara understood why the ghost had stopped time: not to preserve a glorious future, but to make her stop feeding it excuses. [485]
She sat beside it on the swan, their transparent and calloused hands almost touching. [499]
"I regret calling you a coward," she said, "when I was the one who made you carry every abandoned version of me." [521]
Regret did not restore the camera, the seasons, or the dead; it merely stopped pretending that nothing had been lost. [541]
"I cannot give you the life I imagined," Mara said, "but I can stop making you wait for it." [560]
The ghost considered this with the terrible patience of someone who remembered the offer as an ancient refusal. [578]
Then it opened its teeth. [583]
Mara took the wheel, warm as a pulse, set it into the ratchet, and bound its cracked tooth with sound-wire so the mechanism would grip. [608]
Together they pushed the swan, and the carousel lurched, caught, and began turning with a sound like film advancing. [627]
Current unzipped the lagoon; hanging spray fell, bells completed their notes, and the cloud floor thickened beneath the waking stalls. [647]
The ghost thinned with each revolution, now young, now ancient, until it was only a pale exposure on Mara's retina. [667]
Mara did not promise to resurrect her old expedition. [676]
Instead, she unwired the gathered voices and copied them into a salvage ledger, making a map of the lagoon from everything it had almost said. [701]
When the first trader opened his shutters, she bought passage on the morning current with her pry bar, keeping only the ledger and the sound-wire. [726]
She would not chase the ghost across the sky; she would travel slowly enough to hear what the living were saying, and answer before their stories became wrecks. [754]
