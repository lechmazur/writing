<story>When the leash broke, Neri did not call after the dog. [11] The red cord trailed from her fist like a tide line, and Bramble's bell faded up the veiled ridgeway, where evening mist combed the heather. [36] Below, the ferry guides were lighting lamps for their twilight vigil, one flame for every traveler who had crossed the strait afraid and returned able to forgive themselves. [64] Neri had come to keep that vigil, though not to brave anything; she intended only to hold her mother's lamp while the guides sang the water smooth. [91] Now Bramble was gone toward the ridge, and her mother's lamp rocked unlit in her hand. [107] Old Sera, wisest of the ferry guides, listened to the bell's thin ringing and gave Neri a ticket and a punch whose stamp was a small star. [134] "The star is not payment," she said. [141] "It is a promise to come back by a kinder road." [152] Her brother Tamm, the other guide, lifted the gangplank. [161] "The direct path climbs into the wind," he said. [170] "We shall traverse the strait and take the island paths; indirect routes let a frightened creature choose your company." [189] Neri almost refused, for she had spent nineteen years letting others decide which fears she could face. [206] But the star's points pressed her palm, gently persistent as a wave asking entry at a door. [223] She stepped aboard. [226] Their ferry slipped between black reeds while the shoreline lullaby of the guides rose and fell, soft syllables towing the dusk. [247] At the first island, they found only Bramble's prints circling a locked chapel. [260] At the second, a net maker had seen him swimming after a gull, and she offered soup, which Neri accepted though searching seemed more urgent. [285] Each kindness slowed them, and each slowing gave her time to hear what she had mistaken for cowardice: a wish to be safe without being small. [311] By the third island, full twilight had silvered the channels. [321] Across the water, the ridgeway lifted its white veil just enough to show Bramble at the summit, leashless, barking at something Neri could not see. [346] "We can ferry no closer," Tamm said. [353] "The channel has closed." [357] Neri saw the long way back to safety and the steep path forward, shining wetly between rocks. [374] Her legs shook so violently that Sera held out an arm, but Neri did not take it. [391] Instead she asked, "Will you keep my lamp until I return?" [402] "That is what vigils are for," Sera said. [410] The climb was not noble. [415] Neri crawled part of it, wept once, and twice followed goat tracks that bent away from the summit before curving home. [436] The indirect paths gave her breath when courage would have spent itself in one bright dash. [452] At the top she found Bramble guarding a hollow in the stones. [464] Inside lay her mother's old ferry ticket, dropped last winter when Neri, carrying it for luck, had turned back from this same climb. [487] Its star was unpunched. [491] She understood then why Sera had smiled at her lamp: her mother had kept vigils too, waiting without reproach for the daughter who would arrive when ready. [518] Neri set both tickets on a flat stone and used Sera's ticket punch, its star stamp biting cleanly through the old paper, then through her own. [544] No thunder answered. [547] Only Bramble leaned against her knee, and below them the ferry bells began, one note at a time, as if the sea were breathing in its sleep. [574] She had not found a hidden store of unused courage, she thought; courage was not buried anywhere waiting to be seized. [595] It waited in the next kindly chosen step, and in the step after that, patient as a lamp on a far shore. [617] They descended by the sheltered sheep path, Neri carrying the new cord Sera had braided from the broken leash. [636] At the ferry, she lit her mother's lamp from Sera's flame and set it among the others. [653] The vigil would last until moonrise, but Neri no longer mistook waiting for doing nothing. [668] Sometimes it was the shore that taught the wave how to return. [680]</story>
