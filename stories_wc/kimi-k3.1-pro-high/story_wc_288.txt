<story>After the reincarnation memory came, Sen stopped sleeping beside the tidal library's empty pools. [14] It had made her a contemporary of an extinct species, though her body was only nineteen. [30] She remembered being old Nara when the last cloudwhale died below the windows, its calf calling through salt rain. [49] The library stood on a basalt needle above the clouds, and moon-tides still climbed its black stairwells twice a day. [69] At high water, books breathed in their glass cases; at low water, Sen was alone with dripping stone. [87] Since the memory, that solitude had become an instruction: restore the whales, and you will belong again. [104] Nara's brass sundial waited in the upper court, its gnomon green, its face atomically reflective. [119] Nara had polished it until every surface atom returned light, making a noon bright enough to wake the dead. [138] Or so Nara had believed. [143] Sen carried it beyond the library to the ancient stone circle, where nine pillars hummed whenever tide and wind agreed. [163] That hum had guided every awakening in the archive, from marsh seeds to a sleeper's first remembered name. [181] But the catalog called them brittle awakenings: force the note, and what emerged would shatter into hunger or madness. [200] Sen ignored the warning because independence, she thought, meant needing no one's permission to heal the past. [217] She would leave the library only after giving its loneliest ghost a living sky. [231] At dawn she set the dial between the pillars and laid a whale scale beneath its gnomon. [248] Sunrise struck the brass, divided into white roads, and ran ringing through the stones. [262] The circle's hum deepened; the scale swelled into ribs, gauzy fins, and one enormous opening eye. [278] Sen laughed until the eye found her and reflected only Nara's terrified face. [291] Then the calf screamed in an old woman's voice and tore its unfinished body against the stones. [308] It was not rebirth but Nara's grief, given teeth by a command to return. [322] Each impact cracked the court, and below them the library's glass cases began to sing. [337] Sen reached for the dial to double the light, then saw her own reflection multiply across its metal. [355] Every face was different, every life briefly hers, none obliged to finish Nara's work. [369] Independence was not solitude perfected; it was the freedom to choose what could live beside her. [385] She lifted the scale from the gnomon, leaving the creature no command but the circle's ancient hum. [402] Instead of forcing a whale into the world, she hummed the note of her present name. [418] The stones answered unevenly, making room for her uncertainty. [427] The vast shape loosened into luminous mist, and from its grief came smaller cries beyond the cliff. [444] Six ribbon eels rose through the mist, wild little things with no memory of being anything else. [461] They had nested in the stone cracks for years, waiting for a hum that did not sound like a leash. [481] Sen opened her hands, and one curled around her wrist only while it wished. [495] When it let go, she did not call it back. [505] For the rest of the season, Sen repaired the court and unlatched the tidal library's shutters. [521] She lowered shallow basins into the moon-tide so the eels could hunt without fouling the books. [537] In return, their flashing bodies warned her of storms before the barometers stirred. [550] To coexist, she wrote in the catalog, required shelves, labor, and open doors. [563] She kept the brass sundial, but shaded its perfect face with a lattice of shelf wire. [579] Now its reflected noon arrived in gentle pieces, and awakenings began only when the sleeper stirred. [595] Some mornings Sen still woke as Nara, aching for the whale beneath a vanished rain. [610] She let the grief pass through without making it a destination. [621] Then she packed a satchel, crossed the cloud bridge alone, and went to see what lay beyond the maps. [640] At dusk she returned because the library was home, not because it had kept her. [655] Above her, eels and stones hummed different notes, and the whole bright tower held. [669]</story>
