<story>After the happiness mandate ended, the turbines still turned above Edda's farmhouse, their shadows crossing the table like slow blades through bread. [22] The government had called their noise the weather of cheer, and no one had found a legal reason to switch it off. [44] Edda kept her sister's dried flower bouquet beside the stove, its brown heads nodding whenever the windows shook. [62] She had not cried since the burial, where licensed mourners had smiled so hard that their faces seemed to shine. [82] Then Vale arrived, a blank-faced performer carrying a knife, a tin cup, and a bill for services she did not remember ordering. [104] He said the village had pooled its winter coupons to buy her one evening of assisted grieving. [121] Edda let him in because refusing would require an explanation, and explanations had become heavier than furniture. [138] Vale filled the kettle and announced, in a voice built for collapsing kingdoms, that water was approaching its difficult throne. [158] He swept the floor as if exiling an empire of crumbs, and Edda almost objected when something in her chest moved. [179] It was not laughter, only a small lessening, the space a stone leaves when a pocket tears. [196] While the tea cooled, he began to whittle a windfall branch into a narrow bird, laying each pale shaving beside her sister's bouquet. [219] Edda told him that Mira had died in a correction home after being cited three times for visible sorrow. [238] She did not tell him who had made the third report. [249] Vale turned the bird in his hands, his face offering no courtroom and no absolution. [264] He confessed that he had performed in those homes, narrating the serving of soup while guards counted which prisoners refused to smile. [286] His work had kept him fed, and sometimes his jokes had made the counting pause. [301] Sometimes, he said, the pause had only taught the guards to wait. [313] Edda looked at the dried stems and admitted that she had reported Mira to keep the farm from being seized. [333] The confession did not break the air; the turbines continued their patient grinding, and Vale shaved another curl from the wing. [354] He said lightness was not innocence, nor was grief a debt that suffering could finally pay. [370] Then he asked whether she wanted to be punished or to become useful, and the question made both choices sound faintly ridiculous. [392] At dusk, Vale narrated their climb to the control shed as a royal procession of two cold people and one unfinished bird. [414] Edda used her maintenance key to engage the emergency brake, knowing the village clinic would lose its stolen current until morning. [447] One by one, the rotors slowed, and the farmhouse below entered a remade hush, not the silence before the mandate but a thinner thing. [471] It held the tick of cooling metal, Vale's breath, and the dry bouquet knocking gently in Edda's hands. [489] Back at the table, he set down the wooden bird and she gave him one brittle stem. [506] Together they stripped its petals into the cold tea, a useless ceremony that neither improved nor excused anything. [524] Edda expected the old numbness to return, but it merely waited, leaving room for her to hear the cup touch wood. [545] In the morning she would restart the turbines, visit the clinic, and accept whatever restitution the village demanded. [563] She would also keep Vale's bird, though he had carved its beak slightly crooked and its eyes too deep.
