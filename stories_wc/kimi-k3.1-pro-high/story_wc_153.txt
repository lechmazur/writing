<story>Vey, a cryptic medium who answered grief with riddles, poled her skiff into the mangrove labyrinth while the singing spirits braided her dead brother's name through their song. [28] She had come to find the moment before deciding: before she refused Cael's apology, before he rowed into the storm, before silence became the only reply she had left. [57] On her knees lay thirteen unsent letters, twelve from Cael and one from her, each folded around the same furious plea for home. [80] The roots closed behind her, black arches sweating salt, and the spirits sang sweeter whenever she lied to herself about forgiveness. [101] At the labyrinth's heart stood a drowned chapel, its window unbroken though its roof held only moonlight. [118] Above the door, the gargoyle had lost its rainspout; Vey carried that shard in her pocket, a grooved stone tongue Cael had chipped off while fleeing her anger. [146] She set the gargoyle's rainspout shard on the sill, channel down, so dew could run through it and remember the window. [167] Then she breathed Cael's name against the glass, not to summon his ghost, but to evoke the choice still trapped inside his absence. [190] Condensation bloomed, and messages in condensation on windows had always been her clearest revelations: the first line wrote itself, NOT BEFORE. [211] Below it, smaller letters formed: BRING WHAT YOU COULD NOT SEND. [222] Vey laid the letters against the glass, but the chapel ignored Cael's twelve and silvered only her own with frost. [242] Her letter held no apology, only the brutal sentence she had never dared to mail: I wanted you to suffer because I did. [265] The spirits stopped singing, and in the space where an apology waits unspoken, Vey heard her brother bail water from a sinking boat. [288] There was no vision, no mercy of distance; there was only his cold hand around hers and the storm asking whether she would let go. [313] She understood then that the moment before deciding was not Cael's last chance, but hers. [328] The roots split her palms as she hauled against his weight, and though no living body rose from the water, she held on until her fingers tore. [355] Moonlight touched her through the ruined roof, and Vey, who heals in moonlight but never without pain, felt each wound close around a splinter of salt. [381] She welcomed the pain; tenderness without fierceness would have been another lie. [393] On the window, Cael wrote: I DID NOT COME TO BE FORGIVEN. [405] Vey pressed her bleeding palm beside the words and answered aloud, "Good, because I did not come to forgive you." [425] The spirits surged hungrily, their song sharpening into the accusations she had rehearsed for six years. [441] "I came to stay with you before you chose," she said, "and to choose what your death cannot choose for me." [462] She opened Cael's twelve letters at last, reading each by moonlight: seed orders, recipes, jokes, small ordinary rebellions against despair. [482] None contained the villain she needed, nor the saint she wanted; they contained a frightened man asking his sister to plant beans if the flood spared their field. [510] The final envelope held a bean, hard as a little heart. [521] Vey's rage did not vanish; it changed its grip, becoming both shield and spade. [535] She pressed her own letter to the glass and wrote through the condensation, I AM SORRY I MADE YOUR FEAR CARRY MY PAIN. [558] The apology did not absolve either of them; it simply stepped, at last, into the room between them. [576] Cael's hand loosened, not because she released him, but because he no longer had to hold her there. [594] The window cleared, the singing spirits resumed a low cradle tune, and the chapel gave back her letter, its seal broken, and she read it aloud until the ink stopped shaking. [625] Vey placed the bean in the gargoyle's stone channel and poured the last of her drinking water over it. [644] Nothing grew, which made her laugh once, wetly; the dead were not required to perform miracles for the living. [663] At dawn she poled out carrying all thirteen letters, no longer unsent, for she had read them into the labyrinth's listening dark. [685] Back home, she planted the bean beneath her window, where rain would find it without any stone tongue to guide it. [706] When the first leaves came, crooked and fierce, Vey left the gate unlatched, not as forgiveness, but as the place forgiveness could enter if it ever learned her name. [735]</story>
