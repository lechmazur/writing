<story>Mira Venn was the golden-hour portrait thief, though she never stole a frame, a canvas, or anything the police could name. [21] With a sable brush, she lifted only the instant when sunset made a painted stranger seem about to speak. [40] Collectors paid fortunes for those illuminated moments, never noticing that their ancestors afterward looked merely well fed. [57] Mira was quietly savvy about locks, lies, and longing, but her true motive fit inside one inherited sentence. [75] Before vanishing, her mother had whispered, "To press your ear against destiny's door, first steal back your own light." [94] On the last evening of summer, a boy delivered flowers to her attic: green bells, white asters, and one stolen tulip. [115] Her mother had taught her codes in flower arrangements, where bellflowers meant travel, asters meant midnight, and a tulip laid sideways meant self. [138] The ribbon's knot added a command: come before the gold dies. [149] Mira took her brush and the jar of captured moonbeams her mother had left sealed with wax. [166] She reached Emerald Station under a fleeting golden sunset, its glass roof burning green above the last westbound train. [185] No timetable named that train, yet every door stood open, breathing out warm air and violin music. [202] On the platform, the stationmaster wore three asters upside down: midnight reversed, or perhaps a warning against it. [220] "You have until moonrise," he said, unlocking the waiting room. [230] Inside hung Mira's portrait, painted twenty years earlier, though it showed her exactly as she stood now: brush in hand, fear brightening her face. [254] Behind her painted shoulder waited a black door with her mother's initials where a keyhole should be. [271] Mira understood the commission at last, and understanding felt like being pushed from a height. [286] She swept her brush through the sunset on the canvas and lifted her own illuminated moment. [302] The painted face emptied; the real station vanished; she stood before the black door in a darkness without echoes. [321] When she pressed her ear to it, she heard hundreds of voices naming trains, disasters, lovers, and children not yet born. [342] Then her mother said, quite clearly, "Choose one, and it will choose you back." [356] Mira's fingers closed around the brush, hungry for an answer that would make every stolen year worthwhile. [373] One voice promised her mother's return; another promised fame; a third offered a life untouched by grief. [390] Each sounded true, and each sounded like a cell with music in it. [403] She opened the moonbeam jar, expecting its light to reveal which voice was destiny. [417] Instead, silver spilled across the door and showed thousands of fine cracks, each one a future someone else had surrendered. [437] Her mother had not vanished through destiny's door; she had become its lock, holding back the futures people begged to avoid. [458] Mira could free her by choosing, but some stranger would inherit the burden, painted and waiting. [474] The thief in her reached for the loophole; the daughter nearly settled for revenge. [488] Then she did what no client had ever paid her to do: she chose to reinterpret the portrait. [506] It was not a command to steal her light, but a mirror showing where she had hidden it. [524] With moonbeams thinning on her brush, Mira painted the black door open, not onto one future, but onto the moonlit platform. [545] Every voice rushed through her and became a flower: asters for endings, bells for departures, tulips blazing sideways for selves reclaimed. [566] The door dissolved into a bouquet too vast for any code, and the lock opened from within. [583] Her mother stepped out, older by twenty years, laughing with luminous disbelief as the station filled with moonlight. [601] Around them, the nameless train unfolded into carriages of possible lives, then faded because no one was forced aboard. [620] "You found the answer," her mother said. [627] Mira thought of the emptied portraits waiting in wealthy houses, their stolen radiance humming in her attic. [644] "No," she said, "I found better instructions." [651] At dawn, mother and daughter left Emerald Station carrying an empty jar and a brush heavy with ordinary sunlight. [670] Mira had one last collection to return, and after that, whole unscripted mornings to paint. [685]</story>
