<story>The package arrived on the noon tide, though no tides had entered the drowned city of Vael for three hundred years. [21] It lay dry on the library steps, wrapped in undertaker's black, while beyond the glass dome fish moved like slowly turning pages. [43] Elian Voss, the city's weary undertaker and last living clerk, knew every knock its forgotten underwater library made, and this silence was worse. [66] Usually the drowned dead tapped their distress signals through the catalog pipes, three knocks for a name lost, two for a grave washed empty. [90] Elian answered because unrecorded dead could not rest, and because he was inadvertently stubborn in the gentle, ruinous way of men who call refusal just one more duty. [118] Inside the package lay a broken lighthouse lens, its crescent fracture cloudy with salt, and a brass label bearing his own address. [140] Beneath it, someone had written: Revitalize the beacon before the last signal becomes an echo. [155] The lighthouse had crowned the library when Vael stood above the sea, guiding ships and burying storms; after the city sank, its beam guided the dead toward names cut in stone. [186] When the lens broke, the graves filled with silt, and the dead returned to knocking. [201] Elian wanted to set the package down and sleep beside the atlas cases. [214] Instead the pipes knocked once, a signal he had never heard, and every cabinet in the library opened together. [233] Cards slid out like white tongues, each blank except for his name. [245] Stillness chased him down the aisle as he ran, not silence but a listening hush that swallowed his fins, his breath, even the distant bell of his diving suit. [274] He reached the sealed geography room only because the old building had taught him its trick of occupying impossible spaces. [294] Where two shelves declared a wall, he could enter the narrow conviction between them; where a stair ended underwater, he could stand on the dry step it remembered. [322] Thus he crossed the locked room without opening it and climbed through the ceiling by occupying the space beneath a footprint left centuries ago. [346] In the lantern chamber, the distress calls became visible: thousands of pale threads pulled through the water, each tied to a blank catalog card. [370] They were not asking for rescue. [376] They were asking where to stop. [382] The fracture in the lens matched the city's burial atlas, and Elian understood that glass alone could not repair it. [402] A lighthouse needed a coast, a grave needed a name, and rest needed a permanent address. [418] He set the lens in its iron cradle, then pressed his brass label into the broken arc. [435] The package had not been sent to him; it had been addressed by him, in the firm hand of an older man who had finally found the end of his shift. [466] His stubbornness had simply taken the long way home. [475] Elian opened the atlas and began reading names, first the drowned he had buried, then those whose graves had vanished, then the librarians, the keepers, the children chalked on doorstep stones. [506] Each name entered the lens as ink enters water, and the threads brightened into streets. [521] The beacon did not shine outward; it revitalized the buried avenues beneath them, lighting doorways in mausoleums, numbers on tombs, small brass plates on urns. [546] Across Vael, the dead followed their signals home. [554] One card remained blank in Elian's hand. [561] He knew the price of a permanent address was that someone must remain to receive the mail, and for the first time his weariness frightened him. [587] Then the oldest thread touched his wrist, warm as a living finger, and pulled him toward a narrow gap between the lens and its cradle. [612] There was no room there for a man, only for the space his duty had occupied. [628] Elian stepped out of that space and left it standing. [638] The library kept him then, not his body but his stubborn watch, a quiet clerk forever stamping received beneath the names of the dead. [662] He awoke on the library steps with dry hair and an empty chest of tools, while above him the lens turned whole, its fracture shining like a stitched seam. [691] No knocks followed. [694] The stillness that had chased him finally sat beside him, and together they watched the beacon write calm addresses across the dark. [716] When his shift bell sounded far below, Elian did not answer. [727] Rest had found him at last, correctly addressed. [735]</story>
