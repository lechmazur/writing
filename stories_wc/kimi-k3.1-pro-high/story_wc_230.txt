<story>After the empathy virus, truth stopped being a virtue and became a symptom. [13] Mara Venn, a rational truffle forager, had learned to answer questions before anyone asked them. [28] It spared strangers the shock of hearing her fear inside their own chests. [41] Her involuntary honesty was less merciful at home. [49] "I still love you," she told her brother, "but I imagine leaving every night." [63] Tomas nodded, though the shared ache of her restlessness bent him double. [75] Their settlement occupied a hanging orchard in a ravine battered by storms, where apple terraces swung on cables and truffle pigs rooted beneath wet nets. [100] The virus had made privacy feel like cruelty, yet constant exposure had made kindness dangerous. [115] Mara wanted neither silence nor confession; she wanted to cultivate psychological safety, a place where people could choose what to share. [136] Choice required a door, and doors required something the wardens called occlusion. [148] Officially, occlusion was a superstition from discredited textbooks, shelved beside bloodletting and prayers for rust. [163] Mara read them anyway, testing rumored cures with scales, spores, and patients who volunteered. [177] Most failed beautifully. [180] Fermented pear reduced nightmares but attracted hornets; powdered mirror made three lovers unable to recognize themselves. [196] Then a smuggled relic arrived beneath her best truffles: an antique glass eye, green as deep water. [213] Its customs label read SURGICAL DEMONSTRATION, a lie so serene that Mara admired it. [227] The sender, her dead mother's old rival, enclosed a page describing an eye that could occlude feeling without erasing it. [247] "Do not look through it," the page warned. [255] Naturally, Mara built a clamp. [260] She fitted the eye above Tomas's bed, reasoning that if shared emotion traveled by attention, interrupted sight might interrupt contagion. [280] For six nights he slept without dreaming her escapes. [289] On the seventh, he asked whether she had experimented on him. [300] "Yes," said Mara, because refusal was impossible and evasion merely uglier. [311] He struck the clamp, and the eye rolled beneath a basket. [322] Before she could apologize, storm sirens began their ruined music. [332] A cable snapped above the eastern terrace. [339] Forty neighbors fled onto the bridge, each carrying everybody else's terror, until panic became a single animal with many legs. [359] The wardens ordered confession, believing truth would calm them. [368] Instead, names of the missing tore through the crowd. [377] Mara saw no virtue in adding hers. [384] She climbed the swaying orchard, hooked the glass eye into a broken lantern, and aimed its dark aperture across the bridge. [405] The page had called the final step a cure, but its diagram showed only shading, the tender occlusion of a wound. [426] Mara turned the aperture halfway. [431] Nothing vanished. [433] The storm remained, and the missing remained missing. [441] Yet terror stopped pouring from mouth to mouth; each person felt its edges and found their own hands again. [460] They crossed in small groups, asking before touching. [468] Tomas returned to help Mara down. [474] "I was cruel," he said. [479] "You were frightened," she answered, "and I used you without asking." [490] The words hurt cleanly, without consuming either of them. [499] At dawn, the wardens came for the relic. [507] Mara could not hide her answers, so she hid the question's object: she dropped the eye into a truffle sack and let her pig swallow it. [533] "Where is the relic?" they demanded. [539] "Inside my breakfast," Mara said, which was involuntarily truthful, temporally ambitious, and sufficient. [552] While they searched the terraces, the pig trotted into the ravine mist, looking profoundly innocent. [567] By the time Mara recovered the eye, the settlement had held its first meeting without mandatory confession. [584] People sat behind screens woven from storm nets, emerging only when they wished. [597] No one called it cured. [602] Mara mounted the glass eye above the meeting door, its aperture turned halfway, neither blind nor watching. [619] Tomas asked if she still imagined leaving. [626] "Every night," she said. [630] He flinched, then stayed. [634] Outside, the orchard knocked against its cables, broken fruit shining among the leaves. [647] Inside, they practiced the difficult mercy of a closed door, and the greater mercy of knocking. [663]</story>
