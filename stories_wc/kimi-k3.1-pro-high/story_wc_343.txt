<story>Aboard the antique airship Vesper, where merchants drank tea beneath brass rafters and nobody ever seemed alone, Ivo practiced the sociable art of keeping to himself. [26]
He was the curious map apprentice, diligently curious even when curiosity meant chalk on his cuffs and supper missed again. [46]
His master had told him to note the old ship's leaks, but Ivo intended to chart the next great discovery, whatever the captain said about practical work. [73]
That evening, starling murmurations folded around the hull, thousands of wings making one dark body, and every lamp in Vesper answered by dimming. [96]
The passengers laughed and pressed to the windows, while Ivo alone heard a voice beneath their laughter repeating three words in six languages. [119]
He followed it to the observation gondola, where a stranded luminance pulsed inside a glass buoy, too weak to rise and too bright to look at directly. [146]
Vesper's first aeronauts had bottled wandering light for ballast lamps; Ivo suddenly understood why the ship never flew true after sunset. [167]
The voice came again, and he opened his polyglot's phrasebook, finding its dog-ear at a page his predecessor had marked with a tiny star. [191]
Among farewells and market greetings, one obsolete entry read: Light speaks in distances; answer with a road. [208]
Below it, in six translations, waited the words he had heard: Not your lantern. [222]
Ivo looked up and saw the starlings pouring through the dusk in loops that ended at the gondola floor. [241]
There, half hidden by a velvet settee, lay a dusty hopscotch board, its numbered squares scuffed by generations of children. [261]
He worked by noting secret patterns in the dusty hopscotch board: chalk stems became flock lines, and skipped numbers became intervals between the luminance's pulses. [286]
Each note on his chart joined a square, a wingbeat, and a word from the phrasebook, until the children's game revealed a corridor through the flock. [312]
It was a map drawn for light, not airships. [321]
Then the captain entered with two engineers, demanding to know why Ivo had opened the buoy's valves. [338]
Behind them, passengers crowded the doorway, still cheerful, still curious, and suddenly very quiet. [352]
Ivo showed them the chart and explained that Vesper had been steering by a prisoner whose homesickness pulled at every compass. [373]
The captain touched the phrasebook's dog-ear and asked whether releasing it would leave them dark above the mountains. [391]
Ivo admitted that it would, but pointed to the last pattern: starlings crossing square ten, then turning together toward clear sky. [412]
Usually he listened from the margins, but now he moved among them, asking an engineer for a mirror, a tea seller for silver trays, and the children for chalk. [441]
Together they tilted the trays until the buoy's last glow ran along Ivo's chalk road and out through the open hatch. [462]
For one breath nothing happened. [467]
Then the luminance unfolded, no larger than a sparrow and wider than the horizon, and entered the corridor the starlings had kept open. [490]
The flock brightened around it, each bird carrying a fleck of gold, and Vesper's lamps went dark. [507]
Someone gasped, but the children began laughing as light returned outside, moving in a river ahead of the bow. [526]
The river revealed a pass between peaks that no survey had recorded, blue with dawn although dawn was hours away. [546]
The captain ordered the ship to follow, then quietly gave Ivo back his phrasebook. [560]
All around him, people argued happily about who had helped most, and Ivo, still apart at the chart table, found he did not mind their company. [586]
He noted the pass, the flock's turning intervals, and the exact place where freed light became a road. [604]
On the margin he drew a small star, not for the prisoner they had lost, but for the guide it had chosen to become. [628]
Below it he wrote the name of his discovery: Murmuration Pass. [639]
Then, because a road should invite others, he left the chart where anyone could add to it. [656]</story>
