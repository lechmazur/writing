<story>At dawn, the shadowed library held its breath, and Master Ilyan Voss, a reticent scholar who had spent forty years avoiding speech, was asked to testify. [26] The last dictionary had been printed at midnight, its final page declaring language complete, yet by morning green letters had rooted in the margins and branched across the floor. [55] The judges called it decay; Ilyan called it growth, though at first only inside his notebook. [71] Before them lay the crystal he had unhooked from the chandelier, the crime for which he might lose his post. [91] It was sonically reflective, returning not voices exactly, but the moments between them, splintered and bright. [107] "Begin with the theft," said the senior judge. [115] Ilyan turned the crystal, and a suspended hush filled the hall, so dense that the dust seemed to wait above the shelves. [137] "I will testify through fractured sequences," he said, "because growth does not obey our order." [152] In the fractured sequence reflected by the crystal, the judges saw tomorrow's door opening onto a garden where the west wall had stood. [175] Then came yesterday: Ilyan on a ladder, easing the crystal loose while the printed dictionaries cooled below. [192] Last came the first morning, when his assistant, Nell, touched a green letter and vanished without a cry. [210] The senior judge flinched. [214] "You concealed her disappearance." [218] "I preserved it," Ilyan answered. [223] His reticence had begun as a child's bargain: if he never spoke carelessly, no one he loved would be reduced to a careless story. [247] Age had weathered the bargain, but not the innocent hope beneath it. [259] He set his notebook beside the crystal. [266] Its pages mapped every green branch, and each fork matched a pause in the library's old recordings. [283] To decode the pattern of growth, he had needed the silences the chandelier had been collecting for a century. [302] That was why he took the crystal, and why the hush followed him down from the rafters. [319] The judges saw another fragment: Nell in the catalogue room, reading unfamiliar shoots while Ilyan slept beside the printing press. [339] She was young enough to believe a finished dictionary was an invitation rather than a wall. [355] "They are growing toward names we have not spoken," she had told him. [368] He had answered by closing the book. [375] Shame moved across his face now, quietly as dawn. [384] Ilyan rotated the crystal again, aligning its brightest face with the living script. [397] The reflected pauses arranged themselves into roots, stems, and a final opening like a listening ear. [413] From that opening came Nell's voice, not lost, only held where an unspoken name had yet to flower. [431] "Testimony is not what happened," she said. [438] "It is the room we make for what happens next." [448] The senior judge reached to cover the crystal, fearing evidence that could answer back. [462] Ilyan caught his sleeve, then released it, astonished by his own courage. [474] "She left the last name for me," he said. [483] "Not Voss, but stay." [487] It was the word she had wanted from him when he closed the book, and the word he had withheld when she stepped into the green. [513] He spoke it now. [517] The crystal rang without sound, returning every hush it had ever kept. [529] Letters lifted from the floor like leaves reversing autumn, and Nell stepped out of their bright wreckage, one hand full of soil. [551] Behind her, the west wall opened onto no garden yet, only a door and the pale work of morning. [570] The judges asked for a verdict, but Ilyan had none to give them. [583] He gave them his notebook instead, its careful branches ending in blank space. [596] Then he asked Nell to help him carry the dictionaries into the light, where their finished definitions could begin the slow, forgiving labor of becoming incomplete. [622]</story>
