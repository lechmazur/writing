When Sera's fever broke, the mountain weather station was humming with wind, and the aurora beyond its windows looked like green grief trying to speak. [25]
For three days she had dreamed of Niko climbing toward her with medicine, while the real Niko lay somewhere beneath the pass. [47]
The station called Sera its oracle because her forecasts could be wrong without becoming useless. [62]
She fed them failed sensors, contradictory winds, and her own uncertainty, and they bent around each fault instead of shattering. [82]
Hers were swaying certainties, useful precisely because they admitted their motion. [93]
Only once had she hidden a fault, when the north gauge froze and Niko asked whether the pass would hold until dawn. [115]
Wanting the medicine and fearing the empty station, she had answered with a certainty she did not possess. [133]
Now his botanical illustration lay on her blanket, recovered with his pack and delivered by silent rescuers. [150]
It showed dwarf avens rooted in a cliff, each white petal exact, each leaf veined with his patient hand. [169]
Sera could not look at its center, so she remained peripherally aware of the blank margins while she watched the storm instruments. [191]
At the edge of sight, a line of tiny numbers seemed to tilt whenever the window brightened. [208]
She turned the paper and found Niko's observations crowded there: temperature, cloud height, the hardening of snow under his boots. [228]
He had measured what her dead gauge could not, and the final entry read, "Your dawn is sliding closer." [247]
The words did not accuse her; they warned her, hours before the avalanche, and he had kept climbing anyway. [266]
Sera pressed the sheet to her mouth, then jerked away, afraid she might swallow this truth as she had swallowed the other. [288]
Her damp thumb left a smudge across the roots, exposing older pencil beneath the ink. [303]
She almost wept at damaging him, but the pale strokes formed a second sentence: "Do not make my choice your weather." [324]
Below it he had drawn the avens again, not on the cliff but in the wide white border, holding the page together from outside its frame. [350]
Sera understood that he had survived his whole life by occupying margins, finding room where official maps and other people's plans allowed none. [373]
She had mistaken his marginal life for a life she could spend. [385]
The confession she owed the rescue inquiry was not merely that she had lied, but why. [401]
She had believed need could make a forecast solid, and love could be summoned safely through any storm. [419]
To find the weight of that swallowed truth, Sera carried the illustration to the snowfall balance in the instrument room. [439]
She laid the paper on one pan and her station keys on the other, because the keys represented silence, salary, shelter, and the name of oracle. [465]
The keys dropped hard; the paper rose, weightless as an excuse. [476]
Then she removed the keys and set down her radio log, open to the false entry she would now correct. [496]
Nothing balanced, and Sera laughed once, a raw sound that the wind took through the walls. [512]
Truth was not an object awaiting measurement, but a load placed back on the shoulders that had set it down. [532]
She returned to the radio, where a new storm was combing the western peaks. [546]
Three climbers waited below the pass, asking whether to cross. [556]
Sera gave them the failed gauge, the shifting wind, and the margins of possible survival. [571]
"Turn back," she said, and added the words that once would have felt like weakness: "I do not know." [590]
Then she transmitted her confession to the inquiry, leaving no silence in which another certainty could grow. [607]
Near dawn the climbers answered from the valley, alive and angry, and Sera accepted both mercies. [623]
She pinned Niko's illustration beside the console, not as pardon and not as punishment, but as a border around every future voice. [645]
Outside, the aurora thinned into gray morning, and its sorrow did not leave her; it simply stopped asking to be called light. [667]
