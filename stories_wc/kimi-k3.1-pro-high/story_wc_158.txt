<story>Inside the gargantuan shell of a dead moon-snail, Neri had been awake for three hundred years. [16]
He was an insomniac poet because sleep returned him to the day the reef fleets burned, and his beloved Sola sailed beyond the sky. [40]
Her only message was a galaxy postcard, its silver spiral dusted with salt, its back reading, "End their war, then come find me among the stars." [66]
Across centuries of longing, Neri had polished those words with his thumb, wondering how one shy man could end a war older than the shell. [91]
Both reef nations bred soldiers before children could swim, and both claimed the shell as a shrine to their separate dead. [112]
Neri alone tended the dark garden in its spiral chamber, where translucent blossoms fed on lamplight and whatever sorrow he whispered over their roots. [136]
Their petals glowed when his grief thinned, as if his only gift was to photosynthesize pain into beauty. [154]
He had never considered that a gift could also be a strategy until the night two wounded captains crawled inside, each fleeing the other's massacre. [179]
They raised their knives, saw the garden kindle beneath Neri's terrified voice, and lowered them to listen. [196]
For one hour, enemies slept like brothers while Neri kept watch, astonished and jealous. [210]
At dawn they resumed arguing over which nation owned the miracle. [221]
Neri almost fled, but Sola's command felt warm beneath his thumb. [232]
Instead, he proposed a blossom armistice: wherever his flowers opened, no weapon could be carried, no captive taken, no debt of blood collected. [255]
The captains laughed until he offered them a garden shared by both armies, with gates wide enough for every grieving mother. [276]
Their silence gave him a shyly unstoppable courage. [284]
He would ensconce the postcard at the shell's pearly crown, where Sola's stars could see his answer. [301]
Then he carried seedpods through trenches and bone-strewn shallows, speaking terms too softly for generals but clearly enough for the exhausted. [322]
He planted them via half-sung lullabies carried on an off-key wind, because the shell had taught him that imperfect music entered where anthems could not. [347]
Each song opened a furrow; each furrow held one name from both nations' rolls of dead. [363]
Soldiers began deserting to hear their children named without hatred. [373]
Generals shelled the gardens, but the moon-snail's sisters answered from offshore, rising like pale islands and catching the iron in their ancient whorls. [396]
By winter, the safest road between capitals was the blooming one, and commerce accomplished what treaties never had. [414]
Still, peace needed a place to begin publicly. [422]
On armistice morning, Neri stood beneath the postcard while thousands crowded the shell, their spearpoints muffled in petals. [440]
His voice failed. [443]
Then a captain's daughter hummed his cracked tune, another child joined, and the whole reef sang badly enough to make the flowers blaze. [466]
The captains signed in sap, because ink would not hold on petal paper. [479]
No trumpets followed, only children laughing and the tide combing weapons from the shallows. [493]
Neri felt reef-shadow joy: not a blaze of rescue, but cool light under water, with room for sorrow to swim beside delight. [515]
That night he finally slept, and dreamed without burning. [524]
When he woke, the postcard's silver spiral was turning. [533]
A small light descended through it and became Sola, worn by distance, carrying star seeds in her hair. [551]
"You took your time," she said. [557]
"So did you," he answered, hiding behind the shell's rim until she laughed and pulled him into the morning. [576]
Together they planted the star seeds along the armistice road, where their roots would bind both shores against the next ambitious tide. [598]
Neri still grieved, and sometimes still woke before dawn, but now he wrote beside her while the garden drank his darkness and flowered. [621]
Above them, the galaxy turned on, no longer a summons, but a window left open for whoever needed a way home. [642]</story>
