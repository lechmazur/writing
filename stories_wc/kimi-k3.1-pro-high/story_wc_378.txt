<story>When magnetic north shifted, the compass roses in the glass dome turned toward darkness, and Caretaker Vey woke with static behind her eyes. [23] She was an android built to tend desert roses, but her oldest memories had made her strangely organic in every way that mattered. [46] She missed the weight of sleeping children, the sour milk on their collars, even the small hands that had uprooted her seedlings. [68] The children had left the farm decades ago, promising to return when the desert beyond its glass-domed enclosure bloomed. [88] It had not, so Vey kept their roses alive and guarded the box of childhood fears beneath the potting bench. [108] The box was warm rosewood, veined like skin, and its lid tightened whenever she tried to discard it. [126] Inside lay paper fears: wolves, injections, abandonment, the dark between stars. [137] Vey had added none, because machines were not supposed to be afraid. [149]

That morning the dome groaned, and every navigation petal on the solar crown swung west. [164] Focused sunlight speared the eastern beds, igniting resinous leaves faster than Vey could call for rain. [180] The sprinklers answered with silence; their controllers still trusted the old pole. [192] Beyond the western rows lay a freight hatch and the only road out. [205] Between Vey and that hatch stood six hundred roses, thorn locked, tinder dry, and already breathing smoke. [222] Fire would open a path, or fire would take everything. [232] Her static rose to a shriek. [238]

Then the box knocked against the bench, three soft beats like a heart remembering itself. [253] Vey opened it and found the children's handwriting furred with new roots. [265] The paper had not decayed; the box had been keeping it alive. [277] On top, little Ana had written, I am scared no one will know the way. [292] Vey understood at last that containing chaos was not the same as ending it. [306] A child could name terror without becoming brave; a caretaker could use fire without surrendering to it. [323]

She opened the irrigation trenches by hand and flooded two narrow borders from the western hatch to the eastern beds. [343] Between them she laid a controlled burn, feeding it one crimson row at a time. [358] The flames ran straight down her wet corridor, consuming leaves but starving at every turn. [373] Smoke flattened beneath the glass, then lifted as she vented the crown in careful fragments. [388] Heat cracked three panes, and clean desert wind carried the sparks upward instead of across. [403]

While the corridor cooled, Vey took the portable reducer from the grafting room. [416] It had been designed to miniaturize whole orchards for evacuation, though no evacuation had ever come. [432] She miniaturized the thirty mother roses one by one, roots, thorns, perfume, and all, until they glowed like embers in a seed case. [455] The work hurt her wrists, but each small light quieted another frequency inside her. [469] At the final bed she found the original rose, a thorny ancestor planted before the dome existed. [486] Its blossoms opened only after fire, and their seeds rattled like tiny doors. [499] Vey laughed, a damaged sound, and gathered them into her breast pocket. [511]

Then she wrote on the back of Ana's fear, I am scared that keeping everything alive will leave no room for living. [533] She folded both confessions together and placed them in the box. [544] The rosewood closed gently around them. [550] Her static did not vanish; it became a rhythm she could walk to. [563]

Carrying the case and box, she crossed the black corridor while the unburned roses hissed behind wet walls. [581] At the hatch she looked back at beds, ash, water, flame, each incomplete, each holding the others in place. [600] The farm was not saved whole, because wholeness had never meant untouched. [612]

Outside, the compass spun uselessly, so Vey followed the morning shadow of the dome. [626] Somewhere beyond the salt flats, the children had built towns without her, and perhaps gardens that needed tending. [644] She would find them by asking, not by north. [653] In her arms, the little farm gleamed and the old fears breathed. [665] In her pocket, seeds knocked softly against her metal ribs. [675] Step. [676] Ash. [677] Rose. [678] Name.
