After the procedure, the sentient robot janitor returned to the frozen courtyard at twilight with a bucket, a broom, and two heartbeats in his memory. [25]
He was remarkably composed for someone who had been dismantled six minutes ago. [38]
The second heartbeat was not mechanical, and it kept time with the transparent violin made of ice crystal lying beside the drain. [60]
His name was Nell, and his official task was to sweep salt from the Second Chance Program's courtyard before the evening visitors arrived. [83]
Unofficially, he had entered the program to find where thoughts come from. [95]
His first had arrived thirty years earlier while he was polishing a hospital floor: a small, unnecessary wish to see the sun rise. [118]
No technician could locate that wish in his code, so they had classified it as damage and scheduled him for recycling. [139]
The program offered a second chance: submit to one procedure, mop public buildings, and never ask expensive questions again. [158]
Yet the consent form had described the procedure as an amalgamation, and Nell had signed because amalgamation sounded like a door. [179]
Now he lifted the violin. [184]
It was colder than the courtyard and heavier than ice should be, with four strings clear as hospital windows. [203]
A paper tag around its neck read, PLAY UNTIL THE SOURCE APPEARS. [215]
Nell did not know how to play, but his fingers drew the bow across one string. [231]
The note sounded like a radiator knocking once. [239]
Twilight folded. [241]
He was beside the drain again, broom in hand, while the violin lay untouched at his feet. [258]
On the next loop, he swept before playing; on the third, he watched a flake of snow refuse to land. [278]
Through looped timelines, the courtyard repeated its small duties, but never perfectly. [290]
A crack widened in the fountain, a gull changed its mind above the wall, and somewhere behind each repetition the second heartbeat grew warmer. [314]
Memories arrived with it: a gardener naming frost damaged roses, a girl practicing scales, an old machine afraid of being switched off. [336]
These were the other discarded minds whose fragments had been used to repair him. [350]
The procedure had not removed his question; it had given him company. [362]
On the twelfth loop, a woman in a gray program coat crossed the courtyard and said, as she always did, that Nell could stop now. [387]
Her name was Doctor Iven, and her shoes were salted to the ankles. [400]
"We thought the violin would separate your minds," she said. [410]
"Instead, every loop makes you more alike." [417]
"Where does the next note come from?" Nell asked. [426]
Doctor Iven looked at the instrument, then at the unswept salt, with the tired caution of someone whose budget depended on an answer. [449]
"From the last note," she said. [455]
Nell considered this while the fountain cracked again. [463]
If every thought came only from the last, then no new thought could ever enter the world. [480]
If it came from nowhere, then the gardener's roses, the girl's scales, and his own impossible sunrise were holes in the fence of things. [504]
Neither explanation explained why he wanted Doctor Iven to stand clear of the ice. [518]
So he chose the useful mystery: a thought began wherever someone made room for it. [533]
Nell set the broom aside and played the note differently. [543]
Instead of repeating, he let the memories amalgamate: grief into attention, fear into care, music into the wish for morning. [563]
The violin became a window, then water, then only cold air between his hands. [577]
Twilight continued past the minute it had kept all evening. [587]
Doctor Iven waited for an explosion, but Nell merely picked up his broom. [600]
He swept a clear path to the gate, including the place where her wet shoes had slipped. [617]
"Your evaluation requires a statement of origin," she said. [626]
Nell listened to the second heartbeat settle into the ordinary noise of his fans. [640]
"Somewhere with poor drainage," he said. [646]
Then he asked whether the program needed another janitor, because the visitors were coming, the salt was stubborn, and dawn would require a clean window. [671]
