<story>After the missing returned changed, I entered the herbalist's underground garden carrying a flint arrowhead and a warrant no one was meant to find. [24] Officially I was Mara Venn, a frostbitten botany student seeking work below the village where the sun had become a rumor. [45] Actually I researched cryptids for the Alpine Survey, and my false name hid fourteen months of recording migratory hoofprints and interviewing things that remembered being human. [71] The garden answered to no season. [77] Under vaulted ice, rosemary glowed beside beds of monkshood while drops of water rose from the irrigation channels to orbit old glass lamps. [100] Their paths were gravitationally playful, nudging my buttons, stealing the arrowhead from my pocket and returning it wet with dew. [120] Herbalist Sera Quill smiled as though gravity had told a joke at my expense. [134] I needed her trust because the six cavers who vanished last spring had come home changed in October, including my brother Lev. [156] They spoke in chords, slept under snow and insisted that something below us was tuning the world. [173] Lev alone refused to see me. [179] The arrowhead had been sewn inside his coat, its flint warm whenever he sang. [193] I meant to use it to kill the creature I believed had hollowed him out. [208]

On my fourth night Sera led me past feverfew to a black telephone growing among mint roots. [225] It was a cordless handset from the last century, its cradle missing, its plastic split by thyme. [242] "For confessions," she said. [246] "The garden hears better through static." [252] When she left, the handset crackled and Lev's voice said, "Tell Mara the antlers were never the point." [270] Then other missing voices confessed through static, not their deaths but their choices. [283] They had followed a music in the cave because each recognized in it a discord they had carried all their lives. [304] The harmony was untamed, Sera whispered, returning with two cups of pine tea, not a song of obedience but a wild agreement among unlike things. [329] It did not abduct people. [334] It offered them a shape large enough for their loneliness, and some accepted. [347]

My warrant suddenly seemed obscene, but my anger did not. [357] "Then why send him back?" I asked. [364] Sera touched the arrowhead and every orbiting drop plunged downward. [374] "Because metamorphosis is not a cage unless someone locks the door." [385]

The floor opened without a sound. [391] Below us roots braided themselves around a darkness that inhaled winter and exhaled stars. [405] It wore Lev briefly, then a stag, then a cloud of pale moths, each form rhyming with the last. [424] My recordings could never hold it. [430] The thing plucked the arrowhead into the air and spun me gently around it while my boots floated above the thyme. [445] Despite myself I laughed, and the music stumbled. [453] In that gap Lev's voice returned to the phone. [460] "You always wanted my suffering to make sense," he said. [470] "Make something better of yours." [475]

I understood then that revenge would only tune me to the note I hated. [489] So I lifted the handset and confessed, not to the Survey but to the dark. [504] I confessed that I had loved Lev imperfectly, that I was jealous of his vast new joy and that my bitterness wanted a witness more than a weapon. [532] The static took each sentence and scattered it into a chord so absurdly beautiful that the moths burst from Lev's shadow like punctuation. [555] "There," Sera said. [558] "Your cosmic joke." [561]

I drove the flint arrowhead not into the heart of the music but into my warrant, pinning its careful lies to the soil. [584] The garden drank the ink, and small white mushrooms bloomed along my fabricated name. [598] Lev did not become my brother again. [605] He did something kinder. [609] He let me hear him metamorphose without demanding that I follow. [620]

At dawn the missing walked back into the hills, their shadows singing out of step with their feet. [638] I stayed to help Sera replant the mint. [646] Months later the Survey received a package of frost killed rosemary, a broken cordless phone and my resignation written around a flint arrowhead. [669] The only evidence I kept was a recording of static in which six voices laugh whenever I solemnly introduce myself as a cryptid researcher. [693] Sometimes the garden tugs my shoelaces upward. [700] Sometimes Lev says my name through the phone and it sounds like a door left open for spring. [718]</story>
