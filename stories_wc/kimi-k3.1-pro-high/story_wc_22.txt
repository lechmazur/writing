<story>After the reef died, the sea came to Gloom-hill like a blade laid flat against the coast. [17]
No foam broke beyond the harbor, only gray water widening until it seemed to press the village into a single street. [38]
Tomas, the gloom-hill coachman, had carried generals along that street when the battlefield was still a barley slope. [56]
Now he drove an empty carriage down to the blooming jetty at twilight, his horses mincing between flowers split by salt. [77]
Behind him, under a tarpaulin, lay his collection of dried seed pods, gathered from every garden the war had spared. [97]
Each pod rattled like a small, patient bone. [105]
He meant to let a garden reclaim the battlefield, though roots alone could not mend what cannon salt and blood had done. [127]
His mother had called the offshore stillness the submerged concord, a covenant sung between hill seeds and reef polyps. [146]
While it held, clean currents fed the reef, and reef lime fed the shore. [160]
The generals had laughed at her and chained the cove for gunboats. [172]
Tomas had not laughed. [176]
He spoke dead languages, having learned them from carriage inscriptions, condemned prisoners, and the headstones his wheels had cracked. [195]
The living found this gift ghoulish; birds did not. [205]
As he set the brake, a black tern landed on the lantern and cried three notes in Reefcourt, a tongue no human mouth had shaped for centuries. [232]
"Feed the wound before asking it to flower," Tomas translated. [242]
Another tern answered from the mast of a sunken gunboat. [252]
By understanding bird calls, he followed their directions along the jetty, where each board narrowed his choices and the vast dead water took away the rest. [278]
At the final pile they cried, "Below." [284]
Tomas knelt and saw, beneath clear water, bronze plaques bolted to the old battlefield causeway. [299]
Their letters were green with corrosion, but he read the concord aloud. [311]
It was no song and no metaphor, but terms of surrender imposed by the victorious hill: the reef must shelter the fleet forever, receiving nothing. [336]
His mother's covenant had been a conqueror's chain. [344]
The reef had died keeping it. [350]
Tomas looked back at the pods, then at the battlefield rising in torn terraces beyond the jetty. [367]
He had imagined planting over disgrace; instead, he had been asked to inherit it. [381]
The tern pecked his knuckle, impatient as a toll keeper. [391]
So Tomas spoke the oldest refusal he knew, the word used by captives before execution, and struck the plaques with his carriage hammer. [414]
On the third blow, the causeway split, releasing a brown plume that smelled of iron and old commands. [432]
The sea did not heal; he had not expected mercy that cheap. [444]
But a seam of pale reef rubble surfaced, and the birds told him where to carry it. [461]
All night he hauled baskets up the narrow path, crushed the lime between stones, and used it to fertilize the poisoned terraces with manure from his horses. [488]
He opened the pods one by one, matching their husks to calls: gorse where terns whistled wind, sea lavender where sandpipers clicked, barley where no bird sang at all. [517]
Dawn found him asleep in the carriage, hands raw, the battlefield dark with sowing. [531]
Weeks later, green threads crossed the gun pits. [540]
Months later, roots held the trenches open not as wounds, but as irrigation cuts. [554]
The reef remained white beyond the jetty, and Tomas came every twilight to read it new terms. [571]
Shelter us only if you choose; take what our gardens clean; refuse us when we forget. [587]
He spoke in Reefcourt, badly, and the terns corrected his grammar. [598]
One evening, as flowers closed along the jetty and the horizon narrowed to a violet seam, something answered below the water. [619]
It was not words, but a clicking deep enough to stir the pods still waiting in his pockets. [637]
Tomas did not call it forgiveness. [643]
He emptied the last seeds into his palm and listened, a small figure between the immense sea and the immense hill, learning where to begin again. [669]</story>
