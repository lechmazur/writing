<story>Prince Elian arrived at the aviary bedraggled, his coronation mantle snagged into ropes and one boot ringing against the suspended palace's iron floor. [23] Below him, the palace hung from chains anchored to giant stalactites, and every chain was shivering. [39] Farther below, the mechanical world turned its brass valleys with perfect, pitiless punctuality. [52] Nothing there bloomed, varied, or sang, because the Genesis Engine had assembled life from measurements and omitted whatever it could not measure. [74] The court called that omission efficiency; Elian, hypothesizing about everything since childhood, suspected it was the wound in their flawed genesis. [95] If a star was a furnace, if law was frozen fear, if beauty was only unused motion, then perhaps a machine could learn it. [119] His tutors had whipped such questions out of him, but the questions had merely learned to whisper. [136] He had come chasing a rumor of the egg, hoping to reawaken beauty in a mechanical world, though he expected a relic, not a heartbeat. [161] Now the aviary door opened on a nest of clock hands, and in it lay an extinct bird's egg, blue beneath its dust. [184] Elian stopped breathing. [187] The last dawnlark had died three centuries ago, yet something inside the shell knocked, hesitated, and knocked again. [205] The discovery should have been triumph, but the palace quaked, dust streaming upward like reversed rain. [221] A hairline crack whitened the egg. [227] He hypothesized cold, age, sabotage, then rejected every theory except the unbearable one: the aviary's preserving engine was failing. [246] Its repair required the royal regulator, which also kept the palace's anchors synchronized. [259] Removing it might hatch the bird or drop a thousand sleeping courtiers into the gears below. [275] Elian imagined saving them and losing the egg, saving the egg and becoming a murderer, doing nothing and calling caution holy. [296] Then he noticed the chains did not quake together; each shudder followed the aviary's metronome by a different interval. [315] Perhaps the anchors were not weakening but listening. [323] Perhaps the palace, built to imprison wonder, had been waiting for an irregular pulse. [337] It was a foolish hypothesis, so he tested it. [346] He began to assemble a cradle from the nest's clock hands, his mantle's gold thread, and the regulator's warm copper heart. [367] He did not take the regulator; he taught it to beat out of time, using the egg's faint knocking as a counterrhythm. [389] His torn sleeve became insulation; his crown, bent open, held three tiny bells. [402] When the mechanism demanded a temperature, he gave it his hands; when it demanded a song, he gave it his mother's name. [424] He remembered little else about her except that she had called machines honest because they broke where they were weak. [444] The cradle shuddered; the anchors answered; the whole palace seemed to inhale around him. [458] For one terrible instant, Elian felt the cavern itself attending, vast and dark and tender. [473] Then the egg opened. [477] No radiant creature emerged, only a wet, gray thing with a crooked wing and a beak too large for its face. [498] The moment was bitterly sweet: the last dawnlark lived, but its crooked wing meant it might never cross the cavern as its ancestors had. [522] Elian loved it immediately, and that immediacy frightened him more than the falling dust. [536] Had he wanted beauty, or merely a beautiful excuse to disobey? [547] The chick answered neither question; it simply struck his bent crown with its beak. [561] One bell rang flat. [565] The regulator caught the note, wobbled, and passed it to the chains. [577] Stalactite to stalactite, the anchors sounded, not in unison but in aching conversation. [590] Below, factory whistles faltered, loom teeth missed their marks, and a thousand brass valves opened onto silence. [607] Into that silence the chick made a small, cracked noise. [617] The mechanical world did not become gardens at once. [626] A gear kept turning; a worker laughed at the wrong moment; somewhere, a precise clock chose to be late. [645] Elian understood then that genesis had not failed because beauty was left out. [658] It had failed because its makers believed anything left out must remain so. [671] He wrapped the chick in his ruined mantle and carried it toward the waking court, no longer certain a palace needed perfection to remain aloft. [696] Above him, the great stone teeth trembled; beneath him, unmeasured noises multiplied; within his hands, a crooked heart knocked back. [716] Elian walked on, quaking with reverie, while the discovery continued. [726]</story>
