In the nebula garden, petals of condensed starlight fell without sound, each one holding a dead traveler's last pleasant memory. [20]
Neri, an otherworldly sentimentalist, caught them in glass sleeves and labeled them, though she had never known the travelers. [39]
Her grandmother had called the collection ballast; her mother had called it a burden; Neri called it company. [57]
From both women she inherited a calculating machine gear, brass gone violet, its inner rim crowded with their tiny repairs. [77]
The gear governed the reckoner that guided seed-ships through the garden's transit, when folded space made every star a lie. [97]
It divided each error by half, then half again, approximating infinity without ever granting the comfort of a final answer. [117]
For three generations, when the reckoner's numbers blurred, Neri's family had burned the petal beds to make a beacon. [136]
The ships survived, and the garden regrew, serene as a smile hiding a toothache. [150]
Now another transit approached, and Neri's daughter, Ila, was already polishing the copper firehooks. [164]
Watching her repeat the old tender gestures, Neri understood that generational patterns survived by dressing themselves as love. [182]
She would not leave Ila a cleaner inheritance of ash. [192]
Her purpose was simple and nearly impossible: to share light without using fire, and to keep every borrowed memory intact. [212]
She remembered that whales had once sung through black oceans, and proposed sonar pinging through the nebula's crystal roots. [231]
If echoes could locate the roots, their resonance might wake the petals, releasing stored radiance without consuming them. [249]
Ila looked from the firehooks to her mother's glass sleeves. [259]
"You want to save strangers because you cannot bear losing anything," she said, "and you call that hope." [277]
The accusation hurt because it was almost true. [285]
Neri had preserved so many endings that she had mistaken preservation for devotion. [298]
Still, she set the gear into the reckoner and carried it aboard their root-skiff, with Ila beside her and no firehooks. [319]
Across the quiet sigh of falling petals, they entered the folding dark. [331]
Neri pinged; the roots returned a silver shudder, and the gear advanced one patient tooth. [346]
Again and again she sounded the depths while Ila marked each answer, until their map resembled a family tree turned sideways. [367]
Then the first seed-ship called, already inside the transit, its crew whispering coordinates for suns that did not exist. [386]
The reckoner halved their error, and halved it, but the garden kept opening new distances. [401]
Neri's hands shook toward the emergency igniter. [408]
Ila closed her fingers around it, not to take it away, but to hold on. [423]
"Mother burned alone, didn't she?" Ila asked. [430]
"And Grandmother before her, and every keeper who thought love meant sparing us the fear." [445]
Neri heard the old pattern beneath the words: not fire, but solitude. [457]
She gave Ila the reckoner and took the pings herself, dividing the labor as the gear divided error. [475]
Mother and daughter traded readings, corrections, and fear, until the replies formed a chord no solitary keeper could have found. [495]

One by one, crystal trunks struck answering notes, and the petals opened. [514]

The seed-ships followed that radiance home, while not a single memory browned at the edges. [549]
Yet the garden did not become merely beautiful. [557]

Memories lifted like pollen, some vanishing, some settling into Ila's outstretched hands. [617]
Neri grieved them, and let them go. [624]
She kept only the gear, not as a command to repeat, but as proof that an endless approach could still carry two people somewhere new. [649]
Above them, the renewed garden resumed its peaceful falling, lovely and troubling, and this time neither woman mistook its silence for consent. [671]
