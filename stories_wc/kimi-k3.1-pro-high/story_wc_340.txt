<story>On the first floor, Pell entered the elevator with a dead woman's diary in his arms and orders to burn it. [21] Above the clouds, the icebound spire trembled in the wind, and its ten landings shone like blue teeth. [39] Pell was a servant ascending to the furnace, though his softly tilted head suggested a man attending an interesting funeral. [59] He had that warmly inquisitive manner which made even sorrow feel obliged to explain itself. [74] Beside the diary lay Captain Ilyra's airship goggles, their cracked lenses wrapped in her scarf. [89] At the tenth floor, Keeper Vey waited to erase the last record of her daughter. [104] Ilyra had died steering her burning airship into a storm breach, saving the spire and spoiling, rather thoroughly, her mother's talent for peaceful sleep. [128] As the elevator climbed past two, Pell opened the diary and found no grief he could recognize. [145] Every page held ranks of numbers, tiny suns, and absurd little drawings of spoons. [159] The discovery of an encrypted diary would have excited him more if discovering them were not so often followed by executions. [180] Still, Ilyra had once spent an hour teaching him why soup steamed, so he owed her curiosity a decent attempt. [200] At three, sunlight entered the cage, struck the goggles, and broke into scattered light across the open pages. [218] Where brightness touched a sun, its numbers seemed to recede, leaving letters like footprints after snow. [234] Pell laughed once, then looked apologetically at the dead woman's belongings. [245] He set the goggles before the diary and let the rising elevator do the restless work. [261] Through scattered light, Pell watched the first entry unfold: Today I was afraid, and the clouds were beautiful anyway. [280] At four, he read of engines screaming, of a navigator weeping, and of Ilyra trading her dinner for a song. [300] The song had been bad; the navigator had laughed until she could fly. [313] Pell had always suspected that courage was merely fear with excellent timing. [325] At five, the code demanded a spoon drawn beside the moon, and he turned Ilyra's scarf to find the same sign stitched inside. [348] Servants notice such things, largely because nobody expects them to. [358] The next page unfolded in light: Mother believes joy is a room one enters only after suffering has paid the rent. [379] Pell considered this an unreasonable landlord. [385] At six, the entries grew shorter, describing frost on rigging, dwindling fuel, and a city below that had stopped answering. [405] Beneath each account of anguish, Ilyra had hidden one small, stubborn pleasure. [417] Tea shared with a mechanic. [422] A child's paper boat carried aboard. [428] Pell polishing the captain's boots so badly that she had worn them to a formal dinner. [444] He paused there, offended and tenderly pleased. [451] At seven, he understood his own errand less as obedience than as a question. [465] Could he seek out the joy beneath anguish without making the anguish smaller? [478] It seemed a suitably impossible thing to attempt before breakfast. [488] At eight, the final page refused the light until Pell wiped frost from the goggles with his sleeve. [506] The lenses cleared, and scattered radiance trembled over Ilyra's last hand. [517] If Mother reads this, tell her I did not choose death; I chose every face below me. [534] Tell Pell his boots were dreadful and his company was not. [545] I was afraid, and I was glad. [552] At nine, Pell closed the diary, because some sentences deserve a little privacy before being handed to history. [570] The elevator opened at ten on Keeper Vey, the furnace, and a morning so white it seemed freshly invented. [589] "Give it to me," Vey said. [595] Pell gave her the goggles instead. [601] "Read with these," he said, "and begin wherever the light is kindest." [613] Vey looked at the servant, then at the pages, with the wary outrage of a woman whose grief had been contradicted. [634] Behind her, the furnace sighed, disappointed but patient. [642] She sat on its cold step and held the diary beneath the fractured beams. [656] Pell waited while the letters opened, while her mouth tightened, and while, at last, one laugh escaped her like a bird that had forgotten the window. [682] It was not happiness, exactly. [687] It was something better suited to the living: a door. [697] Pell, who knew doors, quietly held this one open. [706]</story>
