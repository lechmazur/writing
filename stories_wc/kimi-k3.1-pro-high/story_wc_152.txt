<story>Autumn turned the cobalt observatory cold before it turned the valley gold, and Mira, the galaxy-chart embroiderer, sat beneath its dome working for astronomers who seldom looked at her. [29]
She dropped spools, misnamed constellations, and asked whether stars needed buttons, a foolishness so carefully practiced that learned men discussed treason beside her without lowering their voices. [56]
Her wisdom was to listen for unheard stories: the washerwoman's missing son, the clerk whose ledger had been rewritten, the night guard ordered to forget a locked door. [84]
She stitched those absences into black thread between the stars, where official charts showed only empty sky. [101]
Three days before the coup, though Mira did not yet know to call it that, Director Voss ordered a new chart for the Founder's Feast. [126]
It must synchronize every provincial telescope, he said, so the kingdom could witness one miraculous instant through adjacent possibilities. [145]
Mira giggled and asked if miracles required soldiers on the stairs. [156]
Voss smiled, and the guard's hand tightened on his spear. [166]
That night she carried her hoop to the observatory's heart, where an eternal candle burned inside a glass star. [185]
Its flame had been inherited from the first observatory, and because it burned identically in every neighboring world, the listening engine used it to distinguish real echoes from imagined ones. [215]
She passed her needle through a flame from the eternal candle, as custom required, then laid her chart across the brass listening bowl. [238]
At midnight, the dome opened onto skies that were almost hers. [249]
In one, the washerwoman's son was already dead; in another, he testified before a terrified parliament; in a third, bells drowned his words. [272]
Each possibility ended with Voss crowned beneath the same white banner. [283]
The synchronization was not for telescopes but for signals: one flare would loose arrests in every province before loyal officers could learn whether their neighbors had obeyed. [310]
A chart in each tower would choose the adjacent instant in which its signal arrived, making rebellion appear simultaneous and inevitable. [331]
Mira understood why the missing clerk had rewritten his ledger and why the guard feared the locked door. [349]
Behind it waited the foreign rifles whose purchase the ledger concealed. [360]
She could burn the chart, but Voss had copies, and a dead embroiderer would explain nothing. [376]
So she played the fool at breakfast, spilled wine on Voss's sleeve, and stole his tuning key while apologizing to his cuff. [398]
All day she added stitches that looked like mistakes. [407]
Each black knot carried a name, each name a voice the engine had found in some neighboring dark. [425]
When the feast bells began, Voss gathered officers beneath the dome and lit the signal lamp from the eternal candle. [445]
Mira bent over her hoop, humming a nursery song, and turned the stolen key with her needle. [462]
"Shall I synchronize the stars now, or are they busy?" [472]
The officers laughed. [475]
Voss said, "Now," and the flame bowed. [482]
Mira did not synchronize the towers with their strongest possibilities, where soldiers moved together and resistance failed. [499]
She synchronized them with the narrow, neighboring instants in which each listener was alone. [513]
Into that solitude her stitches released what the engine had heard: the clerk reading the true ledger, the guard naming the rifle room, the washerwoman asking why her son had been silenced, and Voss himself promising amnesty to assassins. [552]
No general could pretend the signal was a clerical error, for every tower heard the same evidence in a different, unguarded voice. [574]
Then came the echoed silence Mira had sewn toward all her life, not emptiness, but the pause in which powerful men finally heard those they had erased. [601]
In that pause, the night guard opened the rifle room to loyal officers, the clerk unlocked parliament, and provincial commanders arrested Voss's agents one by one. [627]
The coup ended before its first order could be obeyed. [637]
Voss reached for Mira's needle, but the washerwoman, serving wine, caught his wrist. [650]
No one laughed when Mira called him a loose thread. [660]
Later, under the cobalt dome, she unpicked the white banner from her galaxy and used its thread to embroider doors. [680]
Beyond them she stitched no thrones, only listeners, and around every name she left a little darkness where another story might speak. [702]</story>
