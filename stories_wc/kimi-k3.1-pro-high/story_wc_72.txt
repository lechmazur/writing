<story>After Ila revealed the secret of Vael to the surface, she returned to the drowned city before the salvage ships could arrive. [22] She had expected an empty, forgotten ruin, not a garden where anemones burned like lanterns beneath a cracked glass dome. [42] At its center stood a statue of her mother, one stone hand cupped around a foxglove-stained glass ampoule. [60] You were always incisively kind, Ila thought, and cruel only in the archives. [73] The statue's eyes opened, and a voice entered her mind with the gentleness of a blade cleaning a wound. [92] "You came to break me," it said. [99] "You told them where to drill," Ila answered. [107] "To prove you drowned Vael, I had to show them Vael was real." [120] Above them, the first drill struck; silver fractures veined the dome. [131] "Then break my certainty first," the statue said, opening its hand. [142] Inside the ampoule, purple liquid moved against the water's pressure. [152] The statue touched Ila's helmet, and memory replaced thought: raiders above Vael, fire on the docks, her mother opening the sea gates. [174] The city had drowned by her hand, exactly as Ila had sworn in court. [188] Then the memory deepened, revealing citizens drinking foxglove tonic and lying down in garden beds as stone closed softly over them. [209] Drowning had slowed their hearts; stone had hidden their breathing. [219] Her mother had not buried Vael but made it appear dead until the raiders departed. [234] Each revelation broke the floor beneath the last: her mother was the statue, the statues were the city, and the ampoule held the measured poison that could return every heart to its proper rhythm. [268] "Why let me hate you?" [273] "Because your hatred kept you searching," her mother thought, "and because I was afraid that waking them would only restore the past." [295] Another impact tore a seam through the dome. [303] Ila raised her hammer toward the statue, then saw what her certainty had hidden: the plinth's oldest fracture continued into the garden's sealed irrigation spine. [328] Through that structural weakness, one dose could reach every root. [338] She did not shatter the stone. [344] Instead, she took the copper wire from her helmet lamp and used it to thread the ampoule's slender neck through the fissure, guiding it downward until glass kissed the dry channel below. [376] The statue's incisive kindness found her again. [383] "You wanted one clean verdict," her mother said. [391] "Let them wake to more than my version." [399] Ila struck the plinth once, precisely along its flaw. [408] The ampoule broke, and purple fire ran threading through the roots. [419] Life entered what had seemed lifeless without asking its permission. [429] Moss uncurled, stone fingers loosened, and white flowers opened in the silt with small, decisive gasps. [445] Across the garden, sleepers sat up beneath their crumbling skins, while living coral climbed the dome and knitted its fractures into panes. [467] The salvage drill stopped, caught in a sudden braid of green. [478] The first awakened citizen did not embrace Ila's mother. [487] "You chose for us," she said. [493] The living statue bowed, her certainty cracking more painfully than stone. [504] "Yes," she answered aloud, and her new voice was rough with use. [516] Ila waited for absolution, but her mother only took her hand. [527] Forgiveness, Ila understood, would be another door she had no right to force. [540] By dawn, the salvagers had withdrawn, broadcasting images of a city that no weapon could now dismiss as dead. [559] Vael's council debated opening its gates, keeping them closed, or offering the surface gardens instead of gold. [576] Ila listened without reaching for a single answer. [584] She had revealed Vael to condemn one woman; now its revelation continued in every difficult voice. [600] In the brightening garden, her mother tended a flower whose petals were both remedy and poison, and made room for Ila beside her. [623]</story>
