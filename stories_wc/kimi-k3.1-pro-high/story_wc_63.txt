<story>At low tide, the obsidian ridge shone like a blade cooled in blood, and Oren climbed it with the tower key warm against his tongue. [25] He was the local clock tower winder, precise enough to divide a minute by the ache in his wrist, but never precise enough to forget. [50] Below, black channels drank the returning sea, and the village tide gates waited for the pendulum to tell them when to open. [72] For eleven years, Oren had kept that command steady, balancing field against flood, boat against garden, hunger against hunger. [91] Yet balance had once been his excuse. [98] During the red gale, he had stolen the clock tower pendulum bob and weighed down his own eel skiff, meaning to save one catch for his fevered daughter. [126] Without the bob, the gates closed early, trapping three neighbors beyond the ridge while his child lived. [143] He returned the weight before dawn and called the deaths a fault of weather. [157] The lie ran deeper than any root in that glassy stone. [168] His daughter, Sera, grew into the woman who found him oiling the escapement with funeral care. [184] She carried a broken rake handle, its splinters gray from the salt gardens she tended for the widows. [202] Repair this, she said, or stop pretending your hands are clean. [213] The rake could not be made whole; its head had fed the winter fire. [227] So Oren, ethically diligent at last in the smallest available matter, smoothed the handle and carved one truthful line near its break. [249] I took the bob. [253] The next dusk he added, Mara held the gate rope while I lied. [266] Each tiny inscription named a harm, then a repair he had actually made: a roof rebuilt, a debt returned, a channel cleared beside those who cursed him. [293] No carving said forgiven. [297] Absolution came incrementally, if it came at all, like light honing the ridge one grain of ash at a time. [317] He used the handle as a winding crank extension, so every confession passed through his palm whenever he raised the weights. [338] On the eleventh anniversary of the gale, heat made the whole tower tremble with remembered voices. [354] Oren climbed past the bell, seeing not dust but salt, not gears but three hands slipping from wet rope. [373] The sea flashed white beneath him, a furnace without smoke. [383] He opened the pendulum case and found the bob split along its old lead seam. [398] If it swung again, the damaged weight would tear free and the gates would lock before the spring tide. [417] He could bind it secretly, as he had once restored it secretly, and preserve the appearance of balance. [435] Instead, he stopped the escapement and carried bob and handle down to the waiting village. [450] In that pause in the pendulum's swing, no hidden mechanism ruled the shore; every face turned toward him, bright and merciless. [471] Oren seemed to emerge from the man he had been, not cleansed, but visible. [485] He read each inscription aloud, then gave the widows the split bob and the tower key. [501] I cannot return the dead, he said, but today you will decide what my precision is for. [518] Sera stepped forward, not to pardon him, but to take his shaking hand. [531] Together they fitted the bob with a copper sleeve forged from the rake's surviving ferrule, while smith, mason, and fishermen argued over every measure. [555] Nothing was left for one man's immaculate discretion if they meant to restore shared balance. [570] Before restarting the clock, Oren carved a final space, not words, along the crowded handle. [585] It was room for tomorrow's error and tomorrow's answer. [594] Then Sera released the pendulum, and the ridge answered with a slow, common beat. [608] The gates opened; water crossed the black teeth without devouring them. [619] Oren wound only as far as the village instructed, his blistered fingers burning around the little histories. [636] He felt no blaze of grace, only the searing reverie of a coast held, breath by breath, in shared balance. [656]</story>
