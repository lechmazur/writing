<story>After the falcon returned, the frozen lake became so quiet that Mara Vell could hear the outpost rusting. [18] It was a small sound, a patient tick in the walls, and therefore, she decided, the only employee here still doing honest work. [41] Mara was an atlas editor by trade, dispatched to the rusted outpost because three governments had commissioned one map and each expected a different river to win. [68] Her own ambition was simpler and less respectable: to find where the rivers begin. [82] For eleven winters, headwaters had migrated across official plates according to whoever held the engraver's pension, while Mara corrected margins with a subtly confident hand and waited for evidence. [111] The evidence arrived with snow on its talons and a brass capsule wired beneath one wing. [127] Inside lay a lens from the survey station across the lake, where old Anik had gone to measure springs and left behind only a polyglot's phrasebook. [153] Mara set the lens beside the book on the sill. [163] Its dog-ear marked not a useful greeting but a dialect note in Anik's hand: SOURCE IS A WORD FOR PEOPLE WHO HAVE NEVER LOOKED UPSTREAM. [188] Below it he had translated the local name for the lake as THE PLACE THAT HOLDS ITS BREATH. [206] Mara looked through the window. [211] No pressure ridges crossed the ice; no snow devils moved; even the falcon stood with its bell silenced against her glove. [232] The tranquility should have been beautiful, but the lake had been groaning all week, and silence suggested attention rather than peace. [253] She fitted Anik's lens into the empty window latch, where its rim caught the rising sun and laid a pale oval on the glass. [277] At once, condensation bloomed inside the oval, not evenly, but in fine silver branches. [291] Mara almost wiped them away, then remembered the dog-eared warning and let the pattern of condensation on glass finish drawing itself. [312] Seven branches entered from seven valleys, divided, joined, and narrowed to one black point beneath the lake. [329] The lines matched neither the military surveys nor Anik's beloved theories, which would have pleased him had he not recently become a cautionary footnote. [353] Mara brought her nose close to the glass. [361] The point was not an absence of moisture but a circle kept clear by warmth. [376] Somewhere below, one spring was breathing through the ice. [385] She understood then why every search for separate sources had failed: the rivers shared a singular convergence before they ever learned their names. [408] It was not a meeting of waters, as the old geographic term implied, but a single heart forcing meltwater upward until the valleys divided it. [433] The atlas would need one blue dot, three embarrassed prefaces, and an apology to several dead men. [450] Then the clear circle widened. [455] A thread of water slid down the outer pane, though the air remained far below freezing. [471] Anik's translations acquired a less poetic meaning: the lake was holding back a flood, and the warm lens had shown Mara exactly where its breath would break. [498] Beyond the ice, the downriver towns were still asleep inside their disputed borders. [511] Mara could wait for a second observation, as method demanded, or she could trust the first honest map the region had produced in eleven years. [536] She chose with the calm of a woman moving a comma, but faster. [549] On the outpost roof, she opened the signal shutter and used the lens to herald the danger, flashing the old survey code toward every watchtower: RISE, RISE, RISE. [577] Then she tied the same warning to the falcon, adding the phrasebook because Anik's translations might persuade those who distrusted numbers. [598] The bird launched, and far across the lake a tower answered with three white sparks. [613] Only then did Mara pack her plates, ink, and pride into the survey sledge. [627] Behind her, the lake remained smooth, the outpost kept ticking, and the morning wore the worrying tranquility of a held door. [648] At the first crack, she pulled away without running; panic, like headwaters, was frequently a matter of poor editing. [667] She followed the falcon's shadow uphill, already composing the map's small, devastating caption: ALL RIVERS BEGIN HERE; PLEASE GO HIGHER. [687]</story>
