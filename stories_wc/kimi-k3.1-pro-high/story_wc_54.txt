<story>When the carnival left town, it took the music, the last working tractors, and most of the people who still believed departure was a kind of weather. [27] Mara Venn remained because she was a curious civil engineer intern, and because the spaceport's abandoned passenger terminal still owed her one structural survey. [51] Her supervisor had ordered measurements for demolition; Mara secretly hoped to puzzle out new equations for hope before the town's reservoir went dry. [74] Inside, glitter drifted through the arrivals hall and adhered to departure boards naming destinations no ship had served for nineteen years. [95] The terminal smelled of rain on dust, although the roof had not admitted weather since its glass was sealed with salvage panels. [117] Mara heard water ticking beneath a baggage carousel, steady as a pulse refusing diagnosis. [131] Instead of marking cracked columns, she lifted a service hatch and found a bronze arc caught in the grille. [150] It was an explorer's sextant piece, its degrees worn smooth except for one scratched instruction: begin where the water changes its mind. [172] The words matched a notation on the terminal's oldest drainage plan, which Mara had once dismissed as poetry from a less practical age. [195] Below her lay three cisterns, five culverts, and a pumping gallery crossed by pipes painted in rival colors. [213] Each renovation had redirected passengers or water toward somebody's profit: customs queues, concession halls, launch cooling, then the carnival's brief thirsty machines. [235] It was a maze of intentions made tangible, yet its original route remained visible beneath the later welds. [253] Roof catchments had once filled the cisterns; overflow had run through reed beds and onward to the public reservoir. [272] The town had not lost its water supply; it had been taught to forget the path. [288] Mara descended with a lamp, a wrench, and the sextant arc, feeling fear become exact measurements. [304] A storm was approaching, but the main sluice wheel had vanished and its actuator lacked the toothed quadrant that set three gates in sequence. [328] Replacement would take weeks; reverse flow through a wrong gate would split the reservoir main. [343] Mara almost returned to her sensible survey. [350] Then her lamp caught carnival tickets plastered over a municipal warning, and anger gave her caution a useful shape. [369] Being functionally creative, she stopped asking what the mechanism had lost and asked what motion it still required. [387] The bronze arc's surviving teeth matched the actuator drive; its worn graduations could meter each gate, while hose clamps and carousel chain supplied leverage. [411] She was not restoring a sacred instrument, only allowing one careful traveler to help another find direction. [428] For six hours she cleaned screens, opened air valves, and tied the arc into place, checking friction with her bare palm. [449] When thunder shook the terminal, she turned the first gate ten degrees and sent roof water through the old runway drains. [470] Sediment boiled out black, carrying ticket stubs, bright feathers, and a little wooden star from the carousel. [487] At thirty degrees, the second gate filled the reed beds; at fifty, a buried siphon coughed and drew the cisterns toward town. [509] Then the chain slipped. [513] Pressure kicked the wrench aside, and water hammer walked toward Mara like something enormous waking in the walls. [531] She could close everything and keep the terminal safe for demolition, or hold the arc at sixty degrees until the siphon stabilized. [553] She held it, counting seconds as pipe loads traveled through bolts, concrete, and her aching hands. [569] The hammer faded; the redirected flows found their levels, and clear water moved beneath the floor with no audience at all. [590] By dawn, gauges at the municipal reservoir were rising. [599] Mara had not resurrected the spaceport, whose ships belonged to other people's futures, but its patient watershed. [616] Her supervisor arrived with demolition drawings, read her calculations twice, and silently began labeling the gates for preservation. [634] Together they watched reeds bow under the current, two professionals conceding that wonder could be a load a structure was meant to carry. [657] Mara kept the sextant arc in the mechanism, because hope, she now understood, was not the certainty of arrival. [676] It was the discipline of giving every falling thing somewhere useful to go.
