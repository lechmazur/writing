<story>At twilight, Nera Vey poled her salvage barge into the canal district where the city kept its drowned beginnings. [19]
The guild called her an errant legend seeker, a courteous name for someone who followed beautiful stories into ugly water. [39]
Tonight she sought the Mercy, the grain barge of Saint Sera, whose legendary voyage had supposedly saved the city from famine. [60]
Nera's father had designed the famine barriers, then confessed to falsifying their gates and drowning the eastern wards. [78]
His confession had destroyed her family, but its polished perfection had made her suspect another lie beneath it. [96]
She had come not to clear his name, but to understand the architecture of lies: what carried their weight, and what they sheltered. [119]
Her only inheritance was an antique pocket sundial, its brass lid rubbed bright where his thumb had rested. [137]
He had taught her that a shadow was evidence, never instruction; it had to be read against place, season, and the hand that made it. [162]
The canal offered no instruction at all. [169]
Willows trailed in copper water, roofless warehouses held the last sky, and the only sound was her pole entering and leaving the silence. [192]
It was a serenity with weight, as though the district were calmly turning over. [206]
A chart marked the Mercy's wreck below the old customs arch, but three arches stood there, their reflections making six. [226]
Nera almost chose the central one, where legend placed the saint's final blessing. [239]
Instead she opened the sundial and remembered, with hindsight clarity that seemed almost cruel, her childhood self beside her father, correcting its latitude while he watched the canal rather than the sun. [271]
He had not been teaching time; he had been teaching her to notice deliberately misplaced things. [287]
The dial's gnomon was fixed at a false angle, exactly the angle of the western arch's leaning crack. [305]
She sighted along it and found the northern arch framed by the compass ring. [319]
There she moored, dressed in her canvas diving suit, and listened to the radio operator recite river closures. [337]
The morning broadcast would end at dawn, when the drainage engines started and their current made diving impossible. [355]
She had until the silence of morning radio to salvage whatever remained. [367]
Underwater, the arch became a cold mouth and the rope her only thought. [380]
When her lamp failed, panic offered its bright, useless stories: a hand below, her father calling, the surface gone. [399]
She survived by letting training steer her instincts: slacken the rope before turning, read the current through her shoulders, follow the hose's faint pull home. [424]
On her third descent, she found no gilded shrine, only a grain barge crushed beneath a customs launch. [442]
Its hold contained a copper strongbox, stamped with her father's seal and chained to the saint's empty pedestal. [460]
She rigged pontoons, freed the chains, and watched the box rise through black water like a slow, deliberate star. [479]
Back aboard, she used the sundial's edge to break its corroded lock. [491]
Inside lay the barriers' true gate schedule, grain receipts for the eastern wards, and a single page in her father's hand. [512]
The gates had not failed; her father had opened them, diverting the officials' hoarded grain toward the Mercy before the current broke loose and flooded the wards. [539]
The officials invented Saint Sera to conceal their hoarding, while his confession concealed the families who had distributed the rescued grain. [560]
Thus her mercy was built from real bread, real rescues, and one roof broad enough to shelter thieves and feeders alike. [581]
The page ended, "A lie is not a wall against truth; it is a roof, and you must see whom it covers." [603]
She read the page twice, her breathing slow, her conclusions thoughtfully clear: her father was neither innocent nor simply guilty. [623]
She could salvage his complexity without restoring his halo. [632]
When the radio thinned to static and then silence, dawn silvered the canal. [645]
The engines shuddered awake; far off, water lifted against stone, yet her barge barely rocked. [660]
Nera placed her father's page beside the sundial, both open to the light. [673]
She would take the strongbox to the eastern wards first, where survivors could decide what should be toppled and what remembered. [694]
Then she pushed from the arch, and the canal rearranged every reflection without a sound. [709]</story>
