<story>Mara expected the worst and climbed anyway, which was why the wardens called her an optimist. [16] The staircase beneath her boots had no first step, no last, and no landings except those the climbers agreed to imagine. [37] It turned through a lightless tower, perpetual motion carrying each tread down behind her while the next rose to meet her foot. [59] Nothing powered it but climbing, and nothing made climbing necessary but the staircase. [72] At dawn, if dawn meant the bells, Mara reached the proof station and found Councilor Vey waiting with her old dice. [93] One was smooth ivory; the other had a chipped corner, a little wound that made six rare and one common. [113] "Certify the final lemma," Vey said, "and the tower will stop wasting calculators on escape." [128] Mara had spent nineteen years proving that every route upward returned to this station. [142] She had also copied, into her private book, the pressure of every tread, the taste of rust, and the exact drag of a sleeve withdrawn from a hand. [170] That last entry concerned Orren, who had found a door where the staircase briefly touched another world. [187] There had been room for one, and they had rolled the dice for it. [201] Mara won, then noticed the chip and insisted on a fair throw. [213] While she calculated the bias, the door closed, taking Orren's chance and hers with it. [228] "You still keep them," Vey said. [234] "They are the only irregularity in the tower," Mara replied. [244] Vey smiled, believing she meant sorrow. [250] She meant mass. [253] For weeks she had balanced the dice on a proof slate, measuring their falls against the staircase's rhythm. [271] The smooth die repeated its arc; the chipped one never did. [282] From that difference she derived a contradiction in Vey's lemma: a closed system could repeat perfectly only if nothing within it preferred one outcome to another. [308] Yet every climber preferred an ending, and preference, however useless, altered the timing of a footfall. [324] The proof did not promise escape. [330] It promised only a rupture, narrow as a chipped corner, when one step would receive more force than the tower could return. [352] To reach it, Mara had to climb against the motion for nine thousand strides, then throw herself sideways at the precise beat when the next tread withdrew. [379] Cowardice would keep her on the stairs; recklessness would tear her apart before the proof could bite. [396] Her only chance was a balancedly daring act, each risk matched to a number she trusted. [412] Vey read her annotations and went gray. [419] "A rupture would not open Orren's door," he said. [428] "No," Mara said. [431] "Then what do you expect to gain?" [438] "The texture of the chance I missed," she said. [447] "Not the door, not Orren, not forgiveness. [454] I want to know whether possibility feels like stone giving way, or only like another calculation." [470] She rolled the dice across the slate. [477] The smooth one showed six; the chipped one stopped on one, and Mara began to climb. [493] Vey did not call the guards. [499] Perhaps he saw that imprisonment by proof required belief, and his had already cracked. [513] For nine thousand strides the tower tightened around her. [522] Her calves burned, her breath became a second bell, and the dark above her unfolded without end. [539] She thought of Orren's sleeve, rough wool slipping over her knuckles, and did not mistake remembering for return. [557] At the final beat she set both dice on the rising tread. [569] The smooth die jumped in time; the chipped one landed late. [580] That lateness was her proof made visible. [587] Mara stepped sideways, neither fleeing nor surrendering, and drove her heel down. [599] The staircase broke. [602] Not shattered: broke, like a sentence interrupted before its obedient end. [613] A seam opened through the tower, and beyond it lay no landscape, only more stairs, motionless under a sky of unbounded white. [635] Mara laughed once, grimly, because freedom might simply be a larger room. [647] Then she crossed the rupture, carrying neither die, and pressed her palm to the first still step. [664] It was cold, gritty, and imperfectly chipped at the edge. [674] She leaned down, touched the flaw with her tongue, and tasted iron. [686] Behind her, the bells demanded that she return; ahead, the silent steps offered no promise at all. [703] Mara chose the silence, not because it was better, but because, at last, its texture was unknown. [720]</story>
