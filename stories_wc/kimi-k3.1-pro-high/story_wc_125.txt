After the energy beings arrived, starlight gathered in the ditches like spilled milk, and Vesa could sometimes hear tomorrow humming beneath the wheels of passing carts. [26]
She was a drifting roadside poet, trading verses for bread and slyly rhyming magistrates with asses, an ethically playful habit that made cruelty look ridiculous without making its victims the joke. [57]
In her pack lay a frayed coil of rope from a prison escape, the only inheritance she had kept. [76]
Years earlier, during a flood, her brother Niko had held that rope while she climbed, then cut himself loose to draw the guards away. [100]
She had been searching ever since for the texture of time, certain that if she could touch its grain, she would find the place where his ending had gone wrong. [130]
A being of blue fire, calling itself Lumen, led her from the road to a terra-cotta grotto under starlight. [149]
Its light pressed against her skin like warm linen, and the walls breathed with buried colors. [165]
Behind it, the valley hung in one arrested instant, awaiting the choice that would let it move. [182]
"You want to feel time," Lumen said, "so uncover it, but know that every pattern closes one door as it opens another." [204]
Vesa set the rope on the floor and began rubbing the clay with its frayed end. [220]
By uncovering layers of red, ochre, and black, she found not pictures but moments: her small hand in Niko's, his laugh in the prison dark, the rope burning her palms. [250]
She arranged them around the coil in the pattern of a winding road, because a road, unlike a grave, always invited one more step. [274]
The grotto opened two doors of light. [281]
Through one, she saw herself return to the flood, take Niko's place, and die while he escaped; through the other, she saw herself leave the grotto unchanged, carrying his death but also every life her poems might still persuade to gentleness. [322]
"Niko would live in the first world," she whispered, "and I would never have written the bread strike ballad." [341]
Lumen's radiance dimmed. [344]
"In the first world he becomes a jailer and chains forty debtors; in the second your ballad frees them, though he remains beyond your reach." [369]
The choice offered no clean innocence: her brother alive and hardened, or her brother dead and his gift flowering in strangers. [390]
Vesa hated Lumen then, with a passion so quiet it felt like clay cooling around her heart. [407]
She could refuse both doors, but refusal would leave the grotto sealing the valley's time forever, preserving children beside the river and prisoners in their cells in one unending amber instant. [438]
She thought of bribing fate with a poem, some nimble couplet that would make the doors laugh and swing together, but Niko had not saved her so that she could make a cleverness of love. [473]
Weeping, she pushed the rope through the second door. [482]
The first went dark, and somewhere that was not quite anywhere, a living Niko turned his face away. [500]
Then time entered her hands. [505]
It was not smooth, nor was it a line; it was rope against skin, river silt between fingers, the waxy hush of prison candles, and the thin, resilient nap of a well-read page. [538]
Its deepest layer was the pressure of a choice remaining after the chooser had been forgiven. [554]
Lumen unfolded into stars, and the valley resumed: water completed its splash, a prisoner finished his song, and a child laughed at the exact place where silence had been. [583]
Vesa left the rope in the grotto, no longer an inheritance but a pattern, each fray marking what love could not make harmless. [606]
On the road she wrote no epitaph, only a verse for inn doors: Choose gently when you can; when you cannot, let the wound keep telling the truth. [634]
Travelers called it merry, and sometimes it was, which made the tears beneath it more useful. [650]
At night Vesa walked on, feeling time roughen and soften under her tongue, speaking Niko's name not to recover him, but to give the world he had purchased one more tender, dangerous morning. [683]
