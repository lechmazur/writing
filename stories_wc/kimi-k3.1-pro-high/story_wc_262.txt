<story>By the time the storm reached the Orpheum, Mara had counted eleven goodbye letters from her sister and one she had never dared to open. [25] She carried them up to the vintage movie palace projection booth, where Lena had worked before illness made her handwriting stagger. [46] Outside, thunder moved without breaking, a hushed thunder that seemed to shake dust loose inside the walls. [63] Mara was a crystal gazer from the pier arcade, accustomed to seeing possibilities curve inside glass, but tonight she needed evidence. [84] Lena's first ten letters had forgiven her for vanished birthdays, unanswered calls, and the last argument neither could remember starting. [104] The eleventh contained only a film strip and directions to incubate it beneath the booth's old lamp until its frames cleared. [125] The twelfth, Lena warned, must be read where unused love is stored. [137] Mara had come to find that place, though she no longer believed in rooms that could keep anything safe. [156] She laid the strip across her crystal sphere and switched on the lamp. [169] Its amber light made the booth resemble a small planet warming around a patient star. [184] While forgotten lyrics search for tune, Lena used to say, wait for the projector's second click. [200] Their mother had sung at closing time, always losing the words, and Lena had supplied them in whispers. [218] Mara waited through one click, then heard another as the film loosened in the heat. [233] The cloudy frames cleared into measurements of the theater: balcony, aisle, stage, booth. [246] Being spatially imaginative, Mara did not see a floor plan but a body folded around an empty heart. [264] She arranged the letters on the floor according to those measurements, each envelope a wall, each crease a passage. [283] The pattern pointed toward the sealed reel cabinet behind her. [293] Inside sat hundreds of envelopes bearing names of ushers, actors, and patrons long gone. [308] A label read, GOODBYE LETTERS, UNSENT. [314] Mara understood that Lena had collected confessions left after final shows, when people spoke to the screen because facing one another hurt more. [337] Yet understanding the cabinet did not tell her why she had been summoned. [350] She lifted the warmed film strip, and its emulsion stuck lightly to her palm. [364] A psychometry reading was her last resort, since touch gave her other people's memories without asking whether she wanted them. [384] She closed her eyes and let the strip remember. [393] Lena stood in the booth on her final night, filming not faces but distances: the gap between seats, the space below the screen, the doorway Mara had exited years before. [423] Then Lena placed the camera against her chest and whispered, Nothing loved is unused; it only waits for somewhere to go. [444] The vision ended with her threading the projector and leaving the takeup reel empty. [458] Mara opened her eyes, shaking. [463] Unused love was not stored in the cabinet at all. [473] The cabinet held evidence of people stopping at goodbye, and Lena had built Mara a path beyond it. [491] Mara loaded the film strip and aimed the projector through the booth's small window. [505] Light crossed the empty theater and struck the screen, where Lena's measured spaces became doors, windows, and a road leading out. [526] With no takeup reel, the strip spilled gently around Mara's shoes, as if the past refused to be wound up again. [547] She opened the twelfth letter. [552] It said, Forgive yourself for surviving me, then use what remains. [563] Below, in weaker ink, Lena had written their mother's unfinished song. [574] Mara sang the first line, and the forgotten lyrics found their tune in the space between projector beats. [592] Her voice was poor, but the palace carried it tenderly into every seat. [605] She took one old envelope from the cabinet, addressed it to the arcade's frightened new fortune teller, and wrote that grief could visit without being given the keys. [633] Then she wrote to Lena, not goodbye, but where she was going. [645] At dawn, Mara left the booth door open and carried the crystal sphere downstairs. [659] In its curved glass, the theater no longer looked like a body around an absence. [674] It looked like a lantern with room for a flame. [684] Behind her, thunder softened, and the film kept running toward morning. [695]</story>
