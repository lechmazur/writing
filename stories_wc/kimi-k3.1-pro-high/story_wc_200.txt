In the hush of a hidden sunrise, before light could climb the walls of Echo Gorge, Sen arrived carrying a harp and a box of hollow bones for gathering light. [30]
He was a collector of lost souls, though the gorge had quietly collected him. [44]
For nine years he had returned their confessions to the living, yet he could not remember the sound of his own laughter. [66]
Each soul arrived as a blue shiver between the rocks, and each left behind a spectral confession: the cruelty, cowardice, or love that death had made impossible to hide. [95]
Only when the confession was spoken in a human voice could the soul stop echoing and cross. [112]
Sen spoke for them because his dead sister, Lio, had once asked him to make healing sound less like a grave. [133]
Lio had been a ferrywoman, modestly fearless enough to cross floodwater when priests barred the river, and she had worn a crocodile's tooth on a cord for luck. [161]
The tooth now served as his plectrum, its curved point worrying music from strings no living maker had tuned. [180]
This dawn, three souls waited, but only two lights hovered above the water. [193]
His harp was deliberately misaligned, its middle strings tuned a breath apart, so every note split and wandered where a straight note could not. [217]
Through those distorted notes he could pursue a soul around stone, memory, and lies. [231]
He played for the first, a smuggler who confessed that her celebrated courage had begun with abandoning her son. [250]
Sen carried the words to the sleeping village above, where the son, now gray, answered, "She taught me to swim before she left." [273]
His startled laugh broke into weeping, and the blue light thinned into gold. [286]
The second soul confessed to setting the ferry adrift in envy of Lio, and Sen's hands stopped. [303]
It was the ferry on which Lio had died saving strangers, the loss that had turned Sen from a cheerful boatbuilder into a keeper of the dead. [330]
He expected hatred to steady him, but the soul added, "She laughed when the rope reached the last child." [349]
"Not because she was unafraid," it whispered, "but because she thought being afraid should not get the last word." [368]
Sen forced the confession into his own mouth, and when he finished, the soul followed the first into morning. [387]
The third light fled down the gorge, small and quick as a minnow. [400]
Sen followed, modestly fearless in the way of tired people: knees shaking, steps careful, going anyway. [416]
He struck the harp, and its wounded harmonies ran ahead of him, doubling back from every wall until the fugitive had nowhere to hide except inside his oldest grief. [445]
He saw Lio beside their childhood river, holding the crocodile's tooth before his frightened eyes and pretending to bite her own nose. [467]
He had laughed then, a helpless hiccup, while the little ferry rocked beneath them. [481]
The light settled on his shoulder, and he knew her. [491]
Lio's confession was not heroic. [496]
"I wanted to live," she said. [502]
"I saved them, and I was glad, but I still wanted to watch you build ugly boats and grow old." [522]
"Forgive me for leaving you with all that love." [531]
Sen understood why she had run: he had made her a saint because saints were easier to lose than sisters. [551]
He gave the whole confession back in his living voice, without making it braver or cleaner. [567]
He lowered the harp, but she touched the crooked strings. [577]
"One more," she said. [581]
He played the note that had always returned alone, and this time her laugh met it, bright, lopsided, and alive. [601]
The gorge answered with his own forgotten laugh, and the ache in him did not vanish; it opened, letting light through. [622]
Lio crossed in that sound, not cured of death, but whole within it. [635]
At full sunrise, Sen climbed toward the village with the harp, the empty bone box, and the tooth warm between his fingers. [657]
He would keep collecting, for the dead still needed voices, but he would no longer polish every sorrow until it shone like virtue. [680]
Sometimes, he promised Lio's fading echo, healing would arrive as a lamp, sometimes as tears, and sometimes as an ugly, helpless snort beside the water. [705]
When the smuggler's gray son met him at the path, Sen tried it, and after one astonished breath, the old man joined in. [728]
Their laughter entered the gorge below, where the harp's split notes were still wandering, and for once nothing there was lost. [749]
