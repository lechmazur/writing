<story>After the first snowmelt, Sera returned to the bronze grotto under starlight, carrying six empty purses and a kerosene lamp burner wrapped in wool. [24] She was a fractal weaver, and her work was to braid burdens until each great grief repeated honestly in its smallest fibers. [46] Only by collecting the weights of different burdens could she finish the great net that kept her flood-prone village's seed grain suspended above spring water. [71] The grotto offered the last six measures she needed, but its threshold had been built to refuse departure. [89] Once inside, she found no wall at her back, only another bending chamber, and then another, until she was boundary-lessly trapped. [110] Water slipped from the bronze scales overhead, making a shingled hush, each drop muffled by the ringing of the one before. [131] Sera unpacked her whalebone shuttle and let its cold polish return her to the reverie required for listening. [149] A burden, her teacher had said, speaks truly only when its weight is allowed to surprise the hand. [167]

The first basin held a shepherd's relief at finding his flock, light as thawed breath, while the second held his shame for having slept, which bowed the scale. [195] Next came a widow's patience, heavier than her anger, and a child's terror of the dark, no heavier than a seed until Sera admitted she shared it. [222] She tied each measure into the web, splitting every cord into finer cords that kept the same proportions, so no sorrow could masquerade as the whole design. [249] The pattern grew like frost remembering a fern, and the six purses filled with labeled knots rather than stones. [268] But when Sera reached for the seventh empty loop, the one reserved for her own burden, the grotto answered. [287] Bronze runoff thickened around her ankles, and her unfinished threads began to calcify, their branching courage hardening into a cage of white stone. [310] She had weighed strangers gently for years while calling her fear of being needed forever a private and therefore weightless thing. [331] Now the lie fixed her to the floor, and every chamber repeated her rigid silhouette into the dark. [349]

She could have cut the web away, but then the unfinished net would warp, and the spring floods would take the grain. [371] Instead, she fitted the burner to its little kerosene reservoir and lit it with the last dry match. [389] The flame did not show her an exit; it showed her which way the grotto was leaning. [406] By turning the burner until its air slots faced the meltwater draft, she steadied the flame into a narrow blue finger. [427] By orienting properly, she meant not north or home, but the exact relation between a burden and the hand that chose to carry it. [451] She set the shepherd's shame below his relief, the widow's patience beside her anger, and the child's terror within her own open loop. [474] Then she named her fear plainly: she wanted to be needed, but not devoured. [488] The admission weighed more than all six purses, yet once knotted into the fractal, it became also the smallest repeatable loop. [509] A loop could be opened. [514]

Sera passed the whalebone shuttle through it and drew the entire web behind it, not breaking the stone, but teaching it a shape with an inside and an outside. [543] The calcified cage softened into brittle lace. [550] She stepped across a boundary the grotto had never possessed until her pattern gave it one. [566] Behind her, the chambers folded shut like a fan, and the shingled hush became simple water descending through stone. [585] At dawn she emerged beyond the snowline with the burner cold in her pocket and seven weights pulling evenly against her heart. [607]

The village received the finished net, and no seam sagged when the spring floods came. [622] Yet Sera kept the seventh purse for herself, not as a relic, but as a tool. [638] Whenever someone brought her a burden too large for speech, she first checked the burner's orientation, then laid her own small weight beside it. [662] Thus the sufferer could feel the difference between company and captivity before the weaving began. [677] And in the bronze dark beneath the stars, the grotto kept one new chamber, narrow as a doorway, where meltwater sang without an echo. [701]</story>
