<story>On the night of the sky lantern release, Elian worked the late shift as pier lamplighter, trimming wicks while the town prepared its bright petitions. [25] He told the harbormaster, "I only tend what burns," a truth behind which he hid his real errand. [43] For eleven years he had wandered purposefully after closing, seeking the color of sound for his sister, who had heard it before the sea took her. [69] Her only legacy was a hotel skeleton key tag, brass and oval, stamped MERIDIAN, ROOM 0, with a key wired through its hole. [92] The hotel had burned before Elian was born, yet its unlisted room was where guests went to hear lost music, according to her notebook. [116] Tonight, three lanterns rose green above the harbor, the old signal that low tide had uncovered the wreck below. [135] Their color would signify an opening until the last lantern cleared the steeple. [148] Elian lit every pier lamp except the seventh, then hung the key tag from its cold hook. [165] Darkness answered with a stair of foam descending through black water. [176] He followed it beneath the pier, and the sea closed overhead without wetting him. [190] The sunken ship lay in a trench, its hull split like a seed, its lanterns glowing with drowned constellations. [209] Inside was a forgotten library: oak galleries, ladders bolted to curved ribs, and books sealed in glass that breathed faintly. [229] Fish threaded the aisles, silver as turning pages. [237] Elian tried the door marked 0, but the skeleton key turned without opening it. [251] Behind him, a catalog drawer slid out, holding a card that read, VOWS ILLUMINE; QUESTIONS GUIDE. [267] So he wandered purposefully, not searching shelves but letting his longing choose each turn. [281] At every crossing he told the library another truth: "She was named Mira." [294] "She said thunder was red, though she had never seen it." [305] "I promised to learn what color her voice was." [314] Each truthful sentence lit a letter on the spines, yet the aisles returned him to Room 0. [331] Above, hundreds of lanterns must have been rising, for amber shadows drifted through the wreck like slow meteors. [349] Their hush made the library seem to listen with its whole drowned hull. [362] At last Elian understood that he was still hiding behind truth, offering facts so he need not offer himself. [381] Mira had not simply heard a color; she had followed music into the wreck, and Elian had stayed ashore, calling her a liar. [404] "I was afraid," he said, and the admission shook dust from the shelves. [417] "I do not want her answer anymore; I want to stop making her death pay for my cowardice." [435] The key tag warmed, and Room 0 opened onto a single lectern bearing Mira's notebook. [450] Its last page held no answer, only a vow she had written for them both: MAY WHAT WE LOVE LIGHT THE WAY HOME. [473] Elian read it aloud, adding, "I will guide others without sending them where I fear to go." [490] The luminous vow lifted from the paper and wound around the pier lamps far above, joining sky to wreck in one quiet radiance. [513] Around him, other luminous vows kindled in their glass books, promises of captains, cooks, and children patiently keeping watch. [532] Then the library sounded: water in wood, fish against glass, the small bronze note of the key tag. [550] Every sound shone a different color, blue, amber, green, changing as he listened. [563] Mira's voice came last, not from the dead but from his own memory, calling him in for supper. [581] It was the clear gold of a lamp lit before dark, because someone expected you home. [597] When Elian climbed back, the final lantern was passing the steeple and the sea stairs were fading. [614] He relit the seventh lamp, whose flame now burned gold without oil, and carried Mira's notebook to the harbormaster. [633] This time he told the whole truth, then began the nightly rounds with the key tag beating softly against his heart. [654] Each lamp answered in color, and people below looked up, mistaking their music for another constellation. [670] Elian kept walking, no longer after Mira's answer, but within the light of his own. [685]</story>
