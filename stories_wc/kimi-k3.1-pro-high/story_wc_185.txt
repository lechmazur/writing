<story>After the energy beings arrived, the harmonic caves stopped keeping time with the tides and began answering every thought with thunder. [21] Their gently luminous bodies gathered above the entrance like a second dawn, but their fear swept through the stone galleries as a storm. [44] Neri, the last ripple-mark mapkeeper, watched the cave pool shiver into warnings no living hand could read. [61] Others begged her to chart an escape, yet every passage she marked ended in splitting rock or a chorus that drove sleepers screaming awake. [85] She refused to mistake flight for safety. [92] Somewhere inside that violence had to be a still center, and she meant to find the quiet in the storm before the mountain broke. [116] From her satchel she took the echo chamber stone, a palm-sized hollow rock that returned every sound it had ever received. [137] Its oldest echoes were hammers, her mother and grandmother dressing cave walls so each chamber would carry a true note. [157] Neri had inherited their patience along with their scars, though patience now felt like a roof under rain. [175] At the frozen waterfall she halted, because the ice had cracked during the first assault and no map was newer than damage. [197] Examining crack patterns in ice, she saw white fractures crowd around resonant pillars, then thin toward a seam that crossed the sealed eastern wall. [221] Where stress had passed, ice remembered; where silence endured, it remained clear. [233] She followed that clear vein with one finger and understood that it continued beyond the wall. [249] A strike of her hammer opened not a tunnel, but a narrow chamber blue with frozen breath. [266] Inside waited one of the beings, no larger than a child, dimming each time the storm called its name. [285] Neri raised her tool, then lowered it when the stranger pressed light against the floor and revealed the same frightened ripples she had seen in the pool. [312] They were not commanding the storm; they were being tossed inside it, their arrival amplifying one another's terror through the mountain. [333] The small being touched the stone, which answered with three generations of hammer blows and Neri's own sharp breath. [352] Shamed by that breath, Neri finally admitted she had come armed because quiet seemed impossible without conquest. [369] The being pointed deeper, where clear ice enclosed a round hollow like an unopened bud. [384] Her oldest maps named such places wombs of the bloom of hush, a phrase she had dismissed as temple poetry. [404] Now she read the ripple-marks around the hollow and found no instruction for weapon or prayer, only a blank space shaped like listening. [427] To conjure stillness there, she could not command the echoes; she had to let them finish. [443] Neri set the echo chamber stone within the hollow and opened its sealed pore. [457] Out poured pick and hammer, infant cries, funeral singing, her mother's last cough, and the recent thunder, each voice striking her without mercy. [480] She held her ground, neither answering nor shutting her ears, while the small being laid its light against her wrist. [500] Their pulses met, faltered, and met again, until the last echo had somewhere gentle to go. [516] Then the stone gave back nothing. [522] In that nothing, the bloom of hush unfolded, not as emptiness but as room between sounds, petal after petal of measured silence. [544] It passed through ice without breaking it and entered every chamber along the clear veins Neri had mapped. [562] The storm did not die; it separated into countless voices, each audible, none devouring another. [577] Above the entrance, the energy beings settled into slow constellations, and the caves returned their music note by note. [596] When the people emerged, they found Neri asleep beside the frozen fall, her hammer loose in her hand and her face wet but unclenched. [620] The small being guarded her with a glow no brighter than banked coals. [633] Later, Neri redrew the cave maps, leaving broad margins where no path was needed and marking the eastern chamber with an open hand. [656] She taught the beings to read water, and they taught her that even light must rest between one hue and the next. [678] On quiet nights she still heard thunder beyond the mountain, but it no longer sounded like a world ending. [697] It sounded like weather passing over a home made strong enough to listen.
