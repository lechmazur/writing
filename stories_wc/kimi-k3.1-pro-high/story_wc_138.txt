<story>For nine thousand years, Vey had outlasted kings, stars, and every clever way of being bored, until the prismatic escondite offered one final diversion. [24] The refuge hid inside folded space beneath a dying sun, its crystal corridors drinking light and returning whispers. [42] Vey was dimensionally aware, able to feel each wall's private angle, so the first mutiny felt like a held breath. [62] A corridor that had always led toward the observatory turned politely aside and delivered Vey to the sealed tomb of Sera, the only companion who had ever told him goodbye. [92] Beyond the glass, her body remained bright and unchanged, though three centuries had passed since she chose mortality over his endless tomorrow. [114] Around her neck lay the missing half of a cursed amulet fragment; Vey carried its twin beneath his shirt, where it had cooled against his skin for lifetimes. [142] The curse was simple: joined, the pieces would open any door, but something on the other side could open him. [162] A soft impact rolled through the crystal, thunder with its mouth shut. [174] Above them, a solar storm had begun, and magnetic fields combed the escondite's hidden dimensions into visible, trembling threads. [193] Then Warden Ilex arrived, smiling as though the tomb were his own invention. [206] "The rooms are disobeying me," he said, "but they seem eager to please you." [220] Vey noticed the iron dust on Ilex's cuffs and understood the intrigue before the warden could sweeten it with lies. [240] Ilex had been tuning the refuge through magnetic fields, teaching its geometry to refuse old commands and accept new masters. [260] The spatial mutiny was not madness; it was an election conducted with hinges, thresholds, and gravity. [276] "Give me your fragment," Ilex said, "and I will open Sera's tomb before the storm tears us into neighboring centuries." [296] Vey almost laughed from sheer gratitude; at last, an immortal's boredom had become someone else's emergency. [312] Yet Sera's farewell returned to him, not as sorrow, but as a question he had never dared to ask. [331] What happened after goodbye, when presence ended and the beloved continued somewhere beyond witness? [345] He wanted to discover it, not merely die, which was why death itself had always disappointed him as an answer. [365] Vey pressed his fragment to the glass, and Sera's half answered with a pulse like distant lightning. [382] The tomb opened inward, revealing no corpse, but a narrow country of dark air where her last gesture still hung: two fingers lifted, neither beckoning nor farewell. [409] Ilex reached for the joined amulet, and Vey let him take it. [421] That was his own small conspiracy, because he had felt the refuge listening through the storm and knew what it wanted. [442] The warden commanded a door to eternity; instead, every door in the escondite opened onto Ilex's frightened childhood room. [461] While he stared, Vey drew the iron dust from his own sleeve and scattered it across the threshold. [479] The storm seized those filings, and magnetic fields wrote Sera's final gesture into space. [493] The refuge understood, folded once, and gently voted Ilex elsewhere. [503] His cry became weather in another sky. [510] Then the mutinous corridors aligned around Vey, not as subjects around a ruler, but as mourners around a grave. [529] He took up the amulet, now whole and warm, and stepped into the dark country. [544] There was no Sera waiting there, no paradise, no punishments, only the shape every farewell left behind when no one remained to hold it. [568] Vey crossed it and felt his endless years loosen, each memory becoming a room he could finally leave without locking. [588] Behind him, the escondite closed its crystal eyes; ahead, something used his name without speaking it. [604] He answered by letting go of the amulet. [612] The curse opened him gently, and out poured every door he had ever feared, shining, weightless, and empty. [630] Beyond them was not an end, but a vast permission to become unknowable, even to himself. [646] Vey laughed then, softly, while the solar storm made hushed thunder of the worlds. [660] For the first time, goodbye did not sound like absence; it sounded like space making room. [676]</story>
