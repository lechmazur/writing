After the birth, when her daughter's first cry had thinned into sleep, Ione laid the onyx-ink cartomancer's cards beside the newborn and saw that every answer she had ever sold was still drinking from her life. [36]
The village women had gone, and on the windowsill the dolls' tea set saucer she had kept since childhood ticked against its crack, pointing west like a compass. [64]
Ione wrapped her daughter against her heart, took the saucer and her family's birthday tablecloth, and followed that porcelain needle into the sky. [87]
For generations their custom of tracing hands on birthday tablecloths had made a ledger of growth, each child pressing chalked fingers beside the outlines of the dead. [114]
Ione had fled that tenderness at seventeen, preferring destinies she could shuffle to years she had to live. [132]
Her ink, ground from onyx and grief, had since bought other people certainty with pieces of her own hours. [151]
Now, to find where lost time accumulates, she climbed a stair of moonlit vapors until the midwife's cottage sank beneath her and the world became white. [177]
At midnight she entered a glade above clouds, where black lilies drank starlight and every petal held a face she had once advised. [200]
Their beauty choked the air; breathing felt like swallowing roses. [210]
In the center stood a tree of transparent hands, its branches heavy with bright drops, each drop a moment stolen by her cards. [233]
Beneath it knelt a figure bound in cloud, and Ione recognized her own mother, dead these twenty years. [251]
"Cloudbound repentance keeps no prisoners," the figure said, "only promises we refuse to unfasten." [265]
Ione had come for restitution, not riddles, yet she made herself willingly perplexed, letting the old anger loosen before she demanded to know whether this was ghost, judgment, or mercy. [295]
Her mother touched the tablecloth, and the smallest outline bloomed blue: Ione at five, her hand almost hidden inside her mother's. [316]
"You traced round me because I was afraid of being alone," Ione said. [329]
"Then you learned to make every tomorrow stand still until it obeyed." [341]
The infant stirred, and the saucer jerked toward the tree. [351]
Its crack widened, spilling not tea but the hush between a child's question and a parent's answer, all the pauses Ione had sold as worthless. [376]
She understood then that lost time did not gather like coin; it gathered like breath around the people from whom it had been taken. [400]
Her clients below were waking older in their beds, while she had mistaken her own numbness for survival. [418]
To restore them would return every prophecy to uncertainty, including the one she secretly cherished: that her daughter would never leave her. [440]
Ione opened the deck. [444]
The last card showed the girl grown, vanishing down a road whose bends no ink could enter. [461]
Around its edges her own hand trembled, aged beyond naming. [471]
She could keep that single jewel of knowledge and let the glade keep everything else. [486]
Instead, she spread the birthday cloth over the roots and set the cracked saucer at its center. [503]
With her daughter's fist curled inside her palm, she traced both their hands, not as a claim on the future but as an admission of how briefly they could hold one another. [535]
Then she unfastened the silver clasp at her throat, the one binding her cartomancer's mantle and all its thirsty cards. [555]
The mantle lifted like a wing; the deck opened, and onyx ink rose through the glade in thousands of delicate threads. [576]
Each thread found a lily, each lily a face, and the tree began to rain. [591]
Time fell upward. [594]
It returned as a father laughing too long at supper, a widow finishing her song, a boy taking the road he had feared. [617]
Ione's stolen hours entered her as weariness, then years, silvering her hair while her mother faded with a smile of unbound grief. [639]
The glade dissolved into dawn, and Ione woke on her cottage floor with the baby nursing and the saucer whole, though its crack remained drawn in gold. [667]
On the tablecloth, beside their fresh outlines, stood one sentence in her mother's hand: Live without making a shape of it. [688]
Ione burned the cards, kept the gold seam, and when her daughter cried again, did not reach for any future but that small, furious, unknowable mouth. [714]
