<story>After the tower fell, its bell rolled into the dunes and rang whenever the wind remembered violence. [17] By dawn, Mira was already working beneath the glass dome of her desert rose farm, where heat shone like a tranquil blaze. [39] She was a bounty hunter's daughter, but she had never taken a bounty, lifted a rifle, or forgiven the profession its easy names for grief. [64] Her father, Cato, had vanished before the tower's collapse, leaving her his knife, his debts, and a talent for noticing what others buried. [87] Mira was senselessly perceptive: she could not explain why a closed door frightened her, or why healthy roots sometimes made her weep. [109] That morning, the roses turned their dark faces toward the northern glass, though the sun stood south. [126] Beneath their bench lay a scrap from an airship ballast sandbag, stamped with Cato's private mark, three cuts around a seed. [147] She had sewn that coarse cloth into every root bundle, believing discarded weight could teach fragile things to hold. [166] On its newest patch, fresh from some wreck, Cato had engraved quiet with the point of his knife. [184] The words were not a message exactly, but a method. [194] Since childhood, he had been inculcating plants with values, reading to seedlings while Mira tied their stems, insisting that patience entered through repetition. [217] She had mocked him until the farm survived three raids without once being abandoned. [231] Now she pressed her palm to the scrap and felt the dome shiver before she heard the engines. [249] An airship descended beyond the glass, trailing sand and black canvas. [260] Six hunters entered with Cato between them, alive, thin, and manacled. [271] Their captain, Vey, set a warrant on the potting table. [281] "The tower paid for your father's head," Vey said. [290] "The tower is dead," Mira answered, "but I suppose debt is hardier." [302] Vey smiled and offered her half the reward if she surrendered the seed vault, where Cato had hidden the tower's payroll ledger. [324] With it, every unpaid gun could name the officers who had purchased the war. [338] Cato watched his daughter, not the ledger door. [346] That small omission told her he expected refusal, and feared it. [357] Mira drew his old knife from her apron. [365] Vey's people tensed, but she only cut a rose, split its stem, and slid in a strip of sandbag cloth. [385] Then she sheathed the blade in wet soil. [392] "You taught me values take root when witnessed," she told Cato. [403] "So witness this: no one purchases my hands." [411] She opened the irrigation gates. [416] Water threaded the beds, carrying rust red dye from the cloth into channels spelling names across the floor. [434] The hunters looked down and saw their own names among the dead. [446] Cato had not merely hidden the ledger; he had grafted its evidence into the farm, and Mira, reading roots all her life, understood at last. [471] The roses had turned north because their roots avoided Vey's buried transmitter. [483] Her strange perceptions had been the farm speaking through her. [493] Vey fired at the vault, cracking the dome instead. [501] Glass fell in bright, slow flakes, and outside the desert seemed to ignite without burning. [516] The hunters fled toward their ship, but its loosened ballast bags poured sand into the engine intakes. [533] Mira had noticed their knots hours ago, without knowing why she noticed. [545] Cato reached for the knife, then stopped when she covered his hand. [557] Together they opened the outer doors and let the hunters run. [568] Vey alone remained, staring at the warrant as wind worried it from the table. [582] Mira offered him water and a blank page. [590] "Continuity is not keeping the world unchanged," she said. [599] "It is choosing what crosses the fire." [606] At dusk, survivors came from the fallen tower, drawn by signals the roses cast through broken glass. [623] Mira gave each a cutting wrapped in sandbag cloth, with one shared word stitched inside. [638] Some carried testimony, some seed, and some only the courage to begin again. [651] Cato planted beside her, silent for once, while Vey wrote names until his hand stopped shaking. [667] Above them, the dome admitted sky in jagged pieces, yet the beds held their shape. [682] To weave continuity through chaos, Mira decided, was not to mend every tear, but to give each loose thread somewhere living to go. [705] She pressed Cato's scrap around the final root and felt, beneath her fingers, the engraved quiet flowering into flame. [724]</story>
