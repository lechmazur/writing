Mara had painted generals, brides, and children with impossible patience, but since her sister Eda drowned, every face she touched turned blank beneath her brush. [25]
Crestfallen, she carried her brushes and Eda's broken tarot card box to the forgotten library inside the last lighthouse on Bellwether Rock. [47]
The box was the only thing Eda had left her, its corners split, its gilt cards whispering together whenever the tide turned. [69]
Beyond the lighthouse door, reality had come apart without quite falling. [80]
Spiral stairs opened into the same ink scented library at three different heights, rain hung above reading lamps like suspended beads, and books on distant shelves turned their pages in yesterday's wind. [112]
Mara found Eda seated beside the cold hearth, or found a figure wearing Eda's green coat, its face a smooth oval of light. [135]
"You came to finish me," it said. [142]
"Can you finish what the sea has unmade?" Mara asked, then hated the question, because she had spent a year asking the sea the same thing. [168]
The figure opened the broken box, and luminous threads spilled among the cards, each one running through a book, a wall, or one of Mara's memories. [194]
One thread showed Eda laughing on the lighthouse steps; another showed the wave; a third, knotted around Mara's own wrist, showed the portrait she had refused to paint from the recovery stretcher. [226]
At once the walls began to close in, shelves grinding toward the hearth as the sea pressed black hands against the glass. [248]
"The library keeps every moment apart so none can hurt the others," Eda's figure said. [263]
"Now there are too many." [268]
"Was this your mercy?" Mara asked the narrowing room. [277]
"A world where nothing touched, nothing changed, nothing could be lost?" [288]
The questions needed no answer; the shelves answered by splintering. [298]
Mara understood that the figure was not Eda returned, but the library wearing her dearest shape, and that no portrait could rescue it. [321]
To redesign the fractured refuge, she would have to descend into the necessary darkness below it, where all the threads had been knotted around the dead lamp. [348]
"Will I find her there?" she asked. [355]
"You will find where you left her," the figure said, and gave her the broken box. [371]
Down iron stairs, Mara followed the wrist thread through flooding dark, while above her the closing library boomed like a heart under a fist. [395]
At the lamp's root she found a knot vast as a nest, woven from moments she had kept apart: Eda's hand in hers, Eda's hand letting go, her own hand signing the morgue book. [429]
She wanted to cut it. [434]
Wouldn't one clean stroke be kinder than this endless untangling? [444]
But severed moments would only make more fragments, so she began untangling threads, naming each aloud and returning it to its place in the single life they had shared. [473]
The work hurt without wounding her. [479]
Childhood loosened into salt bright mornings; the quarrel before Eda's last patrol loosened into two frightened voices; the wave loosened into water, not punishment. [503]
Last came the stretcher thread, and Mara saw that she had not failed to paint Eda's dead face out of weakness, but because painting it would have made goodbye real. [533]
"Then let it be real," she whispered, and drew the final thread free. [546]
Darkness opened, not into Eda, but into the small blue hollow of the lamp. [560]
Mara did not repair the lighthouse as it had been. [570]
Using the split box as a frame, she redesigned its lamp as a loom, passing the freed threads through slots she cut in the cards so that every turning beam would weave past and present together without binding them. [609]
When Mara climbed back, the walls had stopped an arm's width from her shoulders. [623]
Then the new lamp turned, and the library unfolded with a sigh: stairs chose one ascent, rain fell, and every book opened to a different, bearable page. [650]
The figure in green had become only a coat on a chair, warmed by a bar of pearl colored light. [670]
Mara set up her canvas and painted Eda alive, not as she had looked before the wave, but as she remained in the threadwork of Mara's days: laughing, arguing, leaving, beloved. [701]
When at last she signed her name, the ache in her chest did not vanish; it shone, and by its light she could finally see her own face. [729]
