<story>During the blackout, when the sunken city's last lamps drowned in green dark, Mira Venn kept working in the forgotten library beneath the old customs dome. [26] She was its logical page indexer, trained to give every surviving sheet a place, a relation, and a reason. [45] Without electricity, the catalog engines were silent, and water ticked through the roof like a patient second hand. [63] Yet Mira felt no panic. [67] Her gift, mistrusted by quicker clerks, was to remain tranquilly perceptive when evidence refused to behave. [83] Tonight she sought the founding charter, hoping to trace honest origins for the city before its council rewrote them. [102] The official index pointed to a dry archive, an absurdity in a library that had rested underwater for two centuries. [122] Still, a fresh request had come from above: certify that the founders had freely chosen the sea, not fled a massacre. [143] Mira laid the request beside an antique ink stamp recovered that morning from a collapsed catalog drawer. [160] Its brass handle was warm from her hand; its face bore a gull over the words RETURN TO SOURCE. [179] By lantern light, she noticed blue pigment in its seams, though every approved record used black. [195] The discrepancy offered an alternative hypothesis: the index was not damaged, but deliberately arranged to hide a second sequence. [214] If true, the missing charter would announce itself through misnumbered pages rather than shelf marks. [229] Mira closed her eyes and listened until the building's groans separated from the smaller sounds of paper, dripping salt, and her own breath. [252] In that reclaimed stillness, she remembered that blue gulls had once marked testimony taken from witnesses. [268] She opened three ledgers whose page numbers repeated, pressed the stamp to their margins, and found the gull's wing aligned with nearly invisible cuts. [292] Joined, the cuts formed a tide chart. [299] Its lowest point marked the dome itself, at an hour when the blackout would leave the moon pool exposed. [318] Mira crossed the flooded reading room, her boots stirring silt into clouds that caught the lantern in dappled gold. [337] Beneath a toppled atlas stand, she found a stair descending into air that smelled of iron. [353] The official plan showed solid wall there; her readjusted sequence showed a door. [366] She had to readjust the bronze wheel three times before the hidden lock accepted the tide chart's order. [384] Beyond it, hundreds of sworn statements lined a narrow chamber, each stamped blue, each naming council ancestors as the men who had opened the sea gates. [410] The founders had not chosen drowning; they had survived it, then built their peace on other people's graves. [428] At the chamber's center lay the charter, dry inside a glass bell, its final clause reserving the library for anyone displaced by the city. [452] Mira read the names of the dead, and her hands began to shake. [465] The honest origin was not a clean beginning but an obligation. [476] Above her, stone cracked. [480] The blackout had stopped the pumps, and the sea was resuming its argument with the dome. [496] She could save the charter alone, light enough to carry while climbing, or the heavy testimony that proved why its clause mattered. [518] For one dangerous moment, purity tempted her: a single immaculate document, easier to defend than a hundred grieving voices. [537] Then she stamped the charter's cover, not to claim it, but to bind it to its sources. [554] She packed the statements around it in a canvas atlas case and climbed as water chased her ankles. [572] At the top, the lantern failed, leaving her beneath a roof of black glass and trembling reflected stars. [590] Mira did not run for the council boats already knocking at the upper quay. [604] Instead she woke the sheltering divers, divided the pages among their waterproof tubes, and taught each carrier the sequence. [623] No single capture could now silence the whole account. [632] When dawn silvered the flooded streets, the council found Mira seated on the library steps, case empty, stamp in her lap. [653] They demanded the founding truth. [658] She looked at the scattered boats returning through drowned avenues, each carrying a fragment, each fragment incomplete without the rest. [678] "It begins with listening," she said, and pressed the blue gull into the first blank page of a new index. [698] Around them, the city remained broken, but the silence no longer belonged to those who had made it. [716]</story>
