<story>When the magician's mirror trick failed, the silver glass did not shatter; it exhaled. [14] A cold breath crossed the cursed lake, and every apple tree on its shore turned toward the water. [32] From the reeds, Druid Maera watched petals rise like a host lifting white shields. [46] She was a guarded woman even among her own grove, more practiced with a bow than with pardon. [64] Yet her gift was gentle: she found lost things. [73] Buttons, sheep, sleepwalkers, and once a king's courage had returned at her call. [86] Only her brother remained lost, frozen beneath the lake with his confession sealed in ice around his mouth. [104] The magician had promised to melt that ice by making the moon behold itself. [118] Instead, the failed reflection woke the orchard vortex at the lake's heart. [130] Trunks tore free and began to swirl around a black calm, their branches rowing against the stars. [147] The magician fled, leaving Maera his mirror and one brass seed. [158] She knew the seed by its toothlike shell. [166] Her brother had carried its twin the night the lake was cursed. [178] It was a flesh-eating plant seed, bred in old wars to clean a battlefield of bodies. [194] She had called such crops obscene, yet she slipped this one into her belt pouch. [209] For twelve years she had polished mirrors, hoping one would show why her gentle brother had poisoned their orchard. [228] They showed his crime, her accusations, even the ice closing over him, but never what he had seen beneath the water. [249] Now she meant to discover what mirrors do not show. [259] She laid the mirror face down and unstrung her bow. [269] To find what was lost, she began where she stood and called his name through expanding circles. [286] First the pebbles answered, then the drowned bells, then roots under the spinning orchard. [300] Each wider circle returned something small: a thimble, a baby tooth, her own unguarded laugh. [315] At the seventh circle, the ice answered with his voice. [325] "I planted it," he said, "but not to kill our trees." [336] "There is something under us that wears reflections and eats every face it learns." [350] The confession broke free in shards and struck her like sleet, yet Maera stood serene inside the storm. [368] War had once made that serenity useful; love would have to make it enough. [382] In the vortex's still center rose a white trunk bearing the faces of everyone the lake had taken. [400] Her own face looked out, hard and blameless, exactly as her mirrors had always shown it. [416] Beneath it, her brother hung in clear ice, alive because the thing was saving his voice for last. [434] Maera could shoot the trunk, but its roots wrapped his ribs. [445] She could keep hating him and remain the flawless sentinel of a lifeless shore. [459] Instead she chose to unbend. [464] Slowly, against every lesson of siege and survival, she curved her rigid back and set the seed in her open palm. [485] "I accused you because guilt was easier than grief," she said. [496] The hidden face in the trunk opened its mouth to steal hers. [508] Maera fed it the seed instead. [514] Roots finer than hair entered the false bark, and the flesh-eating flower bloomed without sound. [529] It devoured stolen flesh, chewing reflections away until the trunk became a bare black stone. [544] The orchard slowed, circle by circle, and trees descended into the mud with their roots politely gathered. [561] Her brother fell into her arms, cold, coughing, and real beyond any glass. [574] At dawn, the magician returned for his mirror and found Maera beside it, replanting the orchard with her brother. [593] He asked whether she had learned what mirrors hide. [602] "Everything that matters," she said; "they stop at the skin." [612] She gave him the glass, keeping only its wooden back to patch a nesting box. [627] Around them, petals fell like disarmed arrows, and the lake held the morning without claiming it. [643]</story>
