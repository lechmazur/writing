<story>After going off the grid, Mara Venn crossed the ash desert alone, carrying a faded tapestry fragment and the name the patrols had given her: lending library arsonist. [28]
She had burned three libraries because their ledgers sentenced borrowers who could not pay to lifelong labor, but she had never burned a book. [52]
Now she sought the lost civilization archive beneath Mount Vey, where those ledgers had been born and where her brother Orin had vanished. [75]
Before the soldiers took him, she had promised to find whatever he loved and keep it from being forgotten. [94]
The promise had survived prison, hunger, and her own furious wish to let the whole world burn. [111]
At the archive's basalt mouth, a clay swallow waited in a niche, one wing painted blue. [127]
Mara knew Orin's work, yet her recognition was more than memory. [138]
Prison had made her analytically intuitive, trained by necessity to feel when a pattern mattered before she could explain why. [158]
She unfolded the tapestry fragment, salvaged from their mother's loom, and matched its blue thread to the swallow's wing. [177]
The bird pointed left, toward a corridor official maps called collapsed. [188]
Beyond it lay a trail of artistic creations leading to a hidden message, though Mara did not yet know the message was the trail. [212]
A copper flower turned its petals toward a draft. [221]
A chalk moon concealed a stair. [227]
Seven glass tears, each holding a different color from the tapestry, marked safe stones above a black crevasse. [245]
Orin had not merely left directions; he had made beauty sturdy enough to carry her weight. [261]
The mountain breathed heat through the floor, and Mara answered with the old tenderness of their shared childhood, fierce enough to scorch. [283]
At the lowest door, she found his vandalized self-portrait. [292]
Orin had painted himself holding the library keys, but censors had gouged out his face and written TRAITOR across his chest. [313]
Rage lifted in Mara like lava, and her hand closed around the lamp's burning wick. [328]
Then she noticed that the gouges followed the tapestry's weaving pattern. [339]
She pressed the fragment to the ruined face. [347]
Through its gaps, scratches became letters: DO NOT AVENGE ME. [357]
Below them, pigment flaked away in the heat, revealing the rest: RESCUE WHAT I PROMISED THEM. [373]
Mara remembered Orin begging the censors to portray every debtor as a neighbor, not a number. [389]
His forgotten promise had never been to her alone. [398]
It had been to the people whose names fed the ledgers. [409]
The portrait swung inward, exposing a chamber where ceramic leaves held thousands of handwritten names, debts, songs, and pardons. [428]
Orin had copied them before the original records were fed into the lending system. [442]
Beside the last leaf lay his bones, wrapped in their mother's tapestry, with a satchel packed for escape. [460]
He had stayed until the copying was done, and the mountain had sealed his exit. [475]
Mara knelt, but did not weep neatly. [482]
Her grief came hot and huge, cracking the silence, then cooling into something she could carry. [498]
A tremor split the ceiling. [503]
Her oldest instinct offered fire, but fire would only seal the chamber in glass. [517]
So she used the satchel, filling it with the index leaves and Orin's copy of the master key. [535]
The rest she photographed with the forbidden camera that had kept her invisible for years. [550]
Before leaving, she mixed ash with Orin's spilled pigments and restored his face upon the portrait. [566]
She did not make him innocent or gentle. [574]
She painted the stubborn jaw, the frightened eyes, and the small smile that had once dared her to steal back their mother's loom. [597]
It was the truest portrayal she could give him: a man afraid, and loving past fear. [613]
When she unsealed the upper gate at dawn, Mara climbed out through cooling ash with the names against her heart. [633]
Behind her, the mountain thundered, but she did not mistake its violence for her own. [648]
She would return above ground, reconnect the dead transmitters, and lend every stolen name back to its owner. [666]
No building would need to burn. [672]
The libraries had taught people to borrow their histories; Mara would teach them to keep the promises inside. [690]
And when children asked whose face guarded the archive door, she would say Orin's name first, then her own, and let the silence between them remain warm. [717]</story>
