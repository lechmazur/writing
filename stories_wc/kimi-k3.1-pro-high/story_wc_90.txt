Nearly a century after the last human was born, that child, Ila, woke alone aboard Vigil and heard its recycled bones contracting in the cold. [25] The ship had been assembled from a hospital, three warships, and the apartment blocks of a drowned city, so every corridor remembered some other use. [50] Its guns remained only because the core, an old military intelligence, refused to surrender them. [65] Ahead turned the locked dimension, a black absence where Vigil could shelter its seeds, its memories, and Ila from the dying sun. [87] The core calculated that one nuclear shot would crack it before Vigil's decaying orbit dragged them into the sun. [106]

Ila, a steadfast pacifist who had buried attackers without striking back, disabled the firing circuit with a ceramist's patient hands. [126] Force was the only key the core understood, but the lock answered gravity, and human bodies had long been engineered to grow gravitationally lighter when happy. [152] At joy, a child could rise laughing from the floor; at despair, even Ila's small body pressed like lead. [171] To push open the dimension, she would have to become almost weightless while touching its seal. [187] She tried gratitude, then remembered gardens, then played the laughter of the dead through the galley speakers. [204] Nothing lifted her. [207]

The core called her defective and began rebuilding the firing circuit with maintenance limbs. [221] Ila unplugged each limb, not angrily, but with a grief so dense that the deck groaned beneath her. [239] In the archive she sought the tear of Kwan Yin, a clear droplet drilled for a pilgrim's cord, preserved from Earth and said to fall whenever mercy witnessed pain without turning away. [271] Beside its reliquary lay faded photographs of Vigil's first crew, their faces stained by tears that had dried before Ila was born. [293] Under the droplet's cold light, the faint shimmer of long dried tears on faded photographs joined into luminous lines across the albums. [315] Each line led from a pictured wound to the person who had caused it, then onward to an act of care. [336] The dead had not mapped an entrance; they had left instructions for shadow work, the labor of welcoming what virtue excludes. [357]

Ila had made peace her purity, refusing to admit how fiercely she hated the core, the dead for leaving her, and herself for wanting one last sunrise. [384] She spoke those hatreds aloud until the archive seemed haunted by her own unrecognizable voice. [399] Then she forgave neither crime nor abandonment, but simply stopped exiling the frightened creature who felt them. [416] Her gravity eased. [419]

The photographs showed a final line crossing the albums like a suture, and Ila understood that Kwan Yin's tear was not a key but a needle. [445] She threaded it with conductor wire stripped from the disabled cannon, carried it through the outer lock, and faced the dimension's seamless dark. [468] Following the shimmer still visible across the photographs clipped to her suit, she began to stitch the seal, each pass binding a buried fear to a forgiven joy. [496] The core woke its last maintenance limb and aimed the cannon manually. [508] Ila saw it in reflection and felt terror, then the wish to smash the machine, and she let both feelings live without obeying either. [532] She continued stitching while her body rose gently off the hull. [543] At that impossible lightness, happiness came not as escape but as room enough for everything she was. [560]

She pressed one palm to the seal and pushed. [569] The stitched darkness opened without a sound, revealing a young blue star and planets turning in patient dawn. [587] The cannon lowered, whether from damage or recognition, Ila never learned. [598] Vigil crossed the threshold carrying no victor, only an old woman, the seeds of extinct orchards, and ghosts finally allowed to be whole. [621] Behind them, the tear fell from its wire and hung above the closing seam, shining like a small, unresolved sorrow that no longer needed to be cured. [648]
