<story>After the final warning, when the astrologers had sealed their observatory and fled, Queen Sera dreamed again of starlight bleeding through the ceiling of a cobalt grotto above the clouds. [30] In the dream, each silver drop spoke her name in her dead mother's voice, then showed her the valley withering beneath her perfect, weatherless reign. [55] Disenchanted by seven years of jeweled treaties and smiling famine, she woke with her palms wet and a warmth like a wound behind her ribs. [80] She summoned only Iven, the old lamplighter who had once carried her up the ridge on his back. [98] "You will say this is treason," she told him, softly commanding as ever, "but tonight we climb." [115] He looked at her bare feet, then fetched her boots, two cloaks, and no soldiers. [130] Their final message had been plain: if the crown entered the grotto, the protected sky would break. [147] That was why she left her crown on the pillow. [157] Below the palace, summer's first fireflies were rising from the wet terraces, tiny green stitches in the dark. [175] Iven said the ember grotto on the ridge opened only for two people who breathed as one, though he had forgotten who taught him. [199] They began to climb as the cloud sea swallowed the last windows. [211] The path narrowed to shale and old pilgrim nails; Sera's royal hands tore, and the pain pleased her with its honesty. [232] Above them, the ridge wore vapors like black lace, beautiful and vaguely threatening. [245] At its crest they found no cave, only a cobalt wall slick with cold. [259] Sera struck it once, and something within struck back. [268] Then Iven pointed down. [272] The first fireflies of summer had followed them, hovering at their knees as if awaiting permission. [288] Together they watched one rise, and in the shared exhale that followed, their breath silvered the wall. [305] The stone opened without sound. [310] Inside, the ember grotto glowed red at its seams, while its vaulted heart remained blue and fathomless above the clouds. [330] Threads of starlight hung from the roof in a lace so fine it seemed innocent until Sera saw each thread enter a vein of rock and draw out light. [359] The light ran into a golden basin stamped with her own crowned profile. [372] Her mother stood beside it, not alive, Sera understood, but repeated by the stone. [386] "My little sovereign," said the memory, "every clear dusk has a cost." [398] Sera approached, and the basin showed her the truth: her dynasty had bound a fallen star beneath the ridge, siphoning its radiance to calm storms and fatten their legend, not their fields. [430] The dreams were its diminishing memories, forced through the nearest royal blood. [442] Her recurring nights had not been prophecies but invitations. [451] "Cut one thread," her mother whispered, "and the binding learns your pulse." [463] The lacy ceiling tightened, delicate as a smile around a threat. [474] Sera could still turn back, preserve the gentle weather, and let the star die quietly beneath another century of obedient subjects. [495] Instead she asked Iven whether the valley could survive honest storms. [506] "Some roofs will go," he said, "and some men with them, but the wells may remember rain." [523] She held out her bleeding palms. [529] "Then help me make the ending ours, not theirs." [538] They set their hands to the same silver filament and breathed out together. [551] It parted with a sound like a child laughing after a long illness. [564] The other threads snapped in succession, each one writing a brief white flower across the dark. [580] Starlight bled upward now, through Sera, through the grotto, through the wounded ridge, and she felt the star's last dream: not vengeance, but the simple terror of falling and the wish to be seen. [614] She answered by opening her hands. [620] The star rose. [623] Clouds tore apart below them, and rain entered the valley for the first time in seven years. [640] By dawn, Sera's dream had ended, though she would never again sleep without hearing weather gather. [656] She descended with Iven, no longer protected, no longer deceived, carrying the empty basin like a common bowl. [674] At the palace gates, where frightened courtiers waited behind lace curtains, she set it down and listened to thunder approach without asking her permission. [698]</story>
