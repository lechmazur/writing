<story>After rain, the viaduct glowed above Vey, its lamps burning inside each suspended drop while the city below held its breath. [21] Mira arrived by falling upward from the canal, a gravity defier whose illegal gift had spared her from every collapse except the one that mattered. [46] In her coat lay an ancient clay tablet with runes, stolen from the archive before its tower became three contradictory ruins. [67] Its inscription named this place periodically eternal, alive only between the drop and the ripple, then restored whenever the next storm repeated the instant. [91] Tonight Mira meant to end that mercy. [98] Years ago, her brother Iven had fallen from the unfinished viaduct, and she had reversed her own fall instead of catching him. [120] Grief had driven her to the tablet, whose forbidden procedure fractured timelines until one contained a living Iven. [138] None did. [140] Each attempt only multiplied her choices, and now three Veys overlapped, their citizens fading whenever two memories disagreed. [158] The tablet's final rune promised repair if she would retrace the first fracture through the labyrinth of self. [176] She pressed her thumb into the clay. [183] The runes brightened, and the viaduct unfolded around her through procedural generation, each arch computed from a decision she regretted, each stair from an excuse. [208] Its beauty was almost forgiving. [213] Then the lamps turned from gold to white, so brilliant that every shadow showed her walking away from Iven. [232] Mira followed her old footprints down the generated stairs. [241] At the first landing, a younger Mira barred the arch, dry-eyed and furious. [254] "Catch him this time," the double said, "and admit you were always better than me." [269] Mira saw the trap: another perfect revision, another world pasted over the wound. [282] "I was afraid," she answered, "and I made your fear carry my guilt." [295] The double thinned into rain. [300] Deeper in, the labyrinth generated a courtroom where a hundred Miras prosecuted her with flawless arguments. [316] She could not defeat them, because each was right about what she had done. [330] Instead, she descended through the floor, letting gravity take her where defiance never could. [344] She landed on the original viaduct, unfinished and slick beneath a younger sky. [357] Iven slipped. [359] Her body rose by habit, weightless with escape, while his hand struck empty air below her. [375] This was the instant she had revised into a maze. [385] Mira kicked toward him, but the tablet dragged at her coat, offering architectures of rescue: bridges of light, reversed rain, an Iven who forgave before he fell. [412] She understood then that the labyrinth was not punishment but sovereignty, every corridor built to keep her choosing herself. [431] To conquer it, she stopped generating choices. [438] She seized his wrist, not to rewrite the fall but to share it. [451] Together they dropped as the first raindrop struck the canal. [461] The ripple opened. [464] Mira expected extinction, yet found herself standing on one viaduct beneath ordinary lamps, the tablet powdering between her fingers. [483] Iven was gone, and the single city around her was scarred, solid, and unbearably present. [498] In the canal, the ripple widened without resetting, carrying scraps of gold into the dark. [513] Across Vey, overlapping towers settled into smoke-stained brick, and people woke grieving losses they could finally name. [530] Some would curse Mira for choosing their wounds over her paradise. [541] She accepted that verdict too, though acceptance felt less like peace than a floor beneath her. [557] At dawn, she returned to the canal and found Iven's old measuring cord still knotted around the railing. [575] She untied it and began mapping the viaduct by hand, recording each crack rather than commanding it away. [593] When children gathered to watch her float above the water, she let herself sink until her boots touched the current. [613] "Teach us," one called, and Mira laughed at the terror and tenderness of being asked. [628] "First learn where you stand," she said. [635] Behind her, the viaduct's glow darkened gently into morning, no longer eternity, only a bridge that rain could cross. [654]</story>
