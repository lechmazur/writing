<story>Every morning, before the town woke, Eren Voss swept the labyrinth beneath the tower and weighed its sorrow. [18] The black salt left by bad dreams came to forty grams on market days, sixty after funerals, and never nothing. [38] Grief, the council said, was manageable once quantified. [46] Eren, its maintenance worker, replaced cracked tiles, oiled gates, and recorded the figures without asking who felt lighter. [64] His wife, an astronomer named Sera, had asked nothing else. [74] She believed the trees leaning over the labyrinth witnessed every private grief, and kept better accounts than the council. [93] To discover what trees witnessed, she had begun mapping scattered dreams with a sextant, treating each sleeper as a small, unreliable star. [115] Then lightning struck the tower, Sera fell, and Eren inherited her broken instrument and a sorrow that daily exceeded his scale. [136] A year later, on the anniversary of her death, a storm silvered the windows while moonlight poured through the open roof. [157] In the stormlit tower, the two kinds of brightness made ordinary dust look newly invented. [172] Eren climbed down to adjust a sticking gate, carrying the sextant only because Sera had once asked him never to leave it in damp air. [197] The corridor went silent around him. [203] At the moment a pin was heard dropping somewhere ahead, every salt gauge in his satchel clicked to zero. [222] He found the pin beside a hairline crack, bright as a period ending a sentence. [237] Through the crack came not air, but dreams: a baker burning a loaf, a boy losing a red mitten, an old woman forgetting her own name. [263] They scattered across the floor, each casting a cold shadow toward a different wall. [277] Eren understood, with the annoyance of a man finding a fault clearly labeled, that Sera's map had finally begun. [296] He fitted the pin into the sextant's empty hinge, and its bent arm held. [310] The mirrors could no longer measure stars, but moonlight struck them and divided into the exact angles of the scattered dreams. [331] Following those angles, he chalked lines between dream shadows, root tips, and salt stains until the floor became a sky turned upside down. [354] At its center lay one instruction in Sera's hand, revealed where chalk crossed old ink: deconstruct the labyrinth, or sorrow will build it forever. [378] Eren laughed once, because a year of grief had become a work order. [391] He pried loose the first tile. [397] Moonlight touched the exposed mortar, and the labyrinth, which healed in moonlight, knitted it shut almost at once. [415] So he worked by the map, breaking only where no silver fell, while thunder supplied the noise his careful hands usually avoided. [437] Each opened wall released measured sorrow, not as salt but as scenes the roots had drunk. [453] The trees had witnessed the baker giving the burnt loaf's soft center to his dog, the boy's sister knitting another mitten, the old woman's daughter patiently becoming her memory. [482] Their testimony was not that pain was small, but that almost no pain went unanswered. [497] At the final wall, Eren found Sera's last dream, weighing exactly one gram. [510] In it, he was repairing their kettle, scowling at a screw, while she watched him with such ordinary tenderness that he had to sit down. [535] The trees had seen that too. [541] He wept without improving the scene, and the last wall opened. [552] By dawn the labyrinth was a roofless spiral of low stones, useful for sitting on and easy to leave. [571] The town's sorrow still arrived each morning, but Eren weighed it, read the names, and carried each portion to people who could answer it. [595] Sometimes this meant soup; sometimes a locked door opened; sometimes only a witness staying until the kettle boiled. [613] On clear nights he set the mended sextant beside him, though its mirrors showed no stars. [629] It pointed instead toward the trees, whose leaves held rain, moonlight, and the small shine of work done without applause. [649] Eren entered zero in the ledger, then added, in Sera's old shorthand, "Not absent; transformed." [664]</story>
