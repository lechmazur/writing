<story>After the time traveler arrived, the mountain bookstore stopped singing. [10] Mara Venn, an arcane linguist, felt the silence in her teeth as she climbed from the bindery toward the bells. [30] The labyrinthine store occupied the whole mountain, its galleries threaded through roots and ore, its staircases rearranging themselves around sleeping readers. [51] Normally, turning pages made a symphony no ear could hear, a pressure of harmonies that guided rescuers toward people lost between shelves. [73] Now three rescue missions had failed, and the last team had returned carrying only blank maps. [89] The traveler waited in the bell chamber, young despite his silver coat, holding a botanical diary against his chest. [108] "I came back to prevent the collapse," he said, "but your wardens have locked me out of every index." [127] Mara was carefully resourceful by necessity; mountain rescues taught linguists to pack twine, chalk, patience, and spare meanings. [145] She did not ask for his prophecy. [152] She asked who was still missing. [158] "Archivist Senn and six apprentices," he answered. [165] "They entered seeking the library of unlearned lessons, where every book records what its reader failed to understand." [183] Mara had sought that library for years, hoping to learn why her brother's final rescue had left him speaking only in roots. [205] The diary trembled when she mentioned it. [212] Its pages held pressed leaves, each labeled in her brother's hand, though he had vanished before the traveler was born. [232] Instead of seizing it, Mara offered the traveler ink and asked him to renegotiate his bargain with the future. [251] "You came to save a building," she said. [259] "Help me save its people, and I will help you change what breaks it." [273] He agreed, and the diary opened to a gap between two paginated leaves. [286] Mara researched gaps because absence was the mountain's oldest grammar: missing stairs led down, missing echoes led inward, missing words led home. [308] Comparing the diary's skipped dates with blank rescue maps, she found a corridor omitted from every era. [325] They followed it with twine tied to the bell rope, while the traveler described disasters and she translated only his hesitations. [346] At each pause, a shelf moved, revealing Senn's chalk marks. [356] The corridor ended at a garden growing through shattered catalog drawers. [367] There the missing apprentices slept beneath translucent ferns, and Senn stood waist-deep in briars, guarding a door without a handle. [387] "It feeds on lessons we refuse," he warned. [395] "The wardens bargained with it: silence for safety, forgetting for walls that never fall." [409] Mara understood then that the traveler's arrival had not caused the silence; it had made the old bargain audible by ending it. [431] The door demanded her brother's name as payment. [439] She almost surrendered it, but the diary showed her a better absence: no lesson had been entered for mercy. [458] Carefully, she read that blank space aloud, giving mercy the cadence of a rescue call. [473] The traveler added the moment he had feared to remember, when he abandoned his own teacher to reach this mountain. [493] Senn spoke his shame at obeying the wardens. [501] One by one, the waking apprentices named lessons they had avoided, and each confession became a green note in the dark. [522] The unheard symphony returned, not as guidance but as an opening. [533] The door unfolded into the library of unlearned lessons, where books opened only when readers accepted they could be wrong. [553] On the first shelf, Mara found her brother's rescue record and the gap where his fate should have been. [572] She did not fill it with grief. [579] She wrote, "He went ahead so others could return," and followed his root-scratched directions out. [594] They carried the apprentices home along the twine, while behind them the traveler replanted the diary's leaves in the library's soil. [615] Each grew into a shelf that would hold one future mistake without collapsing beneath it. [630] By dawn, rescue bells rang through the mountain, and every staircase kept still long enough for the lost to answer. [650] Mara finally heard no music, only footsteps joining hers, which was how she knew the symphony had learned to listen. [670]</story>
