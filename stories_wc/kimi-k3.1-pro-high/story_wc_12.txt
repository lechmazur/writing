<story>At the dimensional anchor beneath Brine Cathedral, Father Ilex kept his loop pedal chained beside the altar, for reality there had to be rehearsed to remain real. [27]
He was the last loop pedal priest, a vocation mocked inland and indispensable at the world's thin edge. [45]
Each dawn he layered footsteps, bell strokes, and breath until their repetition persuaded the anchored town that dawn had meaning. [65]
On the morning the possession began, his loops returned with a second breath inside them. [80]
The candle flames bent inward, and Sister Maelin spoke without moving her mouth: "By vespers, this house will wear me." [100]
Ilex saw gray threads lifting from the motionless candles, an unraveled stillness that made quiet things forget their places. [119]
Where a thread parted, a flagstone became transparent over starless depth. [130]
Maelin, an icon painter before her vows, clutched her artist's palette with one color left on it: a hard blue bead named Witness. [153]
She had ground it from anchor glass, and it showed only what its painter refused to assume. [170]
"Does it?" Ilex asked, and the question, small as a plumb line, stopped her shaking. [185]
He tied his measuring cord to the palette and lowered it into the anchor's throat to plumb the invasion. [204]
At thirty feet the cord slackened; at forty, the palette rose from below and struck his hand. [221]
Its blue paint had become a sentence: "Stillness is consent." [231]
That was the hidden assumption beneath their liturgy, older than the cathedral and sweeter than doctrine. [247]
They had believed a silent anchor was obedient, a silent sister faithful, a silent god absent. [263]
The possessing thing had only to enter those conclusions and wait. [274]
Ilex understood then that exorcism would not illuminate it; argument might. [285]

"If stillness consents," he asked the vaults, "does a grave consent to its grave robber?" [338]

"We are still; we are not still: which witness will you believe?" [360]

"Yes," said Ilex, and his concession made her blink. [395]
"But fear is not ownership, and listening is not surrender." [405]

His teacher had built the pedal, then stopped speaking when the anchor took his voice as tribute. [493]
Ilex had called that sacrifice holy because calling it theft would make his own priesthood complicit. [509]

"Must the anchor eat a voice?" he asked. [528]
"Must a wound be worshiped to keep a world?" [537]
