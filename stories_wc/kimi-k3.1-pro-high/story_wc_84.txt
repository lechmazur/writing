At dusk, Mara Venn saw the pilgrim climbing toward the ancient observatory, and surprise stopped her hand above the drone controls. [21] She was the settlement's sharp eyed drone pilot, paid to notice loose tiles and stolen copper, yet she remained perpetually surprised whenever anyone chose to arrive. [47] No road had been safe for years, and the observatory had nothing whole enough to offer. [63] Its dome was a crown of missing pieces, its great lens lay blind in velvet, and its walls kept only the blue dusk and a thin, unresolved hum. [91]

Mara launched her drone, Wren, to guide the stranger through the fallen stairs. [104] Its lamp made a small, brilliant path in the dimness, and the pilgrim followed without fear. [120] Old custom required tea after the pilgrim arrived, Mara remembered, but she had only hot water and two chipped cups. [140]

The pilgrim, who gave his name as Sef, set a cloth packet beside the receiver that had occupied her life. [160] Inside lay a type foundry matrix chip, no larger than a fingernail, its bronze face cut with the silhouette of a tiny door. [183] Mara knew that door from the empty socket in the receiver, where her mother had once pointed and called the signal the frequency of belonging. [208] Her mother had vanished while searching for the chip, leaving Mara to mistake decoding it for a way of summoning her home. [230]

Sef said his caravan had carried it through three countries, following her mother's map in reverse. [246] "She saved us from a raider's beacon," he said. [255] "Then she walked north, because others still needed warning." [264]

Mara felt the old hope rise, then change shape. [273] She fitted the chip into the socket, and the hum broke into pulses with gaps between them. [290] The machine printed no message, only a row of empty letter molds above a sketch of circling stars. [308]

"More missing pieces," Sef whispered, but Mara's sharp eyes caught silver dust inside the molds. [323] The dust was lens grit, and the sketch was not decoration but a flight chart. [338] To read the signal, Wren would have to carry the receiver's antenna through the open dome by following star trails, letting each arc complete one silent interval. [365]

The dome, however, had not moved since Mara was a child. [376] Together they hauled the counterweight rope, but the mechanism only shrieked and shed rust. [390] Sef braced his shoulder against the seized wheel while Mara climbed into the brilliant dimness above the catwalk. [408] There she found the final obstruction, not a broken gear but a votive chain nailed across the track. [426]

Generations had locked the sky away so no enemy could use the observatory's beacon. [440] Opening it might guide her mother's friends, or her mother's killers. [451] Mara looked down at Sef, whose caravan had trusted a woman they could not repay, and understood why her mother had kept walking. [474] Belonging was not a door that closed behind one person, but a direction offered to the next. [491]

She wrapped the chain around Wren's landing frame and sent the drone straight up. [505] "Pull," she told it, though Wren understood only thrust, and the chain snapped free with a sound like a bell cracking. [526]

The dome opened, spilling dusk over their faces, and the first stars appeared in the gaps like letters being cast. [546] Mara guided Wren along their slow trails, east to zenith, zenith to west, while Sef held the antenna wire clear. [566] With every arc, a gap in the pulses filled, not with sound but with a name spoken in a different voice. [587]

Some names belonged to villages, some to the dead, and one, spoken by Sef's youngest daughter, belonged to Mara. [606] The frequency was a roll call carried between refuges, proof that someone had arrived and someone else was expected. [625] Her mother was not calling from it; she had built it, leaving empty molds so strangers could add themselves. [644]

Mara waited for grief to empty her, but instead she felt the peculiar shock of a larger room. [662] When Wren returned, she spoke her own name into the receiver, then Sef's, then the name of the road below. [682]

Far off, a light answered on the northern ridge, brief as a wink, and Mara laughed in surprise. [700] This time, she was not surprised to be found, but by how much room remained for everyone still coming. [719]
