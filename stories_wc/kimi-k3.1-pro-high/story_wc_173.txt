<story>In Vael, snow polished the palace courtyard silver, and the citizens praised the hush it laid over their footsteps. [19] Each evening, the Regent's lullaby rose from the prism sanctuary beneath the starlight, promising every sleeper a painless, identical dream. [39] Those who sang along woke serene, while those who refused were taken away and returned serene. [55] No one screamed anymore, which was why Mira distrusted the silence. [66] Mira was the court's chaste courtesan, paid to accompany lonely nobles, hear their confessions, and leave their hunger unowned. [85] Her art was bodily poetic: breath supplied the meter, spine the turning line, and hands the final, unrepeatable rhyme. [104] Because desire had never been allowed to command her, she recognized the lullaby as another hand closing around a throat. [124] On the winter solstice, the Regent summoned her to dance for the harvest of echoes. [139] He explained that sorrow, rage, and disobedient joy were merely noises, and that the city flourished when such noises were stored away. [161] Mira thought of her mother, who had laughed offbeat and died refusing the song, and agreed to dance. [179] Her compliance made the guards lower their spears. [187] At midnight, citizens filled the silver courtyard under snow, their faces tilted toward the sanctuary's clear, many-sided walls. [205] Inside it shimmered thousands of captive voices, each folded small enough to resemble a star. [220] The Regent began the lullaby, and Mira moved with such grace that even the falling flakes seemed to obey her. [240] Then she saw the threads. [245] They ran from every open mouth, fine as spider silk, carrying each person's newest echo toward a black knot above the sanctuary door. [268] One thread within the knot smelled of oven smoke and hummed with her mother's offbeat laugh. [284] Mira nearly broke her rhythm, but breaking would only tighten the weave. [296] Instead, she let the dance become listening. [303] A bent wrist loosened grief; a held breath separated terror from the useful fear beneath it; three patient fingers freed her mother's laugh. [326] By untangling threads rather than severing them, she spared each echo from the coming harvest and exposed the lock hidden in the knot. [349] The Regent smiled, believing her trance was obedience. [357] Around him, the people smiled too, and their calm became terrible. [368] Mira understood that one clean motion could still complete the harvest. [379] She also understood that panic would bind the threads beyond repair. [390] So she danced more softly, defying the lullaby of conformity without disturbing its tune. [404] Her refusal lived in pauses too small for guards to measure and in choices too free for music to predict. [424] The lock was not metal but a promise: that no voice could open it unless every voice agreed. [442] Mira laughed offbeat. [445] Her mother answered from the loosened thread, then a baker sobbed, a soldier swore, and a child began giggling at the snow. [467] Their discord struck the promise like a key. [475] In the hush between sigh and smile, Mira hooked the final thread around her smallest finger and pulled. [493] The knot blossomed open. [497] She used its loosened loop to unlock the sanctuary, and the prism under starlight filled with wings of sound. [516] The harvest of echoes returned as a harvest of witnesses. [526] Every stolen argument, lullaby parody, and last goodbye entered the courtyard without breaking the snow. [541] The Regent did not shout. [546] He simply kept singing, but now his beautiful voice sounded like a door closing inside an empty house. [564] One by one, the citizens stopped providing the chorus. [573] Their silence was not peaceful; it was loaded. [581] The guards looked at one another and discovered, with quiet horror, that each had been waiting for permission to lower his spear. [603] The Regent reached for Mira, and she offered him her hand. [614] For an instant he mistook mercy for surrender. [622] Then she drew him into the dance, where his body faltered, remembering a boyhood tune he had buried beneath the law. [643] He wept without ceasing to smile, and that alarming serenity finally left his face. [657] What remained was frightened, human, and free to choose. [666] Mira did not ask the city to sing her song. [676] She asked only that no song be sung for them again. [687] Around her, answers rose unevenly, ugly and radiant, while snow kept falling with the patience of a world that had not ended. [709]</story>
