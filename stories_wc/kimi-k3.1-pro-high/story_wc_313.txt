[65] Below, the drought had returned Vey, the town drowned to fill the reservoir, roof by roof and street by street. [85] Its resurfacing had drawn officials eager to expunge Vey from the hazard register before weekend visitors arrived with cameras. [104] Without a registered past, they argued, there could be no claims, no liable dead, and no delay to redevelopment. [123] Nell had signed their inspection order, but not their erasure. [133]

At the last gauge she found a photograph weighted beneath a bottle fragment, its image centered on a sapphire-hued liqueur label. [154] The picture showed Vey's Blue Hour Cafe, and on its back, in her dead father's hand, were the words, For Nell, when the water gives us back. [181] A second photograph waited at the foot of the exposed boat ramp, then another beside the church gate. [199] Each showed her as a child, holding the same blue-labeled bottle while townspeople posed behind her, solemn as actors in a play she had forgotten. [224] The trail should not have been there; the ruins had been surveyed at dawn. [238] Nell radioed closure to the ridge, then followed, because an unexplained visitor could mean sabotage and because her father's handwriting made caution feel like betrayal. [263]

[296] "Your father developed these before the flood; I only set them where your rounds would find them." [475] Her duty was precisely humane: exact about pressure, exact about names, and unwilling to confuse safety with silence. [493] She helped Sera out, photographed every print and unstable wall, then sealed the cellar against looters. [567] The order to expunge Vey was revoked, and the town was recorded both as a hazard and as a home. [587] Nell did not preserve every wall.
