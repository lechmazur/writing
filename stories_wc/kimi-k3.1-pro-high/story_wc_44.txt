<story>In the third year after spoons began bending reality, people ate soup with forks and locked their cutlery outside at dusk. [21] On the floating isle of Vael, the greater strangeness was that every shadow vanished at noon, then returned at one minute past, except the shadow of Master Oren. [49] Oren himself remained, gentle and alive, but something in him had gone silent, and he no longer remembered the runes he had taught. [72] His student, Mira Sen, a budding rune scholar with ink on her cuffs, meant to find where shadows go at noon and bring his back. [97] She was quietly deliberate, never turning a page before she had questioned it, never promising Oren what she could not prove. [118] Each night she climbed to the nocturnal observatory, a brass dome open only after sunset, where starlight kept the isle's old instruments awake. [141] Beneath its floor slept seven sealed awakenings: doors in the rock, each holding a first thought of the isle, dreams of flight, hunger, storm, judgment, root, return, and morning. [170] Oren had sealed them after the isle nearly woke and devoured the moon, or so his damaged notes claimed. [189] His only other legacy was a clockwork automaton key, a palm-sized brass spider whose wound legs could turn locks no hand could reach. [212] Mira had spent months opening small practice loops with it, closing each before dawn, yet the seventh door still carried Oren's fresh scratches. [235] One winter midnight, the spider woke by itself and tapped Oren's final sentence: Morning is not sealed; noon is. [254] Mira understood then why his notes ended in circles: the seal was a loop, and loops could be followed from either side. [276] At the next noon, she carried the key into the darkened dome, having shuttered every star lens except one. [295] The surviving lens held yesterday's starlight, a thin silver thread across the floor. [308] When the bell struck, Mira's shadow tore soundlessly away, but the thread caught its edge, and the key raced after it, stitching brass footprints into stone. [334] She followed through the seventh door before fear could make her wise. [346] Inside was not a room but the isle's dream of morning: an upside-down observatory hanging over a sunless sky, crowded with shadows folded like sleeping birds. [372] They were fuel, Mira saw, borrowed daily to keep Vael believing it could float beneath the changed heavens. [390] Oren's shadow alone stood upright, writing runes around a black sun, completing again and again the loop that held the awakening shut. [412] When it saw her, it spoke with Oren's lost voice. [422] "The spoon plague loosened every law," it said. [430] "I gave my shadow to teach morning obedience, but obedience is not understanding." [443] To stop, Mira had to relearn the seal as the shadow understood it, not as Oren's notes prescribed it. [462] She began with flight, tracing its rune until its end touched its beginning; the key clicked one leg shut. [481] By closing loops rather than cutting them, she could return each borrowed shadow without tearing away the isle's power. [500] Hunger fed return, storm answered root, judgment folded into flight, and one by one the folded birds rose through the ceiling toward their owners. [524] But morning remained, and the black sun cracked, revealing an eye beneath. [536] Mira's hand shook; she set it flat on the stone and waited until her breathing steadied. [552] Then she saw the final gap: every loop ended with departure, while morning demanded arrival. [567] She drew no new rune, but joined the last stroke of return to the first stroke of morning, making awakening mean coming back aware. [591] The automaton key sprang into the gap and became its clasp, its tiny gears reversing as the eye closed. [610] Oren's shadow stepped into Mira, passed through her astonished body, and vanished upward. [623] She emerged at one minute past noon to find the isle still floating and every roof, cypress, and citizen comfortably dark beneath themselves. [646] Oren stood in the courtyard, watching his shadow relearn the shape of his hands. [660] He remembered nothing of the seal, but when Mira offered him her notebook, he turned to a blank page and drew a circle with an opening that faced home. [689] Above them, locked spoons rattled in their box, and for the first time in years, Mira was not afraid of what might wake. [712]</story>
