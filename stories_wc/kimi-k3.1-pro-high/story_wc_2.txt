In the buried town of Cinder, Nox mended broken clocks because their stillness made no promises. [16]
His own life ran on lists: wake, eat, salvage, sleep, and never descend beneath the ashes that had taken his sister. [37]
Above him, the pumps coughed grit, and the council rationed every cup while insisting the old waterworks were lost. [56]
Then three customers brought him three dead clocks, each stopped at eleven seventeen, though each had failed in a different street. [77]
A coincidence, Nox decided, until the fourth clock arrived containing a slip of verdigrised brass: the admiral's lost compass rose. [97]
Admiral Ilen had vanished while mapping Cinder's waterworks, and her rose was rumored to point toward whichever passage would remain open. [118]
Nox, uncertainly certain that the rumor was nonsense, laid it beside the clocks. [131]
Its needle swung from his workbench toward the old greenhouse. [141]
When the clocks all ticked once together, coincidence became pattern. [151]
The pattern might lead to his sister's last known route; it might also be a trap made by hope. [170]
He packed the clocks, food for two days, tools for every repair he could name, and a plan with no blank spaces. [192]
Through the swirling leaves in a neglected greenhouse, he followed the needle while dry vines snapped like small bones underfoot. [212]
The leaves should have been still underground, yet they streamed upward, revealing warm air behind a collapsed trellis. [230]
His plan said turn back; the opening said otherwise. [239]
Nox crawled through. [242]
Beyond it, a subterranean waterfall thundered down a basalt throat, its spray jeweling the ash on an iron walkway. [261]
Across the torrent stood a sealed seed vault, Cinder's future granary, and before it lay the broken intake wheel that should have pumped water to town. [287]
Beside the wheel sat Ilen's skeleton, one arm around a smaller skeleton, both facing a door jammed by ash. [306]
Nox knew his sister by the wooden bird at her throat. [317]
She had not been swallowed randomly; she had followed Ilen here to restart the wheel. [332]
Grief made the cavern narrow. [337]
He wanted to close their eyes, carry them home, and let the dead keep their silence. [353]
Then the compass rose pulled toward the wheel, and above its lowest gear he found Ilen's diagram scratched into brass: release, brace, govern. [376]
The first two steps were clear, but the governor, the device meant to steady the wheel, had shattered. [394]
Nox's plan demanded a replacement he did not have. [403]
He laughed once, appalled by the blank space opening at his feet. [415]
Then he emptied his clocks onto the walkway. [424]
He would improvise: springs for tension, escapements for rhythm, and the compass rose for a balancing vane. [441]
When the wheel surged, his first assembly flew apart; when it dragged, the second folded like a dying hand. [460]
There were enough parts for one more attempt, but no certainty it would hold. [474]
Nox watched spray swell and lessen, leaves tremble in his cuffs, water choose a slightly different path around every stone. [494]
Nothing repeated perfectly, yet nothing was without rhythm. [502]
He stopped demanding that the wheel obey his drawing and built a governor that could yield, using his sister's wooden bird as a sliding counterweight. [527]
The wheel caught, faltered, caught again, and began turning with a sound like rain learning to breathe. [544]
Pipes knocked overhead; behind him, greenhouse glass dripped for the first time in years. [558]
Only then did he open Ilen's field book and read her final line: No route survives unchanged; leave water room to choose. [580]
Nox had come seeking an explanation neat enough to end his uncertainty. [592]
Instead, he buried the dead beside the warmed vault, planted the first seed, and drew a map home with several possible paths. [614]
On future expeditions he would still make lists, because planning was a kindness, but he would befriend uncertainty in planning by leaving margins wide enough for discovery. [641]
When he looked back, the compass rose was turning too, not toward a safe passage now, but toward the open door. [662]
