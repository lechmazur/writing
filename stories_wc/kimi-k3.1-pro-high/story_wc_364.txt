<story>On the orchard planet of Cyan, black trees fruited beneath floating rock arches, and every dawn the stones cast blue shadows over Toma's forge. [24] Toma was a meek blacksmith, apologizing to hinges before he struck them, yet he was quietly determined that nothing entrusted to his anvil should break twice. [50] Then the western arch fell, grinding the village into rubble and leaving the survivors in that narrow hour between understanding and confusion, when they knew the sky had struck but not why. [82] Worse, the impact stunned the orchard's root-heart, and without its slow stirring, no branch would blossom and no stored fruit would ripen. [104] The magician Serein arrived uncrushed, carrying a bolt of silk that shimmered azure even inside the dust. [121] "My cloth wakes sleepers," she said, "but awakenings must be bartered: one clear memory from you for each root I rouse." [142] The hungry villagers accepted, trading weddings, faces, and the names of their children until green shoots rose among people who smiled at strangers. [165] Toma said nothing; he merely noticed that each new shoot leaned toward Serein's sleeve, while the trader's blue eyes grew brighter with every memory surrendered. [190]

That night, deliberate as a man setting a fracture, he wrapped the magician's silk around his hammer and stole into the abandoned mine shafts beneath the forge. [217] The old galleries followed the root-heart, and their timber braces remembered every blow he had ever repaired. [234] If silk could carry awakening downward, Toma meant to offer the roots something sturdier than longing. [250] At the deepest shaft he found the heart split open, not sleeping but clenched around a shard of fallen arch. [270] Understanding hurt: the orchard had caught the stone to spare the village, and Serein had been waking only its frightened fingers. [291]

Toma forged no weapon. [295] He cut his anvil into twelve staples, fitted them around the wound, and threaded the silk through their glowing eyes. [315] When Serein descended, furious, he did not raise his voice. [325] "Take my memory of fear," he said, "if your bargain requires one; leave me the knowledge of where to stand." [345] "Fear is not clear," she whispered, but the silk tightened, tasting his offer, and the magician's smile became uncertain. [364]

Toma struck the first staple. [369] The note ran through shaft after shaft, waking picks, rails, and lamps abandoned by miners who had once taught the mountain to answer human hands. [394] He struck again, and the roots answered with a low blue shudder. [406] On the third blow, the shard lifted, not vanished but borne on a thousand small roots choosing to share its weight. [427]

Serein reached for her cloth, then stopped as Toma's forgotten fear entered her: years of flinching from louder men, and his lifelong discovery that hands could continue anyway. [455] She tore the silk free, awakening nothing more, and fled up the shaft while the mine held. [472] Toma climbed out slowly, his hands empty and his purpose intact. [483]

Above him, the root-heart opened every leaf at once, not restoring the lost orchards but sealing broken branches with silver scars. [504] The bartered villagers did not regain their memories, yet the trees bent toward them now, offering fruit whose seeds carried names. [525] A mother bit into one and whispered, "Mara," and a little girl answered from the rubble where she was stacking stones. [546]

They rebuilt without Serein's miracles, grafting orchard limbs to the staples Toma had hammered and hanging doors from archstone too stubborn to pulverize. [569] When the remaining arches sang in storms, Toma still apologized to his hinges, but he no longer apologized for taking up space beside them. [593] He had not saved Cyan by courage arriving like thunder; he had coaxed resilience from rubble by listening, fastening, and returning each day to strike once more. [620] And whenever the blue dusk made the ruins resemble the moment before disaster, the village lit its mine lamps, an azure defiance shining upward through the roots. [647]</story>
