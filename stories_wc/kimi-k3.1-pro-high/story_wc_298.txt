<story>Within the glow of half-lit corridors, Sera descended into the catacomb orchard, where black fruit shone above her and tree roots snaked between bones. [24]
She was a reckless idealist, which meant she had come to buy back every bartered destiny with only a copper wire, a satchel of names, and a burned tarot card. [54]
The card had once been the Star; fire had eaten its figure, leaving one clear point of light at the center. [75]
Her mother called it a future that refused to be read. [86]
Sera called it her inheritance. [91]
By day, she repaired the living: setting silver into fractures behind their eyes, mother-of-pearl into the emptiness under their ribs, working to patch holes in the soul left by sickness, grief, or bargain. [124]
Her brother Oren had the widest hole. [131]
He had bartered his destiny for medicine when Sera was dying, and now he ate, worked, and smiled without wanting anything at all. [154]
Above them, the city had made the orchard respectable: a marriage traded for rain, a tyrant's death for a child's talent, each bargain signed in root sap and called mercy. [184]
Sera had once believed only cowards refused such arithmetic, until Oren's rescue taught her that love could also make a prison. [205]
The Keeper waited beneath the oldest tree, her gown stitched from contracts, her face kind with exhaustion. [222]
"One destiny for one destiny," she said. [229]
"Yours is bright enough to purchase Oren's, but do not ask for the rest." [243]
Sera had spent a month stealing contract husks and inlaying meaning everywhere the orchard tried to keep blank: names into the wire, memories into the card's scorched edges, and the word return into her own pulse. [279]
If she failed, the roots would keep her nameless forever; if she succeeded, she would lose every possibility except the one she made next. [303]
She had brought no weapon because a blade would only prune what grief kept feeding. [318]
Now she laid the card on the roots and said, "All of them." [331]
The tree opened her chest without blood. [338]
Roots entered the hollow place and found, instead of years, every morning she might have chosen: love, work, failure, children, rage, and age. [361]
As they drank, Sera flung the wire around the trunk, trammeled the thrashing roots, and spoke each inlaid name through her teeth. [383]
The fruit answered. [386]
One by one they split, releasing not mist but memories: a baker's ambition, a thief's hope of mercy, Oren laughing before their mother died. [410]
The Keeper slashed at the wire with a bone knife. [420]
"Without offerings, the roots will rise into the city!" [429]
Sera could have pulled her down, but she caught the knife hand in a fiercely gentle grip, refusing both surrender and harm. [451]
"Then give them my tomorrow until they learn to live without buying anyone's," she said, though terror made the words grate. [472]
The offer was unfair, enormous, and accepted. [479]
The wire flared; every named destiny flew home through the corridors, while the unclaimed fruit withered into harmless seed. [498]
Above, bells began ringing as sleepers woke weeping from futures they had forgotten how to want. [514]
The Keeper sank among the bones, her contract-gown unraveling, and confessed that she had been the first to trade. [533]
Sera set one pearl into the old woman's breast, not to absolve her, but to give her enough self to stand trial. [555]
"You are still cruel," the Keeper whispered. [562]
"No," Sera said, fastening the last wire. [569]
"Only unwilling to mistake mercy for silence." [576]
Oren found Sera at dawn beneath a tree suddenly bare, her future gone and a dark opening still visible beneath her sternum. [598]
He was weeping because he wanted to live. [606]
"I can close it," he said, reaching for her tools. [616]
Sera folded his fingers around the burned card, now blank except for its surviving star. [631]
"Not yet." [633]
She understood that a patched soul was not a sealed jar, and that hers would need an honest opening through which choice could enter. [657]
They climbed together before the last lamps failed, Oren carrying the seeds and Sera carrying no map. [674]
Behind them, the roots tested their wire and held. [683]
Ahead, the city looked ordinary and difficult, a place where no kindness would be simple again. [699]
Sera walked into it slowly, with dusky resolve, choosing each step as if it were a small light inlaid in the dark. [721]</story>
