<story>At nautical twilight, when the opal dock glowed like a submerged bone, Old Iven came down through the mist to meet the dead. [23] Iven was the lighthouse keeper, an eccentric who salted his tea, named spiders after admirals, and wore a mailbox around his neck for messages nobody sent. [49] The woman on the dock carried no bag, only a lantern with no flame and a dried Edelweiss boutonniere cupped in her palm. [72] Before he could speak, she pinned the flower to his oilskin, touched her temple, then his, and held her thumb and forefinger a narrow space apart. [98] Then she sat against a bollard and died, her gaze softly persistent as the tide until the mist took it. [118] No ship had arrived, and the dock's opal skin showed no footsteps beyond hers, as if the mist had witnessed her isolation and refused to testify. [144] Iven could have rung for the mainland doctor, but the final gesture stopped him. [158] He knew borrowed memories could hide inside objects, but never inside a silence so carefully measured. [174] So he decided to investigate before burying her, carrying the body upstairs and laying her beneath the turning lamp. [193] The lighthouse kept sailors alive by lending their own recollections back to them: a mother's kitchen, a dog's bark, a path through snow, each shining across water when names and stars failed. [225] Iven tended those borrowed memories in blue jars, though he possessed none of his own beyond the day he had awakened beneath the lamp. [249] Instead of opening jars at random, he investigated by observing gaps. [260] There were eleven jars for every year except one year with ten, six steps between landings except one flight with five, and thirty chimes each dawn except mornings after storms. [290] The absences made a path, and Iven followed it with the patience he usually reserved for suspicious soup. [309] In the missing jar's place he found dust shaped like fingers; on the absent stair, a mountain flower carved beneath his boot. [331] At the silent thirtieth chime, he heard a woman's voice inside his skull say, Not what was taken, Iven, but where. [352] He returned to the dead woman and measured the space between her thumb and finger. [367] It matched the gap in the lamp's iron shutter, a slit he had always polished but never opened. [385] Inside lay a reel of logbook film and a key labeled with his own name, though the handwriting was hers. [405] The film showed a younger Iven without his mailbox, standing on an alpine ridge beside the woman, both wearing Edelweiss. [425] The next frames showed a storm, a passenger ship breaking on the reef, and Iven carrying its survivors up the lightless tower. [447] To relight it, he had fed the lamp his memories, beginning with unimportant ones and ending, when the ship still foundered, with her. [470] She had survived to find him emptied, and had spent forty years returning each borrowed recollection except the one that would hurt most. [493] That recollection waited in the key: her last memory, borrowed by the lamp on the same night and never claimed. [513] Iven understood her gesture then: two temples, one shared past, and the narrow gap where she had asked him to look. [534] He could leave it closed and remain safely curious, or open it and learn why she had come alone to die. [555] His hand, usually so theatrical among switches and dials, became softly persistent. [567] He turned the key. [571] Memory arrived not as a picture but as warmth: her hand warming his on a frozen ridge, her laughter when he proposed with the boutonniere, and her last sight of him voluntarily forgetting her so strangers might live. [609] Then he felt her forty years of visits, sitting beside a husband who offered tea and asked whether they had met. [630] Each time she had answered, Not yet, because the lighthouse still needed his emptiness, and because he had made her promise never to trade a ship for her grief. [659] Now no ship remained on the horizon, and her final coming had released him. [673] For once his isolation had a witness, even if she could no longer see it. [688] He carried her to the dock before full darkness and set the unlit lantern between them. [704] Then he placed the dried flower inside it, fed the lamp every borrowed memory except hers, and watched the beam turn outward, crowded with kitchens, dogs, and snow. [732] For the first time, Iven stood in the dark center of his own light, remembering the woman who had crossed forty years of mist to make his loneliness visible. [761]</story>
