On the ancient terrace at midsummer, where three planets leaned toward one another in the slow dance of celestial bodies, Mara Vey opened her ebony clipboard and waited for the armies of the Forever War to muster. [37]
Below, soldiers of both republics climbed in separate columns, their rifles wrapped in white cloth, their officers watching one another with the vigilance of people who had made an art of surviving betrayal. [70]
Mara had negotiated truces between them for twenty-six years, long enough to know that gentleness without procedure was merely another invitation to violence. [93]
She was operatively gentle: she checked the water in the prisoners' cups, stationed healers where the old stones would throw afternoon heat, and greeted each commander by name before speaking of borders. [125]
Her assistant, Tovin, carried ink and thought her kindness inexhaustible; only Mara knew it was discipline, tightened daily around a memory. [146]
At nineteen, serving as a military clerk, she had altered a reconnaissance figure that sent the western army into an undefended valley, and the reprisals had given the war its second century. [178]
She had meant to win promotion, not a massacre, but intention had never repaired a roof or taught a dead child's brother to forgive. [202]
Now both commanders approached the basin at the terrace's center, a cracked bowl older than either flag, while Venus brightened above the western stair. [226]
The treaty on Mara's clipboard would open shared reservoirs, return prisoners, and let mixed crews repair the aqueduct before autumn, but its military clauses were not why exhausted soldiers had agreed to come. [259]
They had come for the Remembrance, the ritual their grandmothers had kept in cellars whenever officials mocked it as superstition. [279]
At conjunction, each army would pour water from its capital's well into the basin, name one harm it could no longer deny, and drink only after its enemy had drunk. [309]
Mara had built the enforceable treaty around that vow because she wanted to anchor ideals in practice, not leave mercy floating above the signatures. [333]
General Sorn poured first and admitted the burning of the eastern hospitals; General Aven answered with the prison ships, her voice breaking on the final number. [359]
Then Tovin unfolded the ritual cloth, and Mara saw the small square of blank linen reserved for the negotiator's confession. [379]
She had added that square herself, telling no one why. [389]
Sorn offered her the pen with a courtier's smile, for he possessed copies of her altered orders and expected her to purchase silence with favorable border terms. [416]
Instead, Mara set the ebony clipboard across the basin, its scratched underside bridging both streams, and read the valley's dead from the list she had compiled in secret. [444]
The names took so long that the planets seemed to move, though everyone knew the heavens were patient and only human endurance was shifting. [468]
When she finished, Mara confessed her forgery, surrendered her diplomatic immunity, and asked that the treaty remain binding whether or not either court spared her. [493]
Aven struck her once, hard enough to split her lip, and Mara did not summon the guards. [510]
She simply pressed a clean cloth to the general's bruised knuckles, then held out her own wrists for arrest. [529]
The gesture did not absolve her; nothing so graceful could. [539]
Redemption, she had finally learned, was not a clean death exchanged for a useful peace, but the price of living answerable to those she had injured while still doing the work before her. [572]
Sorn's soldiers shifted, uncertain, until an old eastern private stepped from their ranks, put down his wrapped rifle, and drank from the basin. [595]
Aven drank next, and the columns followed with no music except cups, boots, and the occasional furious sob. [613]
They signed while Mara stood between two unarmed wardens, her blood spotting the treaty's margin beside Sorn's smeared signature and Aven's uneven hand. [636]
At dawn, Tovin asked what he should do with the clipboard, and Mara told him to give it to the aqueduct crews, who would need something level on which to sketch repairs. [668]
He wept then, but she reminded him that the first inspection was due at noon and that prison regulations permitted her to advise it in writing. [694]
Below them, water entered the broken channel, flashing where it found gaps and vanishing into weeds before returning, thin and muddy, to the light. [718]
No one called it beautiful, yet no one looked away. [728]
