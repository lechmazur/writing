<story>After the last lighthouse keeper left the cliff, the automated light kept sweeping the sea, and Mara kept planting trees beneath it. [22] The arboretum perched there on narrow terraces, its salt-stunted pines holding the soil while its glasshouses faced the prevailing wind. [42] Mara was quietly talented at grafting, coaxing one unlikely branch to accept another without ever calling the work miraculous. [61] She had survived a drone strike at her wedding; calling anything miraculous had become difficult. [76] On the morning the coast guard promised a storm, she found a school party waiting beside the greenhouse, though tours had been canceled. [99] Their teacher had missed the message, and the road bus would not return for an hour. [115] Mara admitted them, checked the shutters, and gave each child a task that could be finished quickly. [132] Real safety, she believed, began with accurate work, not reassurance. [142] A boy named Niko noticed the crumpled photograph she used as a bookmark in her grafting ledger. [159] It showed Mara dancing under a canopy of lights, one hand held by her sister, who had died before the first ambulance came. [182] Her husband had survived too, but their marriage had not; he wanted to forget the day, while she needed to remember it accurately. [205] When Niko asked why she kept such a damaged picture, Mara said, "Because joy happened, and so did the rest." [225] Then a rotor sounded above the rain. [232] Mara saw the delivery drone through the glass, an ordinary machine bringing storm batteries, but her body received only the old message. [254] Her hands stopped over a tray of cuttings, and her breath narrowed. [266] Freeze, her nerves commanded. [270] Once, that command had pinned her beneath a banquet table while other people ran toward the second explosion. [288] Now Niko was on the outside path, chasing his blown worksheet toward the cliff rail. [303] Mara could not afford stillness, nor could she simply order herself to be brave. [317] Her rehabilitation counselor had taught her to read somatic markers: metallic saliva meant terror, a lifted heel meant available motion, and pain in her left palm meant she was gripping what was already gone. [351] She tasted metal, felt her right heel rise, and opened her hand. [363] The photograph stayed on the table. [369] She stepped into the rain and called Niko's name once, low enough not to startle him. [385] When he turned, she pointed to the worksheet caught against the rail, then to the sheltered door. [402] "Leave the paper," she said. [407] "Come back by the stones." [412] He did, and she walked beside him without touching him until they reached the door. [427] Behind them, a gust opened the greenhouse vent with a crack, and everyone flinched. [441] Mara flinched too. [444] Inside, she barred the vent, counted heads twice, and moved the group into the keeper's old cottage, whose stone walls faced away from the sea. [469] Only then did her knees shake. [475] The teacher offered praise, but Mara shook her head. [484] "The rail is rusted," she said. [490] "I logged it last month and let the repair wait." [500] That admission mattered more to her than rescue; optimism without it would be another canopy of lights, beautiful and blind. [520] While the storm struck, she showed the children how a graft was wrapped, firm enough to hold and loose enough to grow. [542] Niko asked whether the little trees would survive the cliff. [552] "Not all of them," Mara said. [558] "I'll record which ones, improve the windbreak, and plant again." [568] She could anchor optimism in realism only by keeping both promises: look at the damage, and prepare for spring. [587] When the drone returned for its undelivered batteries, its hum still hurt her teeth. [601] She let the hurt exist, and located beneath it a pearl of stillness, small and hard-won, not peace but a place from which one choice could be made. [629] She chose to finish the graft. [635] By dusk the wind had eased, and the bus climbed the road through branches and foam. [651] Before leaving, Niko smoothed the photograph's bent corner and returned it to the ledger. [665] Mara did not smooth the rest. [671] Its creases belonged beside her new notes: repair the rail, reinforce the western glass, ask for help before the next warning. [692] Above them, the keeperless light turned on time, indifferent but useful, illuminating the path one honest sweep at a time. [712]</story>
