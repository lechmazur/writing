<story>By dawn they would find the bodies, and every road from the lighthouse prison would lead back to Lio. [19] He stood over Warden Rusk and Doctor Pell in the lamp room, where the sunset laid copperlight across their still hands and the sea kept its repose. [46] Rusk had killed Pell for refusing to sign the drowned prisoners as transfers; Lio had only slammed the lamp door, and Rusk, rushing him, had fallen down the stair. [75] Below, twelve prisoners waited in cells the tide would flood before morning. [87] The guards were on the reef hauling crab pots, but they would return with the bell. [103] Lio had rung that bell too often for Rusk's false drills. [114] He was, after all, the son of the boy who cried wolf, and inherited disbelief fit him like a cell. [134] His father had survived to become a shepherd of one truth: a borrowed story could catch a crowd, but only your own could lead you out. [160]

Pell's hand still held the lattice from the confessional, a cedar screen pierced with diamonds and dark from a century of whispered guilt. [183] Behind it lay the old monastic labyrinth, sealed when the lighthouse became a prison on its jagged reef. [201] Pell had said its passages led past the cells to the boat stair, but opened only for a penitent chasing hush. [222] Lio took the lattice and listened. [228] Beyond the surf and the guards' oars, a silence moved through the walls, pausing wherever a lie had been spoken. [248] He followed it down, speaking because speech was the thread it demanded. [260]

"I lied for Rusk," he said. [266] "I liked being needed, even by a man I despised." [276] The hush deepened, and the lattice grew weightless. [284] It began to hover above his palms, turning its diamonds toward a narrow door left ajar for decades. [302] Through its frame, the scuffs on the threshold joined into lines: heel marks where penitents had turned left, toe scars where they had chosen right. [327] The lighthouse sustains impossibilities, Pell had claimed: flame burning in rain, walls answering voices, and now a map made from the wear of forgotten feet. [352]

Lio read the turns aloud, then confessed what each false alarm had purchased: medicine, extra bread, Rusk's smile. [370] He had meant to survive, not to teach honest men to ignore a warning. [384] At the final turn, the passage refused to open. [393] Beyond it, the prisoners began striking their cups against iron. [403] Lio tried his father's famous cry, "Wolf," and the labyrinth answered with a hundred lying corridors. [419] Then he understood: he could not thread a labyrinth with his father's story, nor escape with a confession polished into another performance. [441]

"I did not kill Rusk," he said, "but I wanted him dead when I shut that door." [458] "I wanted Pell's silence to save me, though it would drown the others." [471] "I am afraid no one will believe me, and I have used that fear as permission." [487] The lattice stopped turning. [491] In its stillness, the door opened. [497]

Lio found the cells, turned Pell's infirmary key, and led twelve gaunt prisoners through the hush while water climbed their ankles. [518] At the boat stair, the guards' lantern found them, and the first man raised his musket. [534] Lio could have whispered that Rusk was coming. [542] Instead he climbed to the wolf bell and rang the true alarm: flood below, murder above, prisoners escaping under his charge. [563] The guards did not lower the musket until the prisoners, one by one, repeated his story in their own voices. [583]

Then came shouts overhead: the bodies had been found. [592] But the road back to Lio was crowded now with witnesses, and none of them would carry him alone. [611] They went back together, not to flee but to face the lamp room, and when Lio had told everything again, the hush left the lighthouse like a held breath released. [641]

Outside, dawn touched the reef with copperlight, gentle and undeserved. [651] Lio watched the prisoners step into the prison boat, each carrying a name, a crime, a pardon, or a wound that no lattice could screen away. [677] He would give his account on the mainland and take whatever followed. [689] For the first time, he was not asking silence to save him, or a crowd to believe him. [707] He had found the narrow door, and his own footsteps were enough to mark the way. [723]</story>
