<story>On the lavender bluff under aurora, where old observatories kept their brass domes polished for nobody, Sena arrived glowing and unconvinced. [21] People called her a luminous skeptic, though the light was only engineered skin responding to radiation, and the doubt was entirely her own. [44] She had come while forests held elections, those slow, fragrant referendums in which roots cast mineral votes and canopies chose a speaker for the century. [69] Below the bluff, three forests were converging by root and seed-drone to settle whether rain should be invited back into the drought districts. [92] Sena's official task was to observe their new decision engine; her private task was to learn why some people carry weather with them. [115] Her mother had carried rain: a private drizzle over her shoulders, a smell of wet dust preceding her into every room. [136] As a child, Sena had loved it; as an adult, she had published proof that so-called weather-bearers only attracted attention, not clouds. [158] Then the drought districts blossomed on the maps, and her mother walked into one, leaving Sena a tin compass ring and no explanation. [181] The ring was absurdly primitive, a bent tin circle around a floating seed of magnetized iron, yet its needle had trembled since dawn. [204] At the polling stones, Sena touched the electoral sap, and the interface on her tongue began sensing through taste changes. [224] Birch arguments arrived as cold sweetness, pine objections as resinous bitterness, and every uncounted root tasted faintly of iron. [243] The method offended her training, which demanded instruments that could be cleaned, calibrated, and blamed. [258] Still, when the sweetness broke into metallic sourness, she turned before any alarm sounded and saw six strangers climbing the bluff. [279] Each wore a different climate: one snowed lightly onto her sleeves, one trailed heat-shimmer, and one was followed by a cloud no larger than a dog. [305] Sena's needle swung among them, then toward the forest, never north. [316] "Compasses point to available directions," said the snow-haired woman. [325] "Direction is just an answer that has learned to move," Sena replied, hating how much she sounded like her mother. [345] The strangers were volunteer witnesses, summoned because weather-bearers always appeared near deadlocked elections, though no archive explained why. [363] At midnight the root count stalled, and the aurora folded overhead like a green page being turned. [380] The six climates converged around Sena, not touching, yet making a circle of fog, frost, warmth, and pressure. [398] Her mouth filled with the taste of pennies, then rainwater, then the vanilla dust of her childhood home. [416] On the ring, the needle stopped trembling and divided into six shadows, each pointing at a different witness. [434] For one terrible second she thought the machine was commanding them. [445] Then she noticed the shadows changed whenever one witness breathed, grieved, or remembered. [458] There was no hidden hand, only an emergent compass: direction arising from roots, weather, memory, and need, with no single voter able to choose it. [483] Sena finally understood that her instruments had failed because they kept asking where north was, instead of asking what the world was trying to become. [508] Her mother's rain had not followed her mother; her mother had followed the places whose thirst tasted like home. [527] The ring had simply inherited her last agreement with them. [537] Sena pressed the ring into the polling stone, offering its tiny field as one more vote. [553] The forest did not elect a speaker; it elected a route. [564] Below them, channels brightened through the dust, and the witnesses turned together toward the districts, their separate weathers braiding into cloud. [585] It was a terrestrially cosmic thing, Sena thought, this compass made of mud and starlight, older than her gadgets and newer than the next rain. [610] She followed the witnesses down from the bluff, her engineered radiance dimming until it resembled ordinary dawn. [627] For years she had wanted weather to be a trick, because tricks could be exposed and grief could be kept tidy. [648] Now, tasting iron soften into rain, she accepted a harder mystery: belonging was not belief, but a direction practiced together. [668] Behind her, the counting roots resumed, and ahead, very gently, it began to rain on her alone. [685] Sena laughed, wiped her face, and kept walking, luminous at last with something she could not engineer. [702]</story>
