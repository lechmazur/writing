<story>Three minutes after the resuscitation, Mara understood why the abandoned carnival funhouse had kept its lights on for forty years. [20] Every bulb was dead, yet a lingering luminance coated the mirrors, the same green radiance she had seen beneath the paramedic's hands. [42] Jonah was outside fetching an ambulance that would never find the flooded road, and she was alone with her camera, her mother's opera cloak clasp, and a voice. [70] The voice belonged to the whispering advisor who had spoken in her childhood nightmares, courteous as an usher and patient as rot. [92] Do not run, it whispered from the silver clasp; fear only opens doors when you turn your back. [110] Mara had come to end it: photographically cursed, she appeared as a corpse in every picture, while each new print left her body colder and the funhouse brighter. [138] Her mother had worn the clasp the night she vanished here, leaving only a dream dictionary whose margins were crowded with paradoxical notes. [161] Mara now saw that the entries formed instructions, provided she read each meaning against itself. [176] To dream of a mirror, one note said, means you must cover it; therefore uncover what you most wish to hide. [197] To dream of light means blindness; therefore follow the glow that disappears when watched. [211] She wanted to unbosom herself of the old terror, but the advisor warned that confession was merely fear wearing Sunday clothes. [232] Instead, she would trick fear into revealing its weakness by pretending to surrender. [245] She pinned the opera cloak clasp at her throat, raised her camera, and announced that she wanted one final portrait. [265] The mirrors answered with a luscious stench of violets and decay, and her mother's face unfolded from the luminous grime. [285] It was beautiful in the way a wound is beautiful to the blade that made it. [301] Smile, the advisor whispered, but Mara heard its eagerness and understood that the voice belonged to the thing wearing her mother. [322] So she smiled without mirth and confessed, not her fear, but her delight in being afraid. [338] The admission was revolting, sincere, and false enough to be useful. [349] She described the exquisite chill of hunted flesh, the sweet pull toward the dark, until every mirror leaned closer. [368] The clasp grew cold; the advisor stopped whispering, unwilling to interrupt its feast. [381] Then Mara opened her mother's dictionary at Camera, defined as a room that dreams it is an eye. [399] Beneath it, a note read: To capture the dreamer, become the dream; to escape the dream, photograph yourself waking. [418] She turned the lens inward, not at her face but at the green afterimage floating behind her eyes, and pressed the shutter with her eyes shut. [444] No flash came, only a click like a tooth breaking. [454] The funhouse screamed through her mother's mouth as the photograph slid out: not Mara's corpse, but the advisor crouched inside a camera, feeding on its own tiny reflection. [482] Its weakness was vanity; it needed to witness itself being feared. [493] Mara covered every mirror except the one holding that image, trapping fear in an audience of itself. [510] The green radiance dwindled to a stain, and her mother's face softened, almost apologized, then vanished. [526] Before leaving, Mara unbosomed the truth she had withheld from the dead: she had stopped searching for her mother years ago, and guilt, not love, had brought her back. [555] The confession no longer fed anything; it simply belonged to her. [566] Outside, Jonah found her at dawn with warm hands and a blank photograph. [579] When he asked what had happened, she gave him the clasp and kept the picture, because some allure survives by being believed destroyed. [602] That night, the blank paper developed a single silver word: Again. [613] Mara laughed once, without turning her back, and for the first time the whisper had to wait for her permission.
