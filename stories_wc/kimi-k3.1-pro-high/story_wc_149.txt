Beyond the village, where winter-cracked fields lay hushed beneath an endless night, the alchemical orchard greenhouse glowed like a drowned emerald. [21]
Its glass roof held no stars, only the black sap of spellbound pear trees and the pale lanterns of fruit that had forgotten dawn. [45]
Tomas, its last gardener, had come seeking medicine for the village children, though the gate opened only after his carved stone bead warmed against it. [70]
His mother had called the bead a seed from his first life, and he had always laughed before her grave silenced him. [92]
Inside, a bride ghosted through ephemeral cameo appearances: veiled in one pane, silver in a ladle, then breathing within the steam above his tea. [116]
The ashen-veil ghost bride never crossed a threshold, yet every brief reflection guided him deeper, her raised finger warning him away from the silver pears. [141]
When fever drove him toward one anyway, she appeared in its skin and mouthed, "Not yet." [157]
He woke beneath a black tree with a bitter pear in his hand and her fingerprints blooming blue around his wrist. [178]
The bead pulsed against his breastbone, and the tree answered with a note too low for hearing. [195]
Roots stirred; glass sang; somewhere under the soil, a heart remembered its name. [208]
It was the first of the resonant awakenings, and it returned to Tomas a memory of standing beside that bride while an alchemist poured venom into her wedding cup. [237]
He had been Aurel then, the alchemist's servant, and she had been Mira, purchased to root the orchard in a living soul. [259]
Aurel had drunk the cup himself, but Mira had torn his soul free before death could keep it, binding her own ghost to the trees as payment. [286]
Thus he returned in body after body, while she waited in glass and frost, owing him a life across lifetimes. [306]
Tomas found her next in the mercury mirror of a pruning knife. [318]
"You keep saving me," he said, and her reflection smiled with such mournful elation that the blade wept. [336]
"You keep forgetting the door," she answered. [343]
She led him by glimpses to the orchard's heart, where three trees grew from one trunk: white for memory, red for grief, black for mercy. [368]
At her nod he ate their fruits together, and understanding became lovingly venomous, opening him with pain because tenderness alone had never kept him awake. [393]
He saw himself in a hundred lives entering the greenhouse, loving her, and choosing the silver pears because they offered a painless eternity beside her. [418]
Each time she had let the orchard kill him gently, sending him out to try again. [434]
Her love had been his poison and his rescue, her debt a wheel she could stop only by losing him. [454]
The bead, he now knew, was the alchemist's petrified heart, carved by Mira into a key. [470]
Set in the trunk, it would wake the buried sun and unmake every ghost the orchard sustained, including her. [489]
Tomas held it over the hollow as Mira appeared whole for the first time, ash drifting from her veil like spring blossom. [511]
"There is medicine enough in dawn," she said. [519]
He thought of the children coughing beyond the fields, then of the thousand dawns she had spent refusing to mistake captivity for devotion. [542]
"Stay," he whispered, but he pressed the bead home. [551]
The note that followed passed through root, bone, and ghost, waking every seed in the fields and every name death had muffled. [573]
Mira's body thinned as light climbed the glass, yet her face was radiant with grief completed. [589]
"At last you are awake," she said, and at last he understood that awakening was not remembering her, but becoming able to release her. [613]
She kissed him with lips bitter as almond and vanished before the sun cleared the panes. [629]

Tomas kept no bead, only a small scar above his heart shaped like a seed. [664]
Years later, when his own children asked why he planted white, red, and black pears together, he told them that some debts are repaid by return, and some by release. [694]
Then spring wind moved through the branches, three notes becoming one, and he wept without sorrow, certain that somewhere beyond borrowed lifetimes, Mira was waking too. [720]
