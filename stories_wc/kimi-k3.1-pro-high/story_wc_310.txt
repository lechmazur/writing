<story>After the universe contracted, the sky above Orison was no larger than the ambered observatory deck, and its last star burned beyond the glass like a coal under a fingernail. [30]
Sen was its keeper, its decision maker, and the last person alive who believed casting lots was an honest way to greet disaster. [53]
She was quietly enthusiastic about catastrophe, not because she desired it, but because every ending produced static, and every static burst might contain a reason. [78]
For nineteen years she had tried to wavelength align with purpose, tuning her pulse to the observatory's receivers until purpose seemed less like a calling than a frequency she had failed to hear. [111]
That final night, the choice was simple enough to fit on two lots: distill the last star into a seed for expansion, or let contraction finish in peace. [139]
No living council remained, so Sen reached for her collection of foreign coins, gathered from refugees whose countries, planets, and suns had successively vanished. [163]
Their unfamiliar faces and scripts prevented affection from weighting her hand. [174]
A square copper coin meant seed; a silver disc with a hole meant silence. [188]
Before she could cast, the receiver spat three bursts through the deck, rattling amber dust from the rafters. [206]
Sen fed them into the still, a glass apparatus built to distill signals by freezing noise until only repeated patterns evaporated. [227]
Understanding static bursts had always depended on resisting speech; meaning lived in the pauses, where expectation could not imitate a voice. [248]
Yet this time a voice emerged, patient and unmistakably her own. [259]
"The star is gone, and nothing follows." [266]
"You chose the silver coin." [271]
"Do not trust the order in which you hear me." [281]
"Begin with the smallest doubt." [286]
A backwards prophecy, her teachers had called such messages: consequences arriving first, each sentence pulling its cause behind it like a drowned swimmer. [309]
Sen reversed the recording and heard an impeccable future: begin with doubt, distrust the message, choose silver, and let everything end. [330]
Her hand warmed around the coins. [336]
The seed engine waited beneath the deck, its empty chamber shaped like a question. [350]
Expansion might restore immeasurable life, or merely repeat the long cruelty of making worlds only to gather them back into one fist. [372]
Silence offered certainty, and certainty glittered more dangerously than any star. [383]
Sen cast. [385]
The copper coin landed squarely on seed. [392]
Relief arrived before dread, which shamed her. [399]
The lot had not discovered her purpose; it had only spared her the loneliness of choosing it. [416]
Then she noticed the silver disc already lying beneath her other palm. [428]
She had removed both lots before casting, yet the prophecy required silver to be chosen, not merely present. [446]
A future could be accurate about every fact and still lie about their meaning. [460]
She could place silver in the receiver, call that a choice, and fulfill the words without believing them. [478]
Or she could refuse to make obedience the last surviving law. [489]
For the first time, Sen understood that her future voice might be less a command than a fossil, pressure printed into time by someone she need not remain. [517]
She set the silver coin beside the copper one and pressed seed. [529]
The deck groaned as mirrors unfolded toward the star. [538]
Doubt stayed with her, stellar and immense, but it no longer resembled permission to do nothing. [554]
The still shrieked; the star thinned to a white thread; the receiver released one final burst. [570]
Sen expected her own voice and heard instead a child counting, in a language not yet born, the countries on a handful of impossible coins. [595]
Then darkness closed over Orison. [600]
Inside the seed chamber, something small began ticking against its walls, irregular as rain and patient as an unanswered question. [620]
Sen laid her palm to the glass and matched her breathing to it, not because the rhythm was destiny, but because she had chosen to answer. [646]
When the first new wavelength trembled through her bones, her purpose did not arrive with it. [662]
She made one anyway: to listen without surrendering, and to leave the next decision maker better doubts than hers. [681]</story>
