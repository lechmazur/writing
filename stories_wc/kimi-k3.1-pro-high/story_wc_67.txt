<story>At the Vanishing Point Gallery, every corridor narrowed toward a black door that no visitor remembered opening. [17] The owner, Master Ilex, promised that inside it sorrow could be viewed without danger, and grief-starved people believed him because he never exactly lied. [41] He merely gave them incomplete information, letting each hopeful mind supply the mercy he had omitted. [57] Mara Venn knew the omission: the portraits did not imprison sorrow, but consumed the happiest memory that made sorrow bearable. [77] She was the gallery's charcoal portrait ash scatterer, the funerary artist who drew the dead from survivors' descriptions, burned each drawing, and fed its ashes to the frames. [105] The dead then appeared for one final hour, serene and harmless, while the living forgot why they had ever loved them. [126] Mara stayed because her mother remained behind the black door, rocking in the chair where she had died during the gallery's first exhibition. [149] For twelve years, Mara had refused to draw her, though preserving that last tender look was the only wish that still made her tremble. [173] She wanted to crystallize a fleeting emotion, not embalm a ghost: the instant her mother, burned and terrified, had chosen to smile so her child would run. [200] On the final night, she entered wearing her mask made from shattered porcelain shards, all that remained of the blue tea service destroyed in that fire. [226] Its fractured surface turned her face into twenty cautious strangers, hiding her expression from frames that fed on recognition. [245] Ilex had told her it would keep her safe. [254] Now she heard the careful space around that promise. [263] Safe from flames, perhaps, but not from loss. [271] Beyond the door, her mother rocked beside a blank canvas, while ash fell upward like snow returning to a wounded sky. [292] "You took your time," the apparition said, and the chair's runners kept their patient measure. [307] "You are not her," Mara answered, though her hands had already begun selecting charcoal by touch. [323] "No," it admitted, "but I am the room where your last memory of her can live without hurting you." [340] That was the mirage of safety on which the gallery had been built: pain removed by removing its meaning. [359] Mara thought of visitors leaving dry-eyed, unable to recall a lullaby, a shared joke, or the warmth of a hand. [379] She had woven truth carefully for Ilex, captioning each portrait "At peace" and never adding "At a price." [397] Tonight she would use the same narrow honesty against him. [407] "I will finish her portrait," she said, "if you show every visitor what the frames take." [423] "Then no one will come." [428] "Then no one will come uninformed." [434] The apparition smiled with her mother's mouth, and for one dangerous instant Mara believed consent could be a kind of love. [455] She drew quickly: the singed sleeve, the level eyes, the impossible smile opening a path through fire. [472] Yet she left the face unfinished, because a complete portrait would feed the frame, and an incomplete one could only tell its story. [495] Through incomplete information she had once protected the gallery; through an incomplete image she would now release its captives. [514] When Ilex reached for the charcoal, she crushed the remaining sticks beneath her heel and began to powder the drawing with their black dust. [538] She blew, and the likeness dissolved into ash-colored wings that slipped beneath every frame. [552] Throughout the gallery, portraits darkened, revealing not tranquil dead but the cherished memories vanishing from their mourners. [569] Visitors saw what safety cost, and their cries became one long, terrible note. [582] The black door opened onto the ordinary dawn. [590] Behind her, the old rocking chair finally slowed to a stop. [601] Without its motion, the apparition was only a woman fading at the edges, afraid and smiling anyway. [618] Mara lifted her mask, accepting the risk of being known. [628] "I remember," she said, though the smile was already loosening from her mind like a leaf in water. [646] She could not keep it whole. [652] Instead, she pressed one porcelain shard into the blank canvas, where it held a single fleck of charcoal: not her mother's face, not peace, only the shape of courage passing between them. [684] By noon the gallery stood empty, its long corridors leading nowhere. [695] Mara carried the canvas into the sunlight, grieving what she had lost and marveling that the loss still belonged to her. [716]</story>
