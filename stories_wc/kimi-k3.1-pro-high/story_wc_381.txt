<story>At the nocturnal haven beside a quiet brook, Sera kept a lamp burning blue against the reeds. [17] She was a solitary summoner, not because she distrusted people, but because every voice near her learned to whisper. [36] The brook carried those whispers downstream, and she listened for the one current that never changed. [52] Amid whispered secrets about lovers and debts, it repeated three cold clicks, a pause, then two. [68] Sera had mapped the pattern for seven nights, marking stones where each sound vanished. [82] By mapping silence rather than sound, she discovered a prophecy spoken in code beneath the water. [98] Its gaps named a date, and its clicks named her valley. [109] At dawn tomorrow, the stars would fall rootward, and every sleeping mouth would speak until it bled. [126] The code could not be destroyed without silencing the brook, whose flow kept the haven's buried embers cool. [144] If those embers woke, the hills would burn. [152] So Sera unfolded the crane's origami wing she had made from a star chart and a hermit's letter. [170] It was all that remained of her teacher, who had entered the sky to escape the same prophecy. [188] Escape had only carried the code higher. [195] Now the wing trembled above her palm, thirsty for a summons. [206] Sera set it on the brook and let it flow between the marked stones. [220] Where silence pooled, the paper darkened; where clicks struck, its creases shone. [232] She followed with her lamp, seeing not a bird but a map of absences. [246] At the final stone, she pinched the lamp flame between finger and thumb. [259] Instead of dying, it became a tranquil blaze, white at its heart and gentle as breath. [275] She lowered it into the largest silence. [282] The brook opened like an eye. [288] From it rose a crane woven of river dark and paper light, vast enough to roof the reeds. [306] Its gaze was terrestrially cosmic, clay brown around pupils full of turning constellations. [319] Sera felt small, yet not erased. [325] The crane dipped its head, and the coded prophecy clicked inside her teeth. [338] Dawn, rootward, every mouth. [342] She understood then that her teacher had not fled upward; he had been answering. [356] A summons was also a reply, and replies gave old words somewhere new to go. [371] Sera climbed onto the crane's back, carrying the blue lamp in one hand. [384] Below them, the haven held its breath. [391] She could command the bird to swallow the prophecy, but that would leave a silence so complete that nothing could grow around it. [414] Instead, she asked it to carry each coded gap away from the next, as her map had taught her. [433] The crane spread its remaining wing, and Sera held out the origami fragment. [446] Paper touched feather. [449] A smoothed hush moved through the valley, not flattening sound but easing its sharp edges. [464] Clicks separated into harmless rain; pauses lengthened into sleep. [473] The date lost its hour, and the valley lost its name. [484] Without those joints, the prophecy could no longer hold its dreadful shape. [496] It flowed apart. [499] Far above, something bright loosened from the heavens, then drifted down as ash white crane feathers. [515] They landed without burning, and beneath them the buried embers settled. [526] When sunrise silvered the brook, Sera found her teacher's letter restored inside the wing. [540] It held no farewell now, only four words: Silence is a door. [552] She folded the wing into a boat and returned it to the current. [565] The crane was gone, but its constellation remained beneath the water, turning slowly among brown stones. [581] Sera walked home through reeds that whispered ordinary secrets, grateful for their small deceits. [595] By dusk, she would teach the village children to read the quiet places, so none need stand alone beside a warning. [616] For now, she left her lamp unshuttered, a soft fire for anyone following the brook. [631] Its flame did not waver when the water spoke her name. [642] She answered, and the whole haven breathed. [649]</story>
