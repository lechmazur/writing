<story>Under starlight, the prism sanctuary turned every color into a wound, and Sera entered carrying the dead admiral's broken mask. [20] She was its restorer, though cracks in her own face, left by the wreck that drowned her crew, no lacquer could hide. [42] Tonight the faceted walls trembled without sound, for the sanctuary's interlaced hush had grown too heavy to hold. [60] Woven from a centuries-old fisherman's net, it hung between crystal pillars, accumulating silence with every confession denied. [77] Restless spirits pressed through its mesh, pale hands opening and closing like fish drowning in air. [93] Sera's apprentice, Nilo, waited beside an empty grave cut into the glass floor. [106] "Tell me why the admiral's mask weeps salt," he said, "or I won't help you exhume him." [123] His demand made the secret a doorway: once they crossed it, there would be no returning to who they had been. [144] Sera set the mask on the grave's rim and drew a tapestry needle from her sleeve. [160] Its gold eye held the last thread spun by her mother, who had stitched funeral faces before the sea took her. [181] "I cut the net loose during the storm," Sera said. [191] "It held our catch, but also the admiral, tangled below deck after Nilo's father chained him there." [208] Nilo looked toward the net, where a tall spirit wore fathoms of darkness. [221] "My father said the admiral murdered the crew." [229] "My father saved himself by feeding them his lie." [238] "And I saved myself by believing him," Sera answered. [247] "Your silence fed mine," Nilo whispered, and the hush tightened until starlight bent. [260] The grave below them remained empty because they had never dared recover the man both families had buried in falsehood. [280] To ground the spirits, they would have to exhume not only his bones, but the truth knotted around them. [299] Sera pierced the first knot with her tapestry needle. [308] The net could not be cut; cutting would free every silence at once and shatter the sanctuary. [325] So she worked as she worked on masks, finding the fracture, honoring its shape, then joining what violence had divided. [345] Nilo held the lamp while she picked fibers apart, naming each drowned sailor as a knot loosened. [362] With every name, a spirit descended, no longer clawing at the walls but standing in its own color. [380] Their silence flowed into the needle's gold thread, which Sera used to sew the admiral's split jaw. [397] The mask did not become whole; it became honestly broken, its seam visible as a river on a map. [416] At the final knot, the net spoke with her mother's voice. [427] "Child, a secret shared is not a burden halved unless both hands carry it." [441] Sera faltered, but Nilo took the net's other edge. [450] Together they unraveled the knot, and the glass grave opened like an eye. [463] Below lay no admiral, only his chains and a small boat's rudder, preserved where the sanctuary had been built above the wreck. [485] His bones were still at sea; what they exhumed was the last object his hands had touched. [502] Nilo lowered the repaired mask beside it. [509] The tall spirit entered its hollows, and for one terrible instant Sera saw not innocence, but fear: the admiral had been cruel, his murderers cowardly, and every simplification another drowning. [539] "Rest does not mean pardon," she told him. [547] "It means nothing more can be hidden." [554] He bowed, then lay down within the prism's fractured light. [564] One by one, the sailors followed, their colors sinking into the floor until the sanctuary held only ordinary darkness. [583] Yet Sera felt no peace. [588] Outside, the sea continued against the cliffs, lucid and indifferent, while inside the loosened net began, almost imperceptibly, to gather new silence. [610] She could restore a mask, but not make a face incapable of lying. [623] So she hung the tapestry needle where everyone could see it and left the grave uncovered. [639] Beside her, Nilo repeated the names, practicing a voice that would not hand its secrets down. [655] When dawn's first star faded, they were still speaking, and the listening walls had learned to tremble. [672]</story>
