After the final grain slipped through the hourglass, Elian wound his wife's red pull cord handle around his palm and listened to the house refuse to breathe. [27] No kettle murmured, no floorboard answered, and Wren, thirteen and solemn as a locked door, sat beside Sera's empty chair. [47] For a year Elian had tended their unspoken grief with useless care: he washed cups, folded blankets, and left every sorrow decently unnamed. [70] Yet tending had become hiding, and he was mournfully eager to restore laughter to their household before silence inherited it. [90]

So he carried Wren and the hourglass to the abandoned railway tunnel where he conducted the leaf blower symphony. [109] The town called it an echo chamber, though Elian knew chambers held only what people were afraid to say elsewhere. [129] Twelve neighbors waited among wet leaves, their machines shouldered like awkward cellos. [141] Each autumn they had made cheerful thunder for Sera's birthday because ordinary music could never make her laugh so helplessly. [161] Tonight there would be no birthday, only the last rite Sera had invented for them: when grief's glass emptied, speak into the wind. [184]

Wren set the glass on a milestone. [191] "Its sand is spent," she said. [197] "Then why are we still counting?" [203]

Elian fitted the red handle to the lead blower and pulled. [214] The engine caught with a rasp so familiar that Wren flinched, and one by one the others woke. [232] He raised Sera's broken baton, actually a teaspoon, and the symphony began off-key. [245]

Under his direction, the machines did not merely roar. [254] Their nozzles scooped air into low cradles, their throttles loosened into sighs, and through the mouthpiece Elian half-sang the lullaby Sera had used to stall bedtime. [280] The other players half-sang too, badly, tenderly, until lullabies traveled on an off-key wind and returned from the tunnel as a woman's almost-voice. [303]

Leaves lifted around Wren's shoes. [308] On each, in Sera's green ink, someone in the house had written what could not be said while she was dying. [329] They had sealed the leaves in jars, then soaked them here for seven nights, following her instructions without believing them. [349] Now the wind began to macerate the softened leaves against a hollow log, grinding them into dark pulp that stained the air with words. [373]

"I was angry," the tunnel whispered in Wren's voice. [382] "I wanted you to stop being brave." [389]

Wren's hand found Elian's sleeve. [394]

"I was relieved when the pain stopped," it said in his own voice, "and I have hated myself for it." [414]

The players faltered, but he swept the teaspoon downward, keeping them gentle. [426] This was the tending: not sweeping grief away, but giving each buried utterance air, time, and somewhere safe to land. [446]

Then came a leaf neither recognized. [452]

"You both snore when you cry," the echo said, warm and worn and unmistakably Sera's. [467]

Wren made a small, strangled sound. [473] Elian stepped toward her, terrified he had broken something beyond mending. [484] The sound came again, and she bent over, clutching the red handle where it hung from his belt. [502]

"Mom said that just to make us laugh," Wren gasped. [512] "I wrote it on the leaf myself." [519] "She was furious about the snoring part." [526]

Her laugh escaped then, not bright or healed, but rusty, hiccuping, alive. [538] Elian's followed because grief, however faithfully tended, had never been the only thing living in their house. [555] One by one the blowers died, leaving their laughter to stumble through the tunnel and come back multiplied. [573] For an instant, a third laugh traveled with it, low and delighted. [585]

Wren heard it too; he knew by the way she reached for his hand without looking away from the dark. [605] They did not call after it. [611] Some presences were for welcoming, not keeping. [618]

At home, Elian placed the empty hourglass beside Sera's chair and hung the pull cord handle above the stove, where its chipped red grip could catch the morning light. [647] Wren put the teaspoon in the cutlery drawer, then changed her mind and set it between their two plates. [666] That night the house still ached, and sometimes the hallway sighed with no wind to explain it. [683] But when the kettle began its off-key bubbling, Wren hummed half a lullaby, and Elian, washing cups, softly sang the rest. [704]
