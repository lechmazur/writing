<story>At seventy-one, Vela had the narrow wrists and unlined face of a girl, though her eyes kept an old woman's patience. [21] A researcher who aged backward, she had spent her long life studying why certain people vanished without leaving fear, damage, or bones. [43] Now her calendar contained only mornings of diminishing speech, and her apprentice, Ilo, knew she was withholding something. [61]

On the last clear night, she led him to the magnetic grotto under starlight, where ore-black walls drew his compass needle upward. [83] Sycamores leaned above its mouth, and their stillness made a sycamore hush deep enough to hold every unsaid thing. [102] Within, a floating rock meditation platform hovered over a round pond, turning gently whenever either of them breathed. [120] Vela stepped onto it and gave Ilo a bag of stones. [131] Tonight, she said, we will teach vanishing to appear. [140]

He wanted to ask whether she meant the missing villagers, her vanished teachers, or herself, but her small hand closed around his wrist. [163] Ask with a stone, she whispered. [169] He threw, and the pond answered with bright rings. [178] At first they seemed ordinary, expanding, crossing, and fading against the magnetic shore. [191] Then Vela told him to sit, match her breathing, and remain through the trance that followed. [207]

Hours loosened. [209] Starlight slid along the grotto's veins, and the platform carried them over water black as closed eyes. [226] Whenever the rings disappeared, Vela made him wait. [234] Long afterward, finer circles rose from the still pond, not spreading from the stone's strike but arriving at its old center. [255] Their delays formed measures, their intersections formed letters, and their broken arcs formed names. [269]

Ilo read the first name and nearly fell. [277] It belonged to his mother, who had vanished from a locked room when he was nine. [293] The returning rings repeated her private name for him, then showed a doorway opening inside a winter apple. [311] Grief made him reach for another stone, but Vela withheld the bag. [323] If you demand an image, she said, you will make only an echo of your wanting. [339]

Her restraint angered him more than a lie would have, yet he set the stone down. [355] Vela had always woven truth carefully, giving each learner only the thread their hands could hold. [371] Now he understood that her silences were not empty; they were places where his own sight had to grow. [390]

He threw the next stone gently and asked no question. [400] The pond returned a map of the villagers, not as captives or corpses, but as figures stepping through moments the living had overlooked. [423] His mother appeared among them, neither young nor dead, pressing one finger to her lips. [438] Behind her stood Vela, not girl or woman, but a tiny brightness nested in the grotto's stone. [455]

Ilo turned, and the delayed revelation opened in him with terrible quiet. [467] Vela had not brought him there to learn whether she would vanish. [479] She had brought him to learn where she would go, and how to receive what the world had not yet shown. [500] You knew, he said. [504] I knew the pattern, not the morning, she answered. [513] I withheld my ending so you would not mistake mourning for attention. [525]

Dawn paled the stars, though none could enter the pond. [535] Vela's shoulders narrowed beneath her cloak, and her voice thinned toward childhood. [547] She gave him the last stone. [553] Will it hurt, he asked. [558] Only if appearing must always resemble staying, she said. [567]

He cast it without a wish. [573] The rings carried her name outward, then inward, and Vela folded smaller upon the platform until the cloak lay empty. [593] Ilo did not call after her. [599] He watched the water become still, honoring the interval she had taught him. [612]

At sunrise, one pale ring surfaced. [618] Within it stood the image of an open door, and beyond the door grew a sycamore whose leaves moved though the grotto air did not. [643] Ilo finally wept, not because she was gone, but because absence had kept its promise to be patient. [661]

He gathered her cloak, left one stone upon the platform, and stepped into the hush with the pond's slow grammar shining behind his eyes. [685] When others came seeking Vela, he would give them no easy answer, only a stone, a place beside the water, and time enough for the returning rings. [712]</story>
