<story>In the mirror world, night had lasted so long that people stopped naming their children after stars. [17] Its last light lived inside an alchemical orchard greenhouse, where glass trees bore silver fruit and roots drank mercury rain. [37] Mara kept that light, though she could no longer keep herself. [48] She was the memory keeper, and her memories lay scattered among labeled seeds, cracked cups, and the pockets of strangers. [68] Whenever someone hummed, she remembered a face; whenever the humming stopped, the face vanished. [82] On the final evening, though evening meant only a bell, the orchard's single mica window clouded from within. [100] Beyond it, the dying realm flickered: villages dark, canals still, hearth smoke thinning into black. [115] Mara pressed her palm to the mica and felt a heartbeat too small to belong to the world. [133] It came from a frost-white apple hanging above the grafting table. [144] When she lifted it, the peel opened like a door, revealing an infant made of dim gold thread. [162] The child had no cry, only a tightening that pulled every branch toward the floor. [177] A memory surfaced: her own hands, younger, winding dawn around a spindle while voices rose below. [193] Then it scattered again, leaving her frightened by the tenderness of her hands. [206] She carried the child to the window, where the realm's last delegates waited in separate pools of lamplight. [224] A mason from the drowned city, a beekeeper, two ferrymen, and a girl representing no one but her silent street had come demanding fire. [248] Instead Mara asked each for a song nobody remembered teaching them. [259] The request angered them until the beekeeper, ashamed, offered three notes used to calm swarms. [274] Mara heard her mother's voice inside them and recalled that dawn was never stored; it was woven. [291] The mason added a stone-setting chant, the ferrymen a crossing tune, and the girl a rhythm clicked against her teeth. [311] Through those forgotten songs, Mara's memories returned not as possessions but as paths between singers. [326] She remembered this greenhouse built over a wound in the sun, and herself choosing forgetfulness so no tyrant could seize the whole design. [349] Each keeper had hidden one measure in ordinary work, scattering the pattern across kitchens, ferries, hives, and cradles. [367] Now the delegates sang badly, stopped, laughed, and began again. [377] Their mistakes made room for one another. [384] Mara wound the child's gold thread around her wrists, binding herself to the work, then guided every voice through the mica window. [406] Mica split each note into colors; the colors crossed, warp and weft, until woven dawns trembled above the orchard like luminous cloth. [428] Still the child tightened, and Mara understood: light forced outward would only tear the wound wider. [444] She took her burial shawl from the seed cabinet, where she had kept it without knowing why, and used it to swaddle the small dawn. [469] Bound gently, the child stopped pulling; held close, it began to shine. [481] Mara set it against the window, and light entered the glass grain by grain. [495] The first village answered with a bread song, the next with oar strokes, then thousands of misremembered choruses reached back. [515] No voice was pure enough to rule, and none too poor to join. [528] The weaving spread from window to window, knitting communities together before it touched the sky. [543] Mara felt the gold bindings loosen. [549] Her memories did not return to imprison her; they streamed outward, finding neighbors, descendants, even enemies who had once shared a table. [571] With every recollection released, her name grew lighter. [579] At sunrise, the greenhouse fruit burst into ordinary seeds, and the child breathed with a sound like paper opening. [598] Mara could not recall whether she had been mother, jailer, or gardener, but she knew what to plant. [616] She gave the delegates seeds for roads they would have to build toward one another, then opened the door. [635] Outside, dawn did not fall upon the realm; it rose from its joined hands, and Mara, unbound at last, walked out singing a song she had never kept. [663]</story>
