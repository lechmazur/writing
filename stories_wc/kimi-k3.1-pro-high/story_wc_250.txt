<story>After the rain, the cobalt dock held the color of a bruise that had decided not to hurt. [18] Its guardian stood beside the lock, silent as the mooring posts, watching black water carry one star at a time. [38] No one knew whether he had taken a vow, lost his voice, or simply found speech too blunt for grief. [58] His only instrument was a dental mirror, small and round, inherited from the mother who had once made frightened children open their mouths and breathe. [83] Tonight he angled it toward the lamp above the customs shed, then toward a skiff approaching without lights. [101] In the skiff sat Mara, whose brother had vanished beneath this canal three winters ago. [116] She had returned with his red shuttle in her pocket and a secret balanced on the edge of her teeth. [136] "I told him the ice would..." she began, but the guardian lifted one finger, not to silence her, only to keep the unfinished words from breaking. [162] He understood that within the edge of a half-spoken secret, a person could still turn back. [178] Mara stepped onto the dock, leaving the skiff untied. [187] "You saw us that night," she said. [194] "Everyone thinks you cannot accuse me because you cannot speak." [204] The guardian's calm moved in slow spirals, from his still face to his loosened shoulders to the hand that offered her a dry cloth. [228] That spiralingly calm attention made her anger feel suddenly badly dressed. [239] She struck the cloth away. [244] "I dared him to cross. [249] He was afraid, and I called him..." [256] Again the words stopped. [260] The canal took them, swelling once against the piles. [269] The guardian did not touch her. [275] Instead, he opened the shutters of the customs shed, then the boathouse, then the little loft where canal keepers stored their nets. [297] Each window held a lamp hooded against the rain. [306] By creating patterns with shutters, he could send brief bars of light across the water, a language older than the dock. [327] Tonight he used the dental mirror to catch those bars and bend them upward, weaving brightness through the rafters. [346] Line crossed line, and the crossings became stars, not sharp white points but small flawed trembling things. [363] Mara watched him weave until her brother's shuttle slipped from her fingers. [374] "A coward," she whispered, finishing at last. [381] "That is what I called him, because I was the coward." [392] The guardian closed every shutter. [397] Darkness returned so completely that Mara gasped and reached for him. [408] He let her find his sleeve, then opened one shutter no wider than a tear. [423] A single reflection trembled on her wet cheek. [431] Another opened, and another, each star appearing only because the dark gave it somewhere to be seen. [448] He was not forgiving her; he was showing her what forgiveness would have to learn. [463] Even blame could not live by devouring every light. [472] His silent empathy did not ask Mara to become clean, only to become visible. [486] She picked up the red shuttle and tied it to the skiff's painter, weaving a knot she could later untie. [506] Then she told him everything, while he polished the mirror with the discarded cloth. [520] When the harbor bell sounded, the guardian returned the stars to the canal, shutter by shutter. [536] Their reflections sank without drowning. [541] Mara took up her oars, not absolved, but no longer eager to disappear. [554] Before pushing off, she pointed toward the black water and then toward the sky. [568] Behind them, the dock kept its blue counsel, and rain gathered in the mirror's rim without spilling. [585] For the first time, Mara looked back not at the place where her brother vanished, but at the lights his absence had taught her to see. [611] The guardian nodded: to teach darkness the value of stars, one did not curse it or fill it with noise. [631] One listened until it opened, and gave it a place to shine. [643]</story>
