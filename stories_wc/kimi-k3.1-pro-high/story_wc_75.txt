<story>After the beekeeper's last visit, the waterfall behind Veyra began to freeze, and its grotto city woke each morning to a silence sweetened by the rosewater sprinkled on museum floors to disguise the smell of damp stone. [37] No one understood the omen better than Ilyra Venn, the museum's cultured guide, who could date a doorway by its chisel marks and recite the lineages of displaced persons sheltered behind the cataract for six centuries. [73] Her historically oriented tours had once celebrated continuity: the same bread ovens, the same aqueducts, the same songs passing from exile to child. [96] Now the city's displaced families were leaving again, because the bees had died, the terraced orchards had failed, and the ice advancing through the falls promised to seal the grottoes before spring. [128] The beekeeper, Master Sel, had come annually with hives and memories of the outer valleys, but on his final visit he had given Ilyra an emerald-tinted opera glass lens wrapped in waxed linen. [161] Through it, he said, lost workmanship could be found, though he would explain no more before following the refugees downriver. [181] Ilyra kept the lens in the museum's cabinet of theatrical curiosities until the night the last baker, Old Nemi, announced that her initials would leave the city unbaked. [209] In her family, bread shaped like initials had named each newborn, mourned each dead, and welcomed each stranger to the table; without beeswax to seal the starter crock, her sourdough had frozen solid. [242] To Ilyra, this was not merely hunger approaching but the death of an art that made kinship legible. [260] She resolved to rescue it, and every other dying art in the grotto, before the evacuation boats departed at dawn. [280] When she lifted the lens to inspect Nemi's starter, green light revealed not mold but a map of hairline fractures running through the crock, each glowing like a tiny river. [310] The fractures continued across the bakery wall, pointing toward the oldest grotto, where the museum stored unclassified debris from the first refugees. [332] There Ilyra found a frozen brass hive, a stone warming table, and a fresco showing bakers setting initial-shaped loaves around its rim while meltwater steamed beneath. [358] The city founders had not fled with treasure; they had carried a living furnace of bees, bread, and shared skill. [378] Through the lens, a green inscription surfaced along the table: Let the first letter warm the rest. [395] Ilyra understood that the opera glass had been ground from the same emerald slag lining the ancient hearth, and that its tint made heat-tracing pigments visible. [421] Sel's gift was a key, not a prophecy. [429] She rang the museum bell and summoned those who remained: Nemi, two potters, a dyer, three children, and the boatmen already itching to abandon them. [454] They cleared the warming table, thawed Nemi's starter beside its banked coals, and shaped the first loaf into V for Veyra, though Ilyra insisted it also stood for the vanished who had taught them. [488] As the dough rose, heat followed channels forgotten under the museum, and the brass hive began to hum with water, not wings. [510] The sound was so delicate and the risen bread so fragrant that everyone smiled, yet rosewater dread tightened in Ilyra's throat: if the channels broke, the thaw would drown them. [540] Outside, the monumental waterfall shuddered. [545] A green vein climbed its ice, branching exactly like the fractures in Nemi's crock, and Ilyra saw that the whole cataract was the city's second starter, frozen around an ancient heart. [577] She ordered the boats loaded not with museum statues but with crocks, tools, pattern books, and coals. [594] Then she sent the children through the grottoes carrying warm initial loaves, each letter an invitation to return and help. [614] People came for the bread and stayed for the work, potters sealing channels, dyers marking safe stones, boatmen rigging sluice ropes. [635] At dawn the falls opened with a roar, releasing a sweet, rotten gust from long-frozen orchards. [651] Water swept the lower docks empty, but the upper city held, and mist rose through the rainbow like incense over an altar no one fully trusted. [677] The evacuation ended before it began. [683] In the following weeks, Ilyra replaced her lineage lectures with working galleries where refugees taught newcomers to bake their names, mend kilns, and read the old heat-signs. [710] She exhibited Sel's lens beside a loaf tin labeled S, certain he had known that rescuing an art meant returning it to hands, not glass. [735] When spring bees finally threaded the grotto, no one claimed they were his, but Nemi baked an S anyway, and its crust split gently, like ice remembering water. [763]</story>
